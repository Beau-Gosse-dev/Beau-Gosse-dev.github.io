STONEWALL IN THE VALLEY: KERNSTOWN (Part the First)

The episode ends before the First Battle of Kernstown. The Loring dispute reduced Jackson’s Valley force to an all-Virginia command of roughly 4,500 effectives in early March. Banks occupied Winchester, then began moving troops east toward McClellan. Acting on orders to keep those troops in the Valley, Jackson reversed his retreat and marched north on March 22. A skirmish near Kernstown wounded James Shields and left Nathan Kimball in field command. Turner Ashby then seriously underestimated the United States force still near Winchester, leading Jackson to order an advance for March 23 against what he believed was a small rear guard

ScopeReported figure
Jackson’s force, early MarchAbout 4,500 men; three infantry brigades, cavalry, and 26 gunsThe episode describes ten Virginia infantry regiments, one five-company Virginia battalion, one cavalry regiment, and five artillery batteries. NPS summaries round the force immediately before Kernstown to about 3,500–4,000 after marching and straggling.
United States strengthThe episode estimates about 38,000 men in western Maryland by late FebruaryThat number describes the broad concentration available to Banks and nearby forces, not the force later engaged at Kernstown. Modern NPS accounts place Banks at roughly 20,000 during the advance and Shields’s remaining division near Winchester at about 8,000–10,000.
