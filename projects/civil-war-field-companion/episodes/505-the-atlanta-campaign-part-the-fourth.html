What happened

Sherman marched toward Meridian

The Meridian raid moved east from Vicksburg and forced Leonidas Polk to determine whether Sherman was heading for Mobile or Meridian.

Polk evacuated military stores from Meridian

Confederate workers removed useful supplies shortly before U.S. troops entered the town.

Davis ordered three divisions to Mississippi

Jefferson Davis directed Johnston to send Cheatham’s, Cleburne’s, and Walker’s divisions from Hardee’s Corps to reinforce Polk.

Grant’s Dalton demonstration was delayed

A false report that Longstreet threatened Knoxville caused Grant to divert 14,000 men before ordering a reconnaissance toward Dalton.

Palmer led 25,000 men toward Dalton

Because George Thomas was ill, John M. Palmer started the reconnaissance from the Chattanooga area on February 22.

The Meridian raid had already ended

Sherman was returning to Vicksburg before the Dalton movement could stop Confederate reinforcements.

U.S. troops tested Rocky Face Ridge

From February 24 through 26, the force skirmished with Confederate troops occupying the ridge west of Dalton.

The northern approach proved very strong

The 10th Michigan lost about 60 men while probing the defenses near Buzzard Roost Gap.

The 39th Indiana entered Dug Gap

Thomas Harrison’s mounted infantry drove off a Confederate company and repulsed a cavalry attack at the pass south of Dalton.

Harrison identified the railroad vulnerability

He reported that a larger force could move east through the gap and cut the Western and Atlantic Railroad south of Dalton.

Cleburne’s troops retook Dug Gap

A recalled Confederate brigade attacked on February 26 after Johnston overstated the size of the U.S. movement.

Thomas studied a deeper turning route

The reconnaissance led Thomas to focus on Snake Creek Gap, which opened toward Resaca and the railroad about 12 miles south of Dalton.

Bragg became Davis’s military adviser

Jefferson Davis assigned Braxton Bragg to help direct Confederate military operations from Richmond.

Congress restored the rank of lieutenant general

Lincoln signed the measure on February 29, clearing the way for Grant to become general-in-chief of the U.S. armies.

Johnston sought a third infantry corps

He wanted a three-corps organization and recommended W. H. C. Whiting, then Mansfield Lovell, for the new command.

Richmond rejected Johnston’s reorganization

The War Department said the army was too small for a third infantry corps, while Davis distrusted both officers Johnston proposed.

Hood received an existing corps

John B. Hood arrived at Dalton in late February and replaced Thomas C. Hindman as corps commander.

Johnston improved the army’s condition

He restored morale and supply but did not resolve the senior officers’ political and personal divisions.

Johnston relied on Hood instead of Hardee

Hood became Johnston’s favored subordinate while privately criticizing him in correspondence with Richmond.

The command conflict continued in private

Hood and Wheeler sent critical reports to Richmond, increasing distrust between Johnston and Davis before the campaign.
