Episode 197 · September 17, 1862 · Two sectors of the Antietam battlefield near Sharpsburg, Maryland: the Lower Bridge and Antietam Creek on the Federal left, and the Sunken Road from the Hagerstown Turnpike east toward the Roulette farm lane in the Confederate center

ANTIETAM (Part the Twelfth)

Between about 10:00 and 11:00 a.m., the Ninth Corps made two uncoordinated attacks against the Lower Bridge. The 11th Connecticut was repulsed with roughly one-third of the regiment hit, Crook’s brigade stopped upstream, Rodman’s flanking column lost time searching for a usable ford, and the 2nd Maryland was repulsed in Nagle’s attack. Near the Sunken Road, Richardson’s division renewed the Federal offensive. Meagher’s Irish Brigade suffered severe losses, Caldwell extended the line, and Barlow’s enfilading fire helped make the Confederate position untenable. Posey’s failed counterattack, disrupted Confederate command, and a misunderstood order in Rodes’s brigade accelerated the collapse. Federal troops seized the road and entered the Piper cornfield, opening a temporary breach in Lee’s center

Important leaders killed or wounded

United States

Col. Henry W. Kingsbury

Commander of the 11th Connecticut. Hit in the heel, leg, shoulder, and abdomen while leading the first attack near the Lower Bridge. He was carried to the Rohrbach farm and died on September 18, 1862.

United States

Capt. John D. Griswold

Company D, 11th Connecticut. Killed while attempting to lead his company across Antietam Creek during the first bridge attack.

Confederacy

Maj. Gen. Richard H. Anderson

Commander of Anderson’s division. Badly wounded soon after reaching the Sunken Road sector. He survived, but his removal left Roger A. Pryor in command during the collapse.

Confederacy

Brig. Gen. George B. Anderson

Commander of Anderson’s brigade, Hill’s division. Mortally wounded in the ankle. Infection followed an amputation, and he died in Raleigh, North Carolina, on October 16, 1862.

Confederacy

Col. Charles C. Tew

Commander of the 2nd North Carolina and successor to George B. Anderson. Killed in the Sunken Road during the final collapse. The automated transcript renders his name as “Charles II.”

Confederacy

Col. John B. Gordon

Commander of the 6th Alabama. Received his fifth wound of the day at the Sunken Road. He survived and returned to military service in 1863.
