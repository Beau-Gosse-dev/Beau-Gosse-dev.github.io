Episode 210 · August 20–October 3, 1862 · Cumberland Gap and the mountain roads of eastern Kentucky from Manchester through Proctor, Hazel Green, West Liberty, and Grayson to Greenupsburg on the Ohio River

MOUNTAIN TREK FROM CUMBERLAND GAP

Successful United States evacuation. Brig. Gen. George W. Morgan surrendered the strategic Cumberland Gap and destroyed stores and fixed defenses that he could not remove, but brought roughly 7,000 soldiers, about 500 wagons, every mobile gun, and the army’s baggage to the Ohio River after a 219-mile, sixteen-day march. Confederate forces occupied the Gap and harassed the column but failed to trap or destroy the Seventh Division

GarrisonAbout 7,000 soldiers in the Seventh DivisionGeorge W. Morgan’s division comprised four brigades under Samuel P. Carter, James G. Spears, John F. De Courcy, and Absalom Baird. Many soldiers in the Tennessee regiments were East Tennessee Unionists.
Rear guardAbout 200 selected soldiers under Col. George W. GallupGallup’s detachment held back Confederate probes and burned post buildings. The fires illuminated the valley as the division moved north.
Road-clearing forceAbout 1,000 soldiers with tools and block and tackleThe pioneers cleared repeated obstructions. At one blockade they cut four miles of new road through forest to bypass a one-mile section.

People and command

United States — Department of the Ohio

Department of the OhioMaj. Gen. Horatio G. WrightDirected the regional response from Cincinnati and authorized emergency defensive arrangements at Cincinnati and Louisville.
Seventh Division, Army of the OhioBrig. Gen. George W. MorganCommanded roughly 7,000 soldiers at Cumberland Gap, chose the Ohio River route, and controlled the sixteen-day evacuation.
Twenty-fourth BrigadeBrig. Gen. Samuel P. CarterIncluded the 49th Indiana, 3rd Kentucky, and 1st and 2nd Tennessee Infantry; accompanied the wagon train on the North Fork Road.
Twenty-fifth BrigadeBrig. Gen. James G. SpearsEast Tennessee infantry brigade, including the 3rd, 4th, 5th, and 6th Tennessee; marched on the Ridge Road north of Proctor.
Twenty-sixth BrigadeCol. John F. De CourcyIncluded the 22nd Kentucky and 16th and 42nd Ohio Infantry; marched on the Ridge Road north of Proctor.
Twenty-seventh BrigadeBrig. Gen. Absalom BairdIncluded the 33rd Indiana and 14th and 19th Kentucky Infantry; helped protect and move the wagon train on the North Fork Road.
Chief engineerLt. William P. CraighillAdvised evacuation, demolished the fixed defenses and magazines, and blocked the road through the pass.

Confederacy — Department of East Tennessee and Army of Kentucky

Army of KentuckyMaj. Gen. Edmund Kirby SmithDirected Confederate forces in eastern and north-central Kentucky but initially misread George Morgan’s route as a move toward Lexington.
Division investing Cumberland GapBrig. Gen. Carter L. StevensonMaintained the siege with roughly 9,000 men and pursued north after the rear guard destroyed the post.
Second DivisionBrig. Gen. Henry HethProbed the Cincinnati defenses with about 6,000 men, then was recalled through Lexington toward Mount Sterling.
Fourth DivisionBrig. Gen. Patrick R. CleburneRecovered from his Richmond wound and was sent west toward Shelbyville and Louisville rather than against the escaping garrison.
Cavalry commandCol. John Hunt MorganMoved first to Irvine under Kirby Smith’s mistaken order, then harassed the column from Proctor toward Grayson with attacks and roadblocks.
