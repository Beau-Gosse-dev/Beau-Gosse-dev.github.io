Episode 162 · June 27–29, 1862 · From the Gaines’s Mill battlefield and the Chickahominy River crossings east of Richmond through Savage’s Station and White Oak Swamp toward the James River, Virginia

SEVEN DAYS' BATTLES: RETREAT TO THE JAMES

During the night after Gaines’s Mill, Fitz John Porter withdrew the V Corps south of the Chickahominy and the bridges were destroyed. George B. McClellan abandoned the Richmond front and the White House Landing supply line, shifted the Army of the Potomac toward the James River, and placed the army on the roads through White Oak Swamp. Robert E. Lee spent much of June 28 determining the Federal route, then ordered the Army of Northern Virginia to pursue
Episode scopeThe aftermath of Gaines’s Mill and the opening movement toward the JamesThe episode begins on the night of June 27 and ends as Lee orders pursuit on June 29, before the fighting at Savage’s Station.
McClellan’s decisionAbandon the York River supply line and move to the James RiverThe move ended the immediate operation against Richmond. McClellan called it a change of base; operationally, the army withdrew southeast while under Confederate pressure.
Command conferenceBetween about 9 and 11 p.m. on June 27 in the episode’s accountMcClellan met his corps commanders after learning that Porter had been defeated. Erasmus D. Keyes was absent because IV Corps had already been ordered toward White Oak Swamp.
Kearny and Hooker objectThey asked to attack the thin Confederate line south of the ChickahominyPhilip Kearny argued directly against retreat and requested permission to attack John B. Magruder. McClellan denied the request.
McClellan’s telegramSent from Savage’s Station at 12:20 a.m. on June 28The Official Records prints McClellan’s charge that Washington had not sustained the army and his final accusation that officials had done their best to sacrifice it.
Transmission uncertaintyThe final sentence did not reach Secretary of War Edwin M. StantonThe episode follows telegrapher David Homer Bates’s later account that Edward S. Sanford withheld the sentence. The complete sentence still appeared in McClellan’s official report and the Official Records; the deletion account rests on Bates’s 1907 recollection.
Lincoln’s response“Save your Army at all events”Lincoln promised reinforcements but explained that they could not arrive immediately. His June 28 message also defended the decision to protect Washington.
V Corps night withdrawalPorter crossed south of the Chickahominy before dawn on June 28Darkness allowed the defeated force to escape. Engineers destroyed the bridges after the crossing, delaying Confederate pursuit.
John F. Reynolds capturedCaptured after falling asleep during the withdrawalReynolds commanded a brigade in George A. McCall’s Pennsylvania Reserves division. Exhaustion left him north of the river when Confederate troops arrived after daylight.
Artillery lost during the crossingThree guns fell from bridges into the ChickahominyThe episode counts twenty-two guns lost by Porter on June 27: nineteen captured by Confederates and three lost in the river.
Distance to the JamesAbout fifteen miles in a straight line, but farther by usable roadsThe army had to follow an indirect route through swampy terrain toward Glendale, Malvern Hill, and the James River landings.
Scale of the movementAbout 100,000 soldiers and more than 300 artillery piecesThe episode also gives twenty-six heavy siege guns, nearly 4,000 wagons, tens of thousands of horses, and about 2,500 cattle. Totals vary according to reporting date and whether attached personnel and trains are included.
Road conditionsNarrow roads, swamp crossings, rain, animals, and wagon traffic slowed the columnMultiple corps and the supply trains crowded onto a small number of routes. The episode states that alternate roads had not been adequately scouted.
White Oak Swamp crossingThe main bridge was rebuilt by about 7 a.m. on June 28Keyes’s IV Corps began crossing soon afterward. A second crossing at Brackett’s Ford, about one and one-half miles away, was completed later that morning.
White House LandingThe former Federal supply base was abandonedMcClellan no longer intended to preserve the Richmond and York River Railroad connection. Stuart’s cavalry and Ewell’s division moved east to determine the status of the base and railroad.
Lee’s information problemThe destroyed bridges and several plausible Federal routes delayed pursuitLee considered a movement east to retain the railroad, a withdrawal down the Peninsula, or a move south to the James. The episode places Lee’s firm conclusion on June 29; the U.S. Army Center of Military History states that he learned late on June 28 that McClellan was moving south.
Gaines’s Mill aftermathThousands of dead and wounded remained on the battlefieldThe episode estimates nearly 2,400 dead and about 9,000 wounded from both armies on or around the field. Casualty totals differ among official reports and later compilations.
Confederate burialsMany dead were placed in mass graves on June 28Confederate soldiers spent much of the day caring for wounded men and burying the dead while Lee assessed McClellan’s direction.
Operational consequenceLee secured Richmond and retained the initiativeMcClellan’s army remained intact, but his siege approach and York River logistics were abandoned. Further rear-guard battles followed before the army reached the James.
Leader deaths in this episodeNone identifiedThe narrated action includes John F. Reynolds’s capture but no important commander killed or mortally wounded during the episode’s June 27–29 scope.
Recommended book“Mr. Lincoln’s Army” by Bruce CattonThe official episode page and podcast book-recommendations list identify this title.

What happened

The episode resumes after Gaines’s Mill

The account begins with Porter defeated north of the Chickahominy on the night of June 27.

McClellan meets his corps commanders

The conference occurs after Keyes has already been sent toward White Oak Swamp.

Kearny and Hooker oppose retreat

The III Corps division commanders seek permission to attack Magruder’s thin line south of the river.

McClellan prepares the 12:20 a.m. telegram

He sends Stanton a report from Savage’s Station that assigns responsibility for the defeat to insufficient Federal strength.

McClellan states his case

The telegram says that he lost the battle because his force was too small and denies responsibility.

The telegram ends with an accusation

McClellan charges that officials in Washington had done their best to sacrifice the army.

The final sentence is withheld

The episode follows the later account that telegraph official Edward S. Sanford removed the last sentence before delivery to Stanton.

Lincoln answers McClellan

Lincoln directs him to save the army and explains why reinforcements cannot arrive immediately.

The night withdrawal is described

V Corps and supporting troops cross the Chickahominy under cover of darkness.

John F. Reynolds is captured

The exhausted brigade commander wakes after daylight to find Confederate troops around him.

Three artillery pieces fall into the river

The guns are lost from the narrow bridges during the confused night crossing.

Porter completes the crossing

Before sunrise on June 28, V Corps is south of the river and the bridges are destroyed.

The scale of the movement is counted

The episode describes about 100,000 soldiers, more than 300 guns, nearly 4,000 wagons, and large herds of animals.

White Oak Swamp blocks the direct route

Engineers have to restore crossings that Federal troops had previously destroyed.

The main swamp bridge reopens

At about 7 a.m., the rebuilt bridge is ready and Keyes’s IV Corps begins crossing.

A second crossing is completed

Engineers finish a bridge at Brackett’s Ford about one and one-half miles away.

Route planning is assessed

The episode states that inadequate scouting concentrated the march on fewer roads than were available.

The Gaines’s Mill battlefield remains occupied

Confederates tend wounded men and bury dead from both armies while the Federal column moves south.

Jackson examines the captured position

He rides over the ground taken by Hood’s men and comments on the difficulty of the assault.

Lee learns the scale of Porter’s force

Confederate questioning establishes that one corps and one additional division had held the Federal line at Gaines’s Mill.

Lee considers three Federal routes

He evaluates a move east to the railroad, a withdrawal down the Peninsula, and a movement south to the James.

Stuart and Ewell move toward White House

Cavalry and infantry reconnaissance checks whether McClellan intends to protect the railroad and supply base.

White House Landing is found abandoned

Reports eliminate continued reliance on the York River supply line as McClellan’s likely course.

Lee identifies the movement toward the James

The episode places Lee’s firm conclusion on Sunday, June 29, and states that he informed Jefferson Davis.

The operational result is summarized

Lee has forced McClellan away from Richmond, but the Army of the Potomac remains intact and continues toward the James.
