Episode 187 · September 14–15, 1862 · From Turner’s and Fox’s Gaps across Boonsboro and Keedysville to the Antietam Creek valley and Sharpsburg, Maryland, with the Confederate escape route running south to Boteler’s Ford on the Potomac River

ANTIETAM (Part the Second)

No general engagement occurred on September 15. Robert E. Lee halted behind Antietam Creek with about 15,000 men then present, while George B. McClellan brought more than twice that number to the opposite side of the creek but deferred an attack. The pause allowed Confederate formations leaving Harpers Ferry to begin rejoining Lee before the Battle of Antietam

Figures reported in this guide
ScopeReported figure
Lee’s force on handAbout 15,000 men by September 15This is the episode’s estimate for D. H. Hill and Longstreet at Sharpsburg. Twenty-six of the Army of Northern Virginia’s forty brigades were still around Harpers Ferry, a hard march away.
Unreliable field reportsMcClellan forwarded claims that Lee was wounded and had lost 15,000 menThose claims were false. McClellan nevertheless sent the reports to Washington as evidence that the Confederate retreat was a panic and the South Mountain victory complete.
New Federal soldiersAbout 20,000 recent recruitsThe episode identifies eighteen new nine-month regiments totaling about 15,000 men and another 5,000 replacements added to existing regiments shortly before Antietam.
Important leader lossSamuel Garland Jr. had been killed at Fox’s Gap on September 14No senior commander was killed or mortally wounded in the September 15 movements. Garland appears because the episode identifies Duncan K. McRae as the officer leading his brigade during the retreat to Sharpsburg.

Army of Northern VirginiaGen. Robert E. LeeChanged the halt at Sharpsburg into a defensive stand after learning that Harpers Ferry would fall, while ordering detached commands to concentrate with him

Detached commands around Harpers FerryMaj. Gen. Lafayette McLaws and Brig. Gen. John G. WalkerReceived orders to hurry toward Sharpsburg after the surrender. McLaws escaped Pleasant Valley by returning through Harpers Ferry
