What happened

Johnston abandoned Cassville

The Army of Tennessee crossed the Etowah River, destroyed nearby bridges, and moved toward Allatoona Pass.

U.S. morale rose after the retreat

In less than three weeks, Sherman had forced Johnston about halfway from Dalton toward Atlanta without a general battle.

Sherman rested north of the Etowah

Thomas camped near Cassville, McPherson at Kingston, and Schofield near Cass Station while supplies accumulated.

Allatoona Pass offered a strong defense

The railroad cut passed through high ground more than 80 feet deep in places. Johnston expected a direct U.S. advance.

Sherman chose to bypass Allatoona

He planned to cross west of the pass and move about 15 miles south to Dallas, away from his railroad supply line.

The U.S. armies prepared for an off-railroad movement

They built up wagon supplies, evacuated sick and wounded men, and repaired the railroad behind them.

Three armies began a wide wheel west

McPherson crossed near Kingston, Thomas at Gillam’s Bridge, and Hooker and Schofield farther upstream.

Confederate cavalry detected the maneuver

Johnston sent Hardee and Polk southwest toward Dallas while Hood temporarily remained at Allatoona.

Wheeler confirmed the Etowah crossing

On May 24, Confederate cavalry reported most U.S. infantry south of the river. Johnston then ordered Hood toward Dallas.

Johnston formed a new line

By May 25, the Confederates extended from south of Dallas northeast to New Hope Church.

The U.S. march was slow

Poor roads, woods, heat, humidity, limited water, and Confederate cavalry delayed the columns.

Hooker reached New Hope Church

The XX Corps found Alexander P. Stewart’s fortified division with 16 artillery pieces on a low ridge.

Hooker attacked around 4 p.m.

Three divisions advanced in columns of brigades through woods and underbrush. Confederate fire stopped the leading brigade of each column.

The New Hope Church attack failed

Hooker lost about 1,500 men. Confederate losses were probably below 500.

The armies formed parallel lines

On May 26, McPherson held the U.S. right at Dallas, Thomas the center near New Hope Church, and Schofield the left.

Howard attempted to turn the Confederate right

A IV Corps division marched northeast on May 27, but Confederate scouts detected the movement and Johnston extended his line.

The attack began near Pickett’s Mill

At about 4:45 p.m., U.S. troops advanced across rough ground and met Patrick Cleburne’s division on a ridge.

Cleburne repulsed the attack

The leading U.S. brigade lost about 500 men in minutes. Additional units also failed to break the Confederate line.

Pickett’s Mill produced heavy losses

U.S. casualties exceeded 1,500. Cleburne reported 398 infantry casualties, and cavalry losses raised the Confederate total to about 450.

Hood’s counterstroke was canceled

On May 28, Hood approached the exposed U.S. left but withdrew after learning the line had been pulled back and fortified.

Sherman could not continue south

His scattered line, muddy roads, ration shortages, and distance from the railroad made a drive to the Chattahoochee impractical.

The U.S. armies shifted back toward the railroad

McPherson moved northeast first, closing gaps so Thomas and Schofield could follow. Cavalry moved to seize Allatoona Pass.

The U.S. line reunited

By June 1, McPherson reached New Hope Church and U.S. cavalry occupied undefended Allatoona Pass.

Acworth fell and the railroad was regained

U.S. infantry entered Acworth on June 1. By June 4, almost all of Sherman’s armies were back on the railroad.
