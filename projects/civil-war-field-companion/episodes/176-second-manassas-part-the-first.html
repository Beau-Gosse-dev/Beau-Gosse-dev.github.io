Episode 176 · August 28, 1862 · Thoroughfare Gap in the Bull Run Mountains and the Brawner Farm–Groveton area along the Warrenton Turnpike, northern Virginia

SECOND MANASSAS (Part the First)

Two different results shaped the day. Longstreet forced the lightly held passage at Thoroughfare Gap and continued toward Jackson. At Brawner Farm, Jackson’s evening attack on Rufus King’s division ended in a tactical stalemate, but it fixed Pope’s attention on Jackson and led Pope to concentrate for battle on August 29
Episode scopeThursday, August 28: Thoroughfare Gap and Brawner FarmThe episode begins after Jackson’s August 27 destruction of the Manassas Junction depot and ends with the night movements that preceded the main fighting on August 29.
Names for the evening fightBrawner Farm, Groveton, or the engagement near GainesvilleThe National Park Service identifies these as names for the opening action of Second Manassas. The farm was west of Groveton and north of the Warrenton Turnpike.
Jackson’s positionStony Ridge and the unfinished railroad north of the turnpikeJackson concentrated Taliaferro’s, Ewell’s, and A. P. Hill’s divisions near the old First Manassas battlefield and used woods and the railroad cuts and fills for concealment and cover.
Pope’s changing concentration pointManassas Junction, then Centreville, then GrovetonPope first sought Jackson at Manassas Junction, redirected columns toward Centreville after reports that Jackson was moving there, and changed his plan again after learning of the Brawner Farm fight.
Thoroughfare GapRicketts delayed but did not stop LongstreetG. T. Anderson reached the eastern end of the gap about 3 p.m. Ricketts resisted until Evander Law’s flanking movement over Mother Leathercoat Mountain threatened the Federal position near sunset.
Forces at the gapRicketts’s division against elements of Longstreet’s wingAnderson and Henry Benning contested the pass while Law moved around the northern flank. Longstreet also sent Cadmus Wilcox toward Hopewell Gap. Ricketts withdrew toward Gainesville.
Thoroughfare Gap casualtiesAbout 90 United States and no more than 25 ConfederateThese National Park Service estimates apply to the limited fighting at the gap, not to the larger Brawner Farm action.
McDowell’s columnReynolds followed by King on the Warrenton TurnpikeReynolds turned south toward Manassas Junction after a short skirmish with Jackson’s screen. King’s four-brigade division then approached Groveton from Gainesville.
King’s marching orderHatch, Gibbon, Doubleday, and PatrickThe episode places Hatch at the head of the eastbound column, Gibbon behind him, Doubleday next, and Patrick at the rear. The division extended for more than a mile along the turnpike.
Division command failureRufus King was incapacitated by a seizure at about 6 p.m.King’s brigade commanders were initially unaware that he could not direct the division. Gibbon took the principal initiative once Confederate artillery opened.
Opening at Brawner FarmJackson attacked King’s passing column near sunsetConfederate artillery fired from north of the turnpike. Taliaferro’s troops and parts of Ewell’s division then advanced from the Stony Ridge area.
Black Hat Brigade2nd, 6th, and 7th Wisconsin; 19th Indiana; Battery BGibbon imposed regular-army drill and distinctive black Hardee hats. The brigade’s performance at Brawner Farm began the combat record associated with the later name Iron Brigade.
Doubleday’s reinforcement56th Pennsylvania and 76th New York joined GibbonThe two regiments filled a threatened part of the Federal line. Hatch and Patrick did not commit their full brigades to the main fight.
Close-range firefightOpposing lines exchanged volleys at roughly 75 to 100 yardsThe fight continued through fading light and darkness. Open fields, fence lines, smoke, and limited cover produced high losses on both sides.
Jackson’s deploymentA superior force entered the fight in separate incrementsThe Stonewall Brigade made the first principal infantry attack. Lawton, Trimble, and other formations extended or reinforced the Confederate line, but darkness and disrupted command prevented Jackson from using his full strength together.
Confederate command casualtiesEwell and Taliaferro were seriously woundedRichard S. Ewell’s left leg was amputated the next day. William B. Taliaferro was struck by three bullets. Alexander R. Lawton and William E. Starke assumed their division commands.
Regimental leaders killedLawson Botts and John F. NeffThe commanders of the 2nd and 33rd Virginia Infantry were killed in the August 28 fighting. The automated transcript notes two Stonewall Brigade regimental commanders killed but does not give their names.
Brawner Farm forces and lossesAbout 7,000 engaged; nearly one-third became casualtiesNPS summaries give roughly 1,100 to 1,150 United States and about 1,250 Confederate casualties. The episode rounds the losses to about 1,300 on each side.
Stonewall Brigade lossesRoughly one-third to two-fifths of the brigadeNPS accounts use different starting strengths and totals. One summary records 635 present and 350 remaining; another widely cited count gives 340 casualties from about 800.
Lawton’s 26th GeorgiaA 72 percent casualty rate in the episode’s accountThe episode also describes one company of the 21st Georgia losing 40 of 45 men. These regimental and company figures are subsets of the broader Confederate total.
Tactical result at Brawner FarmStalemate after darkThe Federal line held against Jackson’s attacks. Gibbon and Doubleday then withdrew in order to the turnpike, and the exhausted Confederates did not pursue.
Operational effectPope concentrated against Jackson while Longstreet approachedPope treated the fight as proof that Jackson was isolated and vulnerable. Longstreet’s successful passage of the mountains meant that the Confederate wings would reunite on August 29.
Night movementKing’s division withdrew toward Manassas Junction at about 1 a.m.King and his brigade commanders chose not to remain exposed to a renewed attack. Pope’s latest concentration orders did not reach them in time to prevent the movement.
Recommended book“Civil War Infantry Tactics: Training, Combat, and Small-Unit Effectiveness” by Earl J. HessThe episode connects the recommendation to the close-order infantry fighting at Brawner Farm.
Transcript normalizationsBrawner, Groveton, Taliaferro, Ewell, Hardee hats, and Irvin McDowellThe automated transcript produces “Bronner,” “Grofton,” “Tolliver,” “Yule,” “hardy hats,” and “Urban McDowell.” It also renders Georgians in Trimble’s brigade as “Persians.”

What happened

The August 27 depot raid is reviewed

The episode begins with Jackson leaving the destroyed Federal supply depot at Manassas Junction.

Jackson moves toward the old battlefield

His three divisions head about seven miles north to await Lee and Longstreet near the First Manassas field.

Stony Ridge becomes Jackson’s concealed position

The wooded ridge and unfinished railroad north of the turnpike provide cover and a position from which to strike passing columns.

Pope directs columns toward Manassas Junction

McDowell’s III Corps advances east on the Warrenton Turnpike, unaware of Jackson’s full strength near its route.

Buford reports Confederates behind McDowell

McDowell sends James B. Ricketts’s division back toward Thoroughfare Gap after cavalry reports Longstreet’s approach.

Longstreet reaches Thoroughfare Gap

The head of Longstreet’s column encounters Ricketts’s infantry and Federal cavalry guarding the eastern side of the pass.

Ricketts delays the passage

Federal resistance lasts several hours before Confederate troops climb the slopes and threaten the position’s flank.

The missed opportunity at the gap is assessed

Using John Hennessy’s analysis, the episode argues that Pope did not place enough force at the pass to keep Lee’s wings separated.

The Thoroughfare Gap firing ends

As Ricketts withdraws, Lee and Longstreet hear the separate battle beginning to the east and continue toward Jackson.

Jackson has chosen to open the eastern fight

The episode shifts from Lee and Longstreet at the gap to Jackson observing McDowell’s passing columns from concealment.

Jackson’s screen fires on Reynolds

A brief skirmish does not reveal the full Confederate position, and Reynolds continues before turning toward Manassas Junction.

King is redirected toward Centreville

At about 5 p.m., McDowell receives Pope’s new order and turns King’s division east along the turnpike.

King cannot exercise command

After McDowell rides away, King suffers another seizure and the division’s brigade commanders do not know why he is absent.

The division’s marching order is identified

Hatch leads, followed by Gibbon, Doubleday, and Patrick on a column extending more than a mile.

John Gibbon and his brigade are introduced

The episode reviews Gibbon’s regular-army background, the brigade’s Midwestern regiments, its drill, and its black Hardee hats.

King’s division passes Jackson’s front

Near 6 p.m., the eastbound Federal column below Stony Ridge presents Jackson with a target.

Jackson orders the attack

The quoted order “Bring out your men, gentlemen” begins the movement against King’s column.

Confederate artillery opens

After the advertisement break, the narrative resumes with Taliaferro’s and Ewell’s troops advancing across the Brawner fields.

Gibbon takes the initiative

He brings up artillery and sends the 2nd Wisconsin north of the turnpike to drive off what appears to be an exposed battery.

The 2nd Wisconsin meets the Stonewall Brigade

About 450 Federals and roughly 800 Virginians exchange fire at approximately 80 yards while Gibbon deploys the rest of his brigade.

Doubleday reinforces Gibbon

With King still absent, Doubleday is the only other brigade commander to send regiments into the main Federal line.

The firing line extends for nearly half a mile

The 19th Indiana, three Wisconsin regiments, and Doubleday’s reinforcements face Virginians, Georgians, North Carolinians, and Alabamians.

Jackson’s use of force is evaluated

The episode argues that he does not translate his overall numerical advantage into a coordinated tactical attack.

The general assault is repulsed

At about 7:30 p.m., Confederate units advance separately in near-darkness and stop about 30 yards from the Federal line.

Senior Confederate losses are counted

Taliaferro and Ewell are wounded, two Stonewall Brigade regimental commanders are killed, and Ewell’s leg is amputated the next day.

Georgia losses illustrate the intensity

The episode gives 40 casualties from 45 men in one 21st Georgia company and a 72 percent casualty rate for the 26th Georgia.

Jackson’s last flanking effort fails

Another brigade moves toward the Federal left but makes no further progress as darkness limits control and visibility.

The fight ends in stalemate

The musketry declines after dark with neither side having broken the other’s line.

Losses are estimated

The episode rounds the casualties to about 1,300 on each side and concludes that Jackson missed the chance to destroy King’s isolated division.

Jackson credits the Federal defense

His report states that the Federals maintained their position with determined resistance.

King’s officers decide to withdraw

They choose to move toward Manassas Junction rather than remain for another attack in the morning.

Pope changes the concentration point again

After receiving news from Brawner Farm, he orders Federal columns toward Jackson’s position near Groveton.

Pope assumes Jackson remains isolated

The episode emphasizes that Pope continues to discount Longstreet’s approach with about 30,000 additional Confederates.

The Confederate wings are about to reunite

The episode closes the military narrative with Lee and Longstreet approaching Jackson before the August 29 fighting.

The book recommendation begins

The hosts recommend Earl J. Hess’s study of Civil War infantry training, maneuver, and small-unit combat.
