What happened

Grant moved around Lee’s right again

After the Spotsylvania operations and failure of supporting campaigns elsewhere, Grant moved toward the North Anna rather than continue attacking the existing Confederate line.

Grant’s objective was Lee’s army

Lee expected the U.S. movement to focus on Richmond. Grant’s principal goal was to defeat the Army of Northern Virginia.

Lee saw a chance to concentrate Confederate forces

The U.S. Army had suffered heavy losses and longer supply lines. Lee had received more than 10,000 reinforcements and was only about forty miles from Beauregard.

Lee asked Davis to unite with Beauregard

On May 23 Lee proposed concentrating the available Confederate forces against Grant before the Army of the Potomac could rest and reinforce.

Grant wanted Lee to leave his earthworks

Grant believed open battle offered the best chance to destroy Lee’s army. He did not intend to pause despite Confederate reinforcements.

Grant prepared to end the Bermuda Hundred operation

He planned to retain City Point but bring most of the Army of the James north, under William F. Smith in the field, to reinforce the Army of the Potomac.

Poor maps delayed the U.S. march

II and V Corps crossed paths while seeking incorrectly marked or nonexistent destinations. The columns required several hours to separate and resume their routes.

Two U.S. corps reached separate crossings

Hancock approached Chesterfield Bridge while Warren reached Jericho Mill about five miles upstream.

Warren crossed at Jericho Mill

Infantry waded the waist-deep river while engineers built a pontoon bridge for wagons and artillery. Two divisions were south of the river by mid-afternoon.

Wilcox attacked the V Corps bridgehead

At about 5:45 p.m., Wilcox’s Confederate division drove back two U.S. brigades before resistance strengthened.

U.S. artillery stabilized the line

Twelve guns crossed the new pontoon bridge. Charles Wainwright’s batteries held while infantry reformed, and Wilcox withdrew after dark.

Jericho Mill cost more than a thousand casualties

The episode reports about 730 Confederate and 377 U.S. casualties in the fighting around Warren’s bridgehead.

Hancock attacked the Chesterfield Bridge redoubt

Twenty guns bombarded three Confederate regiments on the north bank. Three U.S. brigades overran the position at 6 p.m. and secured the bridge approach.

Lee blamed Hill for using only one division

Lee argued that Hill should have committed the full corps against Warren. Hill had received inaccurate cavalry reports that only two U.S. brigades had crossed.

Lee’s commanders designed a new line

The river crossings could not be defended, but abandoning the area would surrender Hanover Junction. Lee, his chief engineer, and senior commanders developed a position anchored at Ox Ford.

Grant ordered a general advance on May 24

Hancock crossed at Chesterfield Bridge, Warren and Wright advanced from Jericho Mill, and Burnside was ordered to cross between them at Ox Ford.

Initial reports said Lee had withdrawn

Escaped enslaved people and the lack of resistance led Grant to tell Halleck that Lee was falling back toward Richmond.

Warren and Wright reached the railroad

They occupied Noel Station and captured about 200 Confederates who had been left asleep when Wilcox withdrew.

Burnside found Ox Ford strongly defended

The south bank dominated the crossing and Confederate earthworks covered the bluff. Burnside shifted part of his corps upstream to Quarles Ford.

U.S. columns found Lee’s new works

A V Corps division crossed near Quarles Ford and encountered entrenchments. Hancock’s advance also struck Confederate skirmishers and a second defensive line near Hanover Junction.

Ledlie attacked without the rest of his division

James Ledlie sent about 1,500 men toward Mahone’s entrenched veterans near Ox Ford. Confederate cannon and rifle fire broke the assault within minutes and captured about one hundred men.

The shape of Lee’s position became clear

The Confederate line formed an inverted V with its point at Ox Ford and its sides covering Hanover Junction.

Ox Ford divided the Army of the Potomac

Grant’s wings could not reinforce each other without recrossing the river and marching about six miles. Lee could shift troops along shorter interior lines.
