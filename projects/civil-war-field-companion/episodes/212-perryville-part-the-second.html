Episode 212 · October 1–8, 1862 · Central Kentucky from Louisville, Bardstown, and Frankfort to Harrodsburg, Danville, and Perryville; the episode ends at Peters Hill and Doctors Creek west of Perryville

PERRYVILLE (Part the Second)

Operational United States success leading into battle. Don Carlos Buell’s rapid advance forced the Confederates to abandon Bardstown and Frankfort, while Braxton Bragg’s faulty information and divided command delayed a coherent response. Bragg ordered Leonidas Polk to fight at Perryville as a temporary check before concentrating at Harrodsburg. Before dawn on October 8, Daniel McCook’s United States brigade drove the 7th Arkansas from Peters Hill and secured Doctors Creek. The main Battle of Perryville occurs after this episode’s narrative ends

Step 1 of 35

Buell’s four-column plan resumes

One column would demonstrate toward Frankfort while the other three advanced through Bardstown to force battle or threaten the Confederate line of communications.

People and command

United States — Army of the Ohio

Army of the OhioMaj. Gen. Don Carlos BuellDirected the four-column advance from Louisville and accompanied Gilbert’s central column; Maj. Gen. George H. Thomas served as second in command.
I CorpsMaj. Gen. Alexander McD. McCookCommanded the left column from Louisville; included Lovell Rousseau’s 3rd Division and James S. Jackson’s 10th Division.
II CorpsMaj. Gen. Thomas L. CrittendenCommanded the southern column on roads through and beyond Bardstown.
III CorpsMaj. Gen. Charles C. GilbertCommanded the central pursuit toward Perryville, including Philip Sheridan’s 11th Division.
11th Division, III CorpsBrig. Gen. Philip H. SheridanHeld the Springfield Pike approach west of Perryville and ordered Daniel McCook to secure Doctors Creek and Peters Hill.
36th Brigade, 11th DivisionCol. Daniel McCook Jr.Led the pre-dawn advance by the 52nd Ohio, 85th Illinois, and 125th Illinois that opened the infantry fighting.
Detached 2nd DivisionBrig. Gen. Joshua W. SillDemonstrated toward Frankfort, drove out the Confederate political ceremony, and then moved south to rejoin Buell.

Confederacy — Kentucky campaign forces

Overall command in KentuckyGen. Braxton BraggControlled the Army of Mississippi and assumed authority over Edmund Kirby Smith’s Army of Kentucky, though the armies remained operationally divided.
Army of KentuckyMaj. Gen. Edmund Kirby SmithMoved toward Frankfort for Bragg’s proposed concentration and later received orders to join the armies near Harrodsburg.
Right Wing, Army of Mississippi; local commander at PerryvilleMaj. Gen. Leonidas PolkOrdered the Bardstown retreat, brought Benjamin Cheatham’s division toward Perryville, and took command of roughly 16,000 Confederates there.
Left Wing, Army of MississippiMaj. Gen. William J. HardeeWithdrew from Bardstown under cavalry cover and halted his divisions under Simon B. Buckner and Patton Anderson around Perryville.
Division, Polk’s Right WingMaj. Gen. Benjamin F. CheathamMoved south on the Harrodsburg Pike to reinforce Hardee under Bragg’s order for the October 8 attack.
Arkansas brigade, Buckner’s DivisionBrig. Gen. St. John R. LiddellHeld Bottom Hill and posted the 7th Arkansas forward on Peters Hill to watch the Springfield Pike and Doctors Creek.
Cavalry brigadeCol. Joseph WheelerCovered Hardee’s withdrawal, checked United States cavalry at Pottsville, and drew back toward Perryville on October 7.

Important leaders killed or wounded

United States

Brig. Gen. James S. Jackson

Commander, 10th Division, I Corps. Killed in the main battle on October 8. The episode foreshadows his death after describing his meeting with Sheridan, Terrill, and Webster the night before.

United States

Brig. Gen. William R. Terrill

33rd Brigade commander, 10th Division, I Corps. Mortally wounded by shell fragments during the main battle and died that night. He was one of the three officers from the October 7 meeting who did not survive.

United States

Col. George P. Webster

34th Brigade commander, 10th Division, I Corps. Mortally wounded during the main battle and died soon afterward. Sheridan was the sole survivor of the four officers who met on the eve of battle.
