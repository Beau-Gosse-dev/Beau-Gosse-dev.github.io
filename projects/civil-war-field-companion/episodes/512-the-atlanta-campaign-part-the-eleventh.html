What happened

The Confederates left Resaca

On May 16, Johnston crossed the Oostanaula River and continued south through more open farming country.

The railroad connected the next objectives

The Western and Atlantic ran through Calhoun, Adairsville, Kingston, Cass Station, and Cartersville before crossing the Etowah.

Johnston rejected a position near Calhoun

A creek split the proposed line and would have prevented the wings from supporting each other.

Adairsville was also unsuitable

Johnston found the valley too wide to defend even after Polk’s reinforcements arrived.

Johnston planned an ambush farther south

Hardee argued for a stand at Adairsville, but Johnston instead looked for a way to strike part of Sherman’s divided pursuit.

Sherman spread his armies across a wide front

Thomas followed the railroad, Hooker and Schofield crossed farther east, and McPherson crossed at Lay’s Ferry.

Wheeler delayed the pursuit

Confederate cavalry slowed Thomas and McPherson, while Hooker and Schofield delayed each other during their crossing.

U.S. troops occupied Rome

A XIV Corps division and cavalry moved down the right bank of the Oostanaula and entered Rome on May 18.

Johnston used two roads toward Cassville

Polk and Hood marched directly from Adairsville; Hardee and the wagon train moved through Kingston and then east.

Sherman divided his pursuit

McPherson and most of Thomas moved toward Kingston, while Hooker and Schofield took the direct Cassville road.

The Confederate army concentrated north of Cassville

Polk blocked the direct road, Hood formed to his right, and Hardee formed on the left. Reinforcements raised the army to about 75,000.

Hood moved to attack on May 19

Accounts differ over who initiated the plan. Hood moved east of the direct road to strike the U.S. column’s flank.

An unexpected U.S. force appeared on Hood’s right

U.S. troops moving on a country road encountered Hood. He halted, deployed skirmishers, and abandoned the planned attack.

The threat to Hood was real

Later evidence confirmed U.S. troops were present and could have struck his flank or rear if he continued.

Johnston formed a new ridge line

Hood held the right, Polk the center, and Hardee the left on high ground south of Cassville.

U.S. artillery enfiladed part of Polk’s line

Batteries northeast of the ridge fired along an angled section of the Confederate position.

Hood and Polk met Johnston

The generals agreed the artillery compromised the position, but their later accounts differed over whether they favored attack or retreat.

Hardee opposed another withdrawal

He arrived after Johnston had decided to leave and objected to abandoning the ridge.

McPherson secured Woolley’s Bridge

U.S. troops captured an Etowah crossing that could have placed them behind the Cassville position and cut Johnston off from Atlanta.

The Confederate army withdrew at midnight

Small parties maintained campfires while the main force left Cassville and crossed south of the Etowah.
