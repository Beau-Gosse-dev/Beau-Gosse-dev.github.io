Episode 179 · August 30–September 4, 1862 · Chinn Ridge and Henry Hill at Manassas, the withdrawal across Bull Run to Centreville, and Ox Hill near Chantilly, Virginia

SECOND MANASSAS (Part the Fourth)

Confederate victory at Second Manassas. United States stands on Chinn Ridge and Henry Hill delayed Longstreet long enough for Pope’s army to escape across Bull Run. Lee retained the initiative, but the September 1 battle at Ox Hill/Chantilly was indecisive and did not cut the Federal route toward Washington

Important leaders killed or wounded

United States

Lt. Col. Joseph A. McLean

Commander, 88th Pennsylvania Infantry, Tower’s brigade. Mortally wounded while his regiment supported the 5th Maine Light Artillery on Chinn Ridge. An Ohio lieutenant tried to control the bleeding before the Confederate advance passed over the position; McLean died on the field.

United States

Col. John A. Koltes

Commander, 1st Brigade, Schurz’s division, I Corps. Killed by Confederate artillery while leading his brigade south across the Warrenton Turnpike in the final effort to hold Chinn Ridge.

United States

Col. Fletcher Webster

Commander, 12th Massachusetts Infantry, Stiles’s brigade. Mortally wounded on Chinn Ridge while attempting to recover the overrun 5th Maine Light Artillery guns. He was captured and died later on August 30.

United States

Brig. Gen. Isaac I. Stevens

Commander, 1st Division, IX Corps detachment. Killed at Chantilly on September 1 while carrying the colors of the 79th New York and leading an attack into Jackson’s line during the thunderstorm.

United States

Maj. Gen. Philip Kearny

Commander, 1st Division, III Corps, Army of the Potomac. Killed at Chantilly after riding into Edward L. Thomas’s Georgia brigade in rain and darkness and being shot while turning his horse away. Lee returned his body under a flag of truce.
