Episode 122 · April 6, 1862, about 2:30 p.m. to 6 p.m.; later interpretation of the battle · The Hornet’s Nest, the ridge west of Pittsburg Landing, Dill Branch, and the Tennessee River, Tennessee

SHILOH (Part the Twelfth)

Grant ordered chief of staff Joseph D. Webster to build a final defensive line near Pittsburg Landing. Webster placed five 24-pounder siege guns near the Corinth Road and collected field artillery around them. Hurlbut, Sherman, McClernand, and survivors from Wallace’s and Prentiss’s divisions joined the line as they withdrew. By late afternoon, the position extended about 2,500 yards from the river and held roughly 18,000 men according to the episode; the NPS estimates nearly 20,000 before Buell’s troops landed. The gunboats Tyler and Lexington supported the left flank. The Hornet’s Nest stand delayed some Confederate formations, but the final line was already strong before its defenders surrendered, and modern historians dispute the older claim that the stand alone saved Grant’s army

What happened

The episode reviews the Hornet’s Nest surrender

Wallace was mortally wounded, and Prentiss and about 2,200 men from the 2nd and 6th Divisions were captured.

Prentiss promoted a postwar interpretation

Prentiss argued that the Hornet’s Nest stand gave Grant time to build the final defensive line and therefore saved the army.

Both flanks had withdrawn by about 4:30 p.m.

Sherman, McClernand, and Hurlbut moved toward the final line while Wallace and Prentiss continued holding the center.

The final line was already strong by 3 p.m.

Additional withdrawn units continued joining the position before the Hornet’s Nest surrendered.

The stand delayed the Confederate concentration

Confederate forces focused on surrounding the center, but the captured defenders were then unavailable to reinforce Grant’s line.

David W. Reed shaped later battlefield interpretation

The wounded 12th Iowa veteran later became historian of the Shiloh park commission and emphasized the Hornet’s Nest in his published history.

The episode examines Grant’s responsibility

Grant’s last order to Prentiss was to hold. The episode argues that he should have sent a new order after the adjacent divisions withdrew.

Webster was ordered to form the last line

Grant assigned his chief of staff to collect artillery and available infantry near Pittsburg Landing.

Webster positioned the siege guns

Starting around 2:30 p.m., he placed five 24-pounders across the likely approach along the Corinth Road and added available field batteries.

Withdrawn divisions joined the line

Hurlbut rallied his division near the artillery. Sherman and McClernand formed farther right, and escaped fragments of the 2nd and 6th Divisions also joined.

The final position extended from the river

The line ran about 2,500 yards west from the Tennessee River and curved north, with Sherman protecting the outer right.

The gunboats supported the left flank

Tyler and Lexington engaged Confederate batteries, then positioned near Dill Branch where their guns could fire into the ravine.

Confederates reorganized after the surrender

Bragg and Polk tried to separate units tangled during the Hornet’s Nest encirclement and move prisoners to the rear.

Chalmers and Jackson attacked near 6 p.m.

Their brigades advanced toward the United States left with less than twenty minutes remaining before sunset and without a fully coordinated supporting attack.
