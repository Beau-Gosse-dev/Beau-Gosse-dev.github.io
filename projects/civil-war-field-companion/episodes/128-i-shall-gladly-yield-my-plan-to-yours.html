Episode 128 · December 1, 1861–February 3, 1862 · Washington, D.C.; the United States and Confederate lines around Centreville and Manassas, Virginia; and McClellan’s proposed route through the Chesapeake Bay, Rappahannock River, Urbanna, and Richmond

"I SHALL GLADLY YIELD MY PLAN TO YOURS"

Lincoln’s effort to force an advance produced two war orders and a direct strategic test for McClellan. Lincoln favored an overland movement against the Confederate railroad communications southwest of Manassas; McClellan argued for transporting the Army of the Potomac by water to Urbanna and approaching Richmond from the east. McClellan’s argument prevailed for the moment, and Lincoln’s January 31 order was not carried out. When Joseph E. Johnston withdrew from the Centreville–Manassas line in March, the Urbanna concept lost its intended advantage and McClellan shifted the landing point to Fort Monroe for the Peninsula Campaign
Immediate planning crisisJanuary 10–13, 1862 White House conferencesMcClellan was recovering from typhoid fever when Lincoln consulted Irvin McDowell, William B. Franklin, Montgomery C. Meigs, and cabinet members.
Opposing armiesAbout 30 miles apartThe Army of the Potomac guarded Washington while Joseph E. Johnston’s Confederates wintered around Centreville and Manassas.
Lincoln’s preferred lineOverland toward the railroad southwest of Manassas
McClellan’s preferred lineBy water to Urbanna, then overland toward RichmondUrbanna is the correct Virginia place-name; the automated transcript renders it “Urbana.”
Proposed waterborne force110,000–140,000 soldiersMcClellan gave this range in his January 31 memorandum to Secretary of War Edwin M. Stanton.
Transportation estimate30 days after authorizationMcClellan proposed collecting canal boats, barges, schooners, wood boats, and small steamers.
General War Order No. 1Issued January 27, 1862It named February 22 as the date for a general movement of United States land and naval forces.
Special War Order No. 1Issued January 31, 1862It directed the Army of the Potomac to seize a point on the railroad southwest of Manassas Junction while leaving Washington secure.
Lincoln’s testFive questions about time, cost, certainty, value, communications, and retreat
Immediate outcomeMcClellan’s plan accepted in principleThe January 31 order was never carried out, although Lincoln did not formally revoke it.
Combat in this episodeNoneThe episode concerns command, logistics, and campaign planning rather than a battle.
