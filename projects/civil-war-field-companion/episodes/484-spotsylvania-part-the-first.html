SPOTSYLVANIA (Part the First)

Grant moved southeast instead of retreating after the Wilderness, but Lee anticipated the move. Anderson’s Confederate infantry marched through the night, reinforced Stuart’s cavalry at Laurel Hill, and stopped Warren’s V Corps short of the Spotsylvania crossroads. Grant then allowed Sheridan to leave with most of the U.S. cavalry to fight Stuart

What happened

The Wilderness battle ended without a decision

The episode counts more than 17,500 U.S. casualties from about 102,000 engaged and about 13,000 Confederate casualties from just over 61,000.

Grant decided not to retreat

He broke contact in the Wilderness and moved southeast around Lee’s right toward Spotsylvania Court House.

Both armies remained behind their works on May 7

Neither renewed the main battle. Patrols and skirmishers remained active while commanders determined the next movement.

Grant shifted his supply line east

Pontoon bridges were removed from Germanna Ford and sent to Ely’s Ford to speed evacuation of wounded soldiers toward Fredericksburg.

Lee concluded Grant was moving away from the Rapidan

The abandoned Germanna Ford route showed that Grant was not retreating north. Lee identified Fredericksburg and Spotsylvania as the two likely destinations.

Roads made Spotsylvania strategically important

Brock Road met routes from Fredericksburg and Richmond near the courthouse. A U.S. arrival first would place Grant between Lee and Richmond.

Lee sent Stuart to cover the roads

At about 2 p.m. on May 7, Confederate cavalry received orders to delay any U.S. movement toward Spotsylvania.

Anderson’s corps received the lead march assignment

James Longstreet was wounded, so Richard Anderson led the corps nearest Spotsylvania. Its position lacked a direct road south.

Confederates opened a military road

William Pendleton cut a rough track from Anderson’s position to Catharpin Road. Infantry could use it, but the artillery had to take the longer route through Parker’s Store.

Lee ordered Anderson to move at 3 a.m.

The original plan was to leave the works after dark, rest briefly, and begin the main march early on May 8.

Anderson continued through the night

His troops started by 10 p.m. Fires and rough ground prevented a bivouac, so the corps kept marching toward Spotsylvania without rest.

The Army of the Potomac began its night march

Warren moved down Brock Road, Sedgwick took a route through Chancellorsville, and Burnside and Hancock covered the rear.

Traffic stalled the U.S. columns

Ambulances, artillery, infantry, fatigue, and darkness backed up Brock Road for miles and slowed the march to a few steps at a time.

Meade found two cavalry divisions at Todd’s Tavern

Gregg and Merritt had no instructions about the night movement even though Meade expected Sheridan’s cavalry to screen the route.

Meade issued orders directly to Gregg and Merritt

Gregg was sent toward Corbin’s Bridge and Merritt toward Blockhouse Bridge through Spotsylvania to deny the Po River crossings to Lee.

The cavalry left Todd’s Tavern at 3 a.m.

Gregg moved west on Catharpin Road while Merritt rode south on Brock Road.

Fitzhugh Lee blocked Brock Road

Felled trees, dismounted cavalry, and artillery stopped Merritt about two miles south of Todd’s Tavern. By daylight his entire division was engaged.

Merritt requested infantry support

John C. Robinson’s V Corps division was about a mile behind. Warren sent Robinson’s leading brigade to clear the road.

Sheridan deployed the arriving infantry

Robinson’s troops initially pushed the Confederate cavalry south, but heat and exhaustion quickly slowed them.

Confederates formed a position at Laurel Hill

Fitzhugh Lee’s troopers built abatis and fence-rail breastworks along a pine-covered rise with a clear field of fire across roughly 400 yards.

Warren ordered an immediate attack

Robinson preferred to wait for his full division, but Warren decided to attack before the defense could grow stronger.

Peter Lyle’s brigade reached within 60 yards

A close Confederate volley stopped the advance. V Corps brigades then entered the fight separately as each arrived.

Robinson was severely wounded

A bullet shattered his left knee. The leg was amputated, ending his field service, and the V Corps assault collapsed.

Anderson’s infantry reached Laurel Hill

Two brigades from Joseph Kershaw’s division reinforced the cavalry in time to repel Warren’s attacks. Two others continued to secure Spotsylvania Court House.

Wilson reached the courthouse and withdrew

James Wilson’s cavalry arrived before Kershaw’s infantry, but Sheridan ordered it back because it was isolated and unsupported.

Meade learned Confederate infantry held the road

He ordered Sedgwick to deploy VI Corps on Warren’s left and directed both corps to attack again.

Meade confronted Sheridan

Meade blamed the cavalry for failing to screen the march and block Confederate routes. Sheridan objected to Meade issuing orders directly to cavalry division commanders.

Grant authorized Sheridan’s independent raid

When told Sheridan said he could defeat Stuart, Grant approved the proposal. Meade ordered the cavalry corps to move against Confederate cavalry and later resupply at Haxall’s Landing.
