Episode 198 · September 17, 1862 · The Antietam battlefield at the Sunken Road and Piper farm east of Sharpsburg, then south along Antietam Creek from the Lower Bridge to the ridges and farms between the bridge and Sharpsburg, Maryland

ANTIETAM (Part the Thirteenth)

At the Confederate center, improvised counterattacks and a concentrated artillery line stopped Richardson’s advance after the Sunken Road breakthrough. Richardson intended to renew the attack but was mortally wounded; McClellan sent Winfield Scott Hancock to hold the captured ground instead. At about 1:00 p.m., the 51st Pennsylvania and 51st New York captured the Lower Bridge. The Ninth Corps then required about two hours to cross, resupply, and reorganize. Its advance began about 3:00 p.m. and pushed D. R. Jones’s outnumbered defenders toward Sharpsburg. The episode ends at about 3:30 p.m., when Lee identified A. P. Hill’s column approaching from Harpers Ferry; Hill’s counterattack occurs in the next episode

What happened

The broken Confederate center is recapped

Federal troops have crossed the Sunken Road and entered the Piper cornfield while Richardson presses the advantage.

Hill organizes a counterattack

D. H. Hill and his officers rally a mixed Confederate force on the Piper farm and send it toward the road.

The 5th New Hampshire waits in the road

Cross’s veterans use the captured Sunken Road as cover while Confederates approach through the cornfield.

Cross orders “war paint”

The New Hampshire soldiers smear black powder on their faces and answer the rebel yell with their own shouted war cry.

A Confederate color bearer reaches close range

The counterattack comes within about fifteen yards of the road before Federal fire drops the flag bearer.

The 81st Pennsylvania reinforces the defense

Its arrival helps the 5th New Hampshire repel the Confederate attack.

Hill leads another charge

Hill takes a musket and personally advances with about 200 men, but the small force is broken and dispersed.

Miller’s battery opens with canister

The Washington Artillery battery in the Piper orchard uses double canister as Federal infantry approaches.

Longstreet’s staff helps serve the guns

With gun crews depleted by casualties, staff officers take positions at the artillery pieces.

The Confederate infantry center is reduced

The episode estimates that fewer than 2,000 disorganized infantry remain in the center.

Twenty cannon support the center

The assembled Confederate gun line compensates for the shattered infantry formations in front of it.

Richardson’s forward movement stops

Artillery fire, exhaustion, and dwindling ammunition contain the advance beyond the Sunken Road.

The division returns to defensible ground

Richardson draws the forward troops back toward the road and the ridge used earlier in the attack.

Richardson plans to renew the attack

He expects to move again after resupply and after fresh infantry and rifled guns arrive.

The episode shifts to the Confederate right

Rodman is reaching Snavely’s Ford while Cox organizes a third assault on the Lower Bridge.

The twin 51st regiments receive the bridge assault

The 51st Pennsylvania and 51st New York of Ferrero’s brigade are selected for the next attempt.

Patterson asks for the whiskey ration

Ferrero promises the Pennsylvanians their whiskey if they take the bridge.

Cox chooses a direct approach

The storming party moves down the hillside toward the bridge with stronger artillery and infantry support.

The two regiments start downhill

The 51st Pennsylvania and 51st New York advance at a run toward Toombs’s Georgians.

The assault pauses under fire

The Pennsylvanians take cover behind a stone wall and the New Yorkers use a fence downstream.

George Whitman describes the firefight

The 51st New York lieutenant records the intensity of the Federal fire across Antietam Creek.

The Georgia defenders begin to waver

They have held the crossing for about three hours and their cartridge boxes are nearly empty.

The color bearers lead the final rush

The 51st Pennsylvania charges onto the bridge and the 51st New York joins the movement.

Federal troops reach the west bank

The Confederate defenders withdraw and the bridge passes to the Ninth Corps.

The Lower Bridge is captured

At about 1:00 p.m., the Ninth Corps secures the crossing later known as Burnside Bridge.

Richardson is mortally wounded

At roughly the same time, a Confederate shell fragment strikes him near the Sunken Road artillery line.

Richardson’s requests are reviewed

Before being hit, he called for fresh infantry and artillery to support another advance.

Fresh Federal troops are available

The episode identifies Smith’s and Slocum’s VI Corps divisions and most of Porter’s V Corps as uncommitted resources.

Hancock is sent to replace Richardson

McClellan personally gives Hancock instructions before he rides to the division.

Hancock orders the line to hold

He tells the troops to remain in position, matching McClellan’s decision to consolidate the captured ground.

McClellan drafts a message to Halleck

The 1:25 p.m. dispatch reports the battle’s severity and McClellan’s belief that he faces unfavorable odds.

The center attack is not renewed

The episode argues that McClellan fails to exploit the Confederate infantry collapse beyond the Sunken Road.

Burnside reports a secure bridgehead

He tells headquarters that the Ninth Corps has crossed the Antietam and can hold the west bank.

McClellan shifts VI Corps north

Smith’s and Slocum’s divisions move toward the East Woods to guard against the Confederate counterattack McClellan fears.

Thomas Key carries a new order

McClellan sends his aide to require the Ninth Corps to continue toward Sharpsburg.

Key may relieve Burnside

McClellan authorizes the aide to remove Burnside if the orders will not be carried out; Burnside promises to advance.

Crossing and supply problems delay the corps

Sturgis needs ammunition, Willcox encounters a bottleneck at the bridge, and Rodman must form after the ford crossing.

The Ninth Corps becomes ready at 3:00 p.m.

Two hours after the bridge capture, Burnside’s attacking divisions receive the order to move toward Sharpsburg.

The northern battlefield grows quiet

The episode estimates 18,500 casualties in the area from the Miller cornfield to the Sunken Road.

Franklin proposes an attack in the West Woods

He and his two division commanders believe VI Corps can renew the offensive against the Confederate left.

Sumner vetoes Franklin’s plan

The senior commander fears a repulse would leave no organized force to stop a Confederate counterattack.

McClellan rides to the northern front

At about 2:00 p.m., he leaves the Pry House and hears the opposing assessments from Sumner and Franklin.

Sumner’s view prevails

McClellan accepts the recommendation against another attack on the northern part of the field.

McClellan declines to risk the right

Franklin later records McClellan’s conclusion that another northern attack would be unsafe.

The Ninth Corps advance begins

About 8,500 Federals in the episode’s estimate move west while Sturgis remains in reserve.

D. R. Jones holds the Confederate right

His reduced division protects Sharpsburg and the army’s route toward Shepherdstown Ford.

The advance divides into two thrusts

Willcox moves directly toward Sharpsburg while Rodman advances south of town before turning right.

Broken terrain slows Willcox

Fences, orchards, fields, ridges, and farm buildings divide the advance into a series of local fights.

V Corps regulars support the Middle Bridge batteries

The infantry provides limited support on Willcox’s right but remains tied to the connecting sector.

Pleasonton requests more infantry

After observing Confederate weakness near the Middle Bridge, he asks for additional V Corps troops to cross.

Headquarters refuses the request

The regulars return to guarding the batteries and McClellan sends no larger supporting movement from the center.

Rodman’s two brigades cross open ground

Fairchild and Harland advance toward the ridge carrying the Harpers Ferry Road.

Confederate guns command the ridge

The episode places roughly twelve cannon on high ground with a clear field of fire over Rodman’s approach.

Fairchild’s New Yorkers charge

The brigade advances about half a mile through shell and canister toward the Confederate gun line.

The brigade reforms in low ground

Federal officers use a sheltered depression to restore alignment before the final rush.

The Confederate guns withdraw

As Fairchild closes, the artillery limbers and moves while Kemper’s and Drayton’s infantry prepare to receive the attack.

The infantry lines meet at point-blank range

The Confederates open fire from behind fences and a stone wall when the New Yorkers appear about fifty yards away.

Fairchild breaks the position

After close-range volleys and a brief hand-to-hand fight, the Federal brigade drives the defenders from the ridge.

The Confederate right retreats toward town

Kemper’s and Drayton’s men fall back into Sharpsburg under Federal artillery fire.

Federal skirmishers reach the outskirts

The Ninth Corps advance approaches the town while the Confederate right is in immediate danger.

Lee rallies the broken line

He gathers artillery and helps officers restore the retreating infantry south of Sharpsburg.

A column appears from the south

Lee watches troops approaching the battlefield while the Ninth Corps threatens his route of retreat.

Lee identifies A. P. Hill

The Confederate flags confirm that Hill’s Light Division is arriving from Harpers Ferry; its counterattack is not covered in this episode.

The book recommendation begins

The hosts recommend James M. McPherson’s “Crossroads of Freedom: Antietam.”
