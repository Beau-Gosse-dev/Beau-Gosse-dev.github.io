THE BURNSIDE EXPEDITION (Part the Second)

United States Army and Navy forces destroyed the remaining Confederate gunboats near Elizabeth City, captured New Bern after breaking the Confederate line at the railroad brickyard, and forced Fort Macon to surrender. New Bern remained a United States base for the rest of the war, but Burnside left with 7,000 men in July before attacking the railroad junction at Goldsboro

What happened

New Bern was the next objective

The city was North Carolina’s second largest and the eastern end of a railroad running to the important junction at Goldsboro.

United States gunboats attacked Elizabeth City

On February 10, the ships charged at close range, overwhelmed the Cobbs Point battery, and destroyed the remaining Confederate mosquito fleet.

Gunboats moved up the Chowan River

Confederates attempted to ambush the vessels at Winton. United States troops returned on February 20, landed, ransacked the town, and burned it.

Branch prepared the New Bern defenses

About 4,500 inexperienced North Carolina troops occupied works between the Neuse River and the swamps near Bryce Creek.

Fort Thompson anchored the river end

The fort held thirteen guns, but ten faced the river because the defenders expected the main attack to come by water.

The defensive line stopped at the railroad

Branch shortened the planned works because he lacked enough troops. A gap of about 150 yards remained near the railroad brickyard.

Branch assigned the front-line units

The 37th, 7th, and 35th North Carolina held the river side. The 24th and 26th North Carolina covered the far side, while recently raised militia guarded the brickyard gap.

Burnside embarked for New Bern

The expedition left Roanoke Island on March 11 with about 11,000 soldiers. Commander Stephen C. Rowan led thirteen supporting warships.

The army landed without opposition

The March 13 landing near Slocum Creek succeeded because the defenders expected a river attack. Heavy rain turned the northward march into deep mud.

Most artillery and reserve ammunition fell behind

The infantry stopped about two miles from the defensive line with only a few light howitzers and the forty rounds carried by each soldier.

The United States attack began

Foster advanced between the railroad and river, Reno moved west of the railroad, and Parke followed in reserve.

Foster attacked before Reno was ready

Confederate infantry and the landward guns of Fort Thompson stopped Foster’s advance.

Naval shells struck among United States troops

Some shells from Rowan’s gunboats overshot the Confederate works and landed in the attacking lines. Rowan continued firing until asked to stop.

Reno found the brickyard gap

He personally led four companies of the 21st Massachusetts through the opening and across the railroad against the flank of the militia.

The Confederate center began to fail

The militia withdrew, exposing the 35th North Carolina. Branch committed the 33rd North Carolina reserve, but it could not close the gap.

Parke committed the reserve brigade

Fresh United States troops passed through the opening and hit the right flank of the 7th North Carolina.

Foster renewed his attack

Foster ordered a charge after Parke broke through. The Confederate line between the railroad and the river collapsed.

Branch ordered a retreat

The Confederates withdrew across the Trent River bridge. Two regiments on the right did not receive the order promptly, and many of their men were captured.

United States forces entered New Bern

The retreating Confederates burned the Trent River bridge, but Rowan’s gunboats passed the river obstructions and reached the city.

Both armies reported heavy losses

The episode gives 470 United States casualties and 578 Confederate casualties, including missing men whom Branch believed were captured or had gone home.

Burnside turned toward Fort Macon

The fort controlled Beaufort Inlet and protected Beaufort, Morehead City, and the railroad terminus.

Parke began the Fort Macon operation

His brigade moved by steamer and railroad, repaired a bridge at Havelock, and reached Carolina City on March 22.

Siege batteries opened fire

On April 25, United States land batteries and gunboats bombarded the fort. Accurate land fire disabled Confederate guns and damaged the masonry.

Fort Macon surrendered

Most Confederate guns had fallen silent by 4 p.m. A white flag went up at about 4:30, and the formal capitulation followed.

Washington redirected Burnside to Virginia

Before the planned advance to Goldsboro began, Lincoln ordered Burnside to reinforce McClellan after the Seven Days Battles.

Burnside left North Carolina

On July 6, Burnside and about 7,000 troops sailed for Virginia. Roughly 8,000 remained to garrison the captured coastal positions.
