What happened

Hood inherited a restricted position

The Army of Tennessee was close to Atlanta with little room to maneuver. The Chattahoochee no longer blocked the U.S. advance.

Atlanta was losing its railroad connections

The Western and Atlantic was under U.S. control, the Montgomery and West Point had been damaged, and McPherson cut the Georgia Railroad near Decatur.

The Army of Tennessee had fewer than 60,000 men

Casualties, sickness, and desertion had reduced the army. Johnston’s removal also damaged morale among soldiers who supported him.

The army staff was in conflict

Chief of Staff William Mackall opposed Johnston’s removal. He remained for one week before Francis Shoup replaced him.

Hardee wanted a transfer

William Hardee resented serving under the younger Hood. Jefferson Davis denied the request because Hardee was the army’s only experienced corps commander.

Other corps commanders lacked experience

Alexander Stewart had led a corps for less than three weeks. Benjamin Cheatham temporarily took Hood’s former corps until Stephen D. Lee could arrive.

Hood had limited staff support

Hood’s wounds restricted his movement, the headquarters staff was weak, and Shoup and artillery chief Robert Beckham were new to their jobs.

The command transfer continued during active operations

Johnston directed movements in Hood’s name for much of July 18 while Hood learned the army’s positions. Davis refused a request to delay the transfer.

Hood needed an offensive result

Holding the city required more than pushing Sherman behind the Chattahoochee. Hood needed to damage part of the larger U.S. force before it could be supported.

Scouts reported a gap in Sherman’s armies

McPherson and Schofield moved toward Decatur while Thomas approached from the north. The separation prevented rapid mutual support.

Hood planned to attack Thomas at Peachtree Creek

The plan was to hit Thomas after he crossed the creek, push his army west, and trap it against the Chattahoochee River.

Sherman expected an attack elsewhere

Sherman expected Hood to attack McPherson or Schofield and ordered IV Corps toward them, weakening Thomas near Hood’s intended target.
