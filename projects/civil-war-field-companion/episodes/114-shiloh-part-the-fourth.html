Episode 114 · April 6, 1862, about 5–9 a.m. · Fraley Field, the Corinth Road, Peabody’s ridge, Spain Field, and Prentiss’s camps at Shiloh

SHILOH (Part the Fourth)

Peabody’s unauthorized patrol delayed the Confederate advance long enough to alert the forward camps. Hardee’s first line began moving at about 6:30 a.m. Wood and Shaver overran Peabody’s brigade, while Gladden and Chalmers broke Miller’s brigade at Spain Field. Prentiss’s division was routed before 9 a.m., but the resistance delayed and disorganized the attackers. Johnston incorrectly concluded that he had already turned Grant’s left and shifted reserves westward

What happened

The battle continued in Fraley Field

Powell’s 400 men and Hardcastle’s 280 Mississippians exchanged volleys in the darkness and first light.

Johnston heard the opening fire

At about 5:30, he ended Beauregard’s renewed argument for withdrawal and ordered the attack to begin.

Johnston divided command

He went forward to direct the firing line and left Beauregard in the rear to move reinforcements toward the fighting.

Hardee’s advance was delayed another hour

The start time had not been specified clearly, and orders moved slowly through an inexperienced command structure.

Powell began withdrawing

After seeing Confederate cavalry moving toward his flank, Powell pulled back from Fraley Field.

Peabody sounded the long roll

He alerted his brigade and sent five companies of the 21st Missouri under David Moore to reinforce Powell.

Prentiss blamed Peabody for starting the engagement

Prentiss had not authorized the patrol and initially treated the fighting as an unwanted action rather than warning of a general attack.

Moore advanced toward the Confederates

He combined his companies with Powell’s patrol, crossed toward the Corinth Road, and met the advancing Confederate line.

Moore was wounded

After about half an hour of volleys, Moore was shot in the leg. Powell resumed command and withdrew the original patrol.

Hardee’s line moved at about 6:30

Extended regimental lines broke alignment in woods, thickets, ravines, and swampy ground.

Woodyard’s force fell back

After about an hour against Wood’s and Shaver’s brigades, the outnumbered United States troops withdrew toward camp.

Peabody formed a final defensive line

He placed the remaining 25th Missouri and 12th Michigan with Woodyard’s men on a low ridge about half a mile from camp.

The Confederate attack reached Peabody

At about 7:30, the United States line stopped the first assault but was soon threatened on both open flanks.

Everett Peabody was killed

Already wounded four times, he was shot in the head while trying to hold the brigade together.

Miller formed at Spain Field

Three regiments and the 1st Minnesota and 5th Ohio batteries occupied the north side of the clearing.

Gladden attacked Spain Field

Four Alabama regiments and the 1st Louisiana advanced from the south. United States artillery mortally wounded Gladden early in the attack.

Chalmers reinforced the attack

His five regiments joined Gladden’s brigade, placing ten Confederate regiments against Miller’s three armed regiments.

Miller’s line collapsed

The survivors fled through their camps. Prentiss’s forward division was routed before 9 a.m.

Confederates stopped to reorganize and loot

Casualties, broken formations, terrain, and the captured camps temporarily halted the four attacking brigades.

Johnston misread the result

He believed the United States left had been turned and sent Breckinridge west. Other United States troops still stood between him and Pittsburg Landing.

The episode distinguishes two kinds of surprise

The patrol prevented a tactical surprise inside the tents, but Grant and Sherman were operationally surprised by the Confederate army’s advance from Corinth.
