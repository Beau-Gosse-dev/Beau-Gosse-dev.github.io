EMANCIPATION PROCLAMATION (Part the Fourth)

Abraham Lincoln kept the promise made in the preliminary proclamation and issued the final Emancipation Proclamation on January 1, 1863. Acting as commander in chief during an armed rebellion, he declared enslaved people in designated Confederate territory free, committed the executive government, Army, and Navy to recognize and maintain that freedom, and opened United States military service to Black men. The order did not abolish slavery throughout the country: it omitted the loyal slave states, Tennessee, and specified portions of Louisiana and Virginia. Its reach expanded as United States forces advanced, while nationwide abolition ultimately required the Thirteenth Amendment

People and command

United States civilian authority

Chief executivePresident Abraham LincolnIssued the proclamation, determined its geographic reach, approved its final text, and directed the executive government to recognize and maintain freedom
Department of StateSecretary of State William H. SewardOversaw preparation of the corrected engrossed copy, attested the final proclamation, and caused the United States seal to be affixed
War DepartmentSecretary of War Edwin M. StantonAdministered Army implementation under the proclamation and the expanding recruitment of Black soldiers
Navy DepartmentSecretary of the Navy Gideon WellesAdministered naval recognition of freedom and the reception of Black men into United States naval service

United States military chain of command

Commander in Chief of the Army and NavyPresident Abraham LincolnClaimed wartime authority for the proclamation and bound military and naval authorities to recognize and maintain the freedom it declared
General in Chief of the ArmyMaj. Gen. Henry W. HalleckSenior professional Army officer beneath the president and War Department during the final proclamation period
Army of the Potomac through November 7, 1862Maj. Gen. George B. McClellanRelieved after failing to pursue Lee aggressively following Antietam; his removal occurred during the hundred-day interval
Army of the Potomac from November 7, 1862Maj. Gen. Ambrose E. BurnsideSucceeded McClellan and commanded the army in its December defeat at Fredericksburg

Confederate national and eastern-theater authority

Confederate chief executive and commander in chiefPresident Jefferson DavisLed the government whose continuing rebellion triggered the January 1 designation in the proclamation
Army of Northern VirginiaGen. Robert E. LeeDefeated Burnside at Fredericksburg, the military reverse that renewed Northern doubts shortly before the proclamation deadline
