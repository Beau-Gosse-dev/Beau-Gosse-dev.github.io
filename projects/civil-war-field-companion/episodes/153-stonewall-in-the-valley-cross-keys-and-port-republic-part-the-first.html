Episode 153 · June 7–8, 1862 · Port Republic and Cross Keys in Rockingham County, Virginia, at the confluence of the North and South rivers near the southern end of Massanutten Mountain; the episode follows Samuel S. Carroll’s advance along the South Fork and his June 8 raid into Port Republic

STONEWALL IN THE VALLEY: CROSS KEYS & PORT REPUBLIC (Part the First)

Confederate tactical recovery at Port Republic on the morning of June 8. Carroll’s small mounted advance surprised the village, reached the North River bridge, and threatened Jackson’s headquarters and trains. Confederate infantry and artillery drove it back before its infantry support could secure the crossing. Jackson retained the bridge and kept Frémont and Shields separated. The Battle of Cross Keys later that day and the Battle of Port Republic on June 9 are announced but not narrated in this episode

People and command

United States

Department of the RappahannockMaj. Gen. Irvin McDowellHigher departmental commander; authorized Shields to continue the pursuit on the east side of Massanutten while Frémont pressed from the west
First Division, Department of the RappahannockBrig. Gen. James ShieldsDivision stretched along the muddy Page Valley road; sent Carroll’s Fourth Brigade ahead toward Port Republic and the South Fork crossings
Fourth Brigade and June 8 flying columnCol. Samuel S. CarrollLed about 150 cavalry and four guns at the head of the advance; infantry elements of the 7th Indiana, 1st Virginia (Union), 84th Pennsylvania, and 110th Pennsylvania followed
Ohio artillery battery attached to CarrollCapt. Lucius N. RobinsonFour guns accompanied the flying column; two remained on Yost Hill and two entered the village with the cavalry
Federal gun at the North River bridgeSgt. James GildayCovered the crossing, nearly fired on Jackson, and discharged double canister before abandoning the piece
Staff officer accompanying the mounted advanceCapt. Miles KeoghReconnoitered the village approach with Carroll; later served with the 7th Cavalry and was killed at the Little Bighorn in 1876

Confederacy

Valley District and combined Valley ArmyMaj. Gen. Thomas J. “Stonewall” JacksonKept headquarters at Madison Hall in Port Republic; about 7,000 men camped north of the North River within supporting distance
Division at Cross KeysMaj. Gen. Richard S. EwellHeld the western approach about four miles north of Port Republic with about 5,000 in the episode’s accounting; faced Frémont later on June 8
Brigade north of the North RiverBrig. Gen. William B. TaliaferroHad his men assemble for inspection; the 37th Virginia and 10th Virginia became the first organized infantry response to the raid
37th Virginia InfantryCol. Samuel V. FulkersonLed the regiment toward the bridge as Jackson ordered it to fire and charge
Small detachment of the 2nd Virginia InfantryCapt. Samuel J. C. MooreRoughly two dozen men guarded an upper South River ford and used a stone wall to block the cavalry’s drive toward the trains
Charlottesville ArtilleryCapt. James McD. CarringtonRallied the new battery after five guns initially joined the wagon panic; its fire helped clear Main Street and the eastern approach
Rockbridge ArtilleryCapt. William T. PoagueCamped on the heights near the bridge and received Jackson’s urgent order to hitch the guns
Chief medical officerDr. Hunter McGuireTried to keep ambulance drivers at the Methodist church with the sick and wounded while the raid entered town
