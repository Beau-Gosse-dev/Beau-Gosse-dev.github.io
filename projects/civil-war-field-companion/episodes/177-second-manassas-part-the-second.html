Episode 177 · August 29, 1862 · The Second Manassas battlefield around Groveton, Stony Ridge, the unfinished Manassas Gap Railroad, and the Warrenton Turnpike in Prince William County, Virginia

SECOND MANASSAS (Part the Second)

Tactical stalemate on August 29. Repeated United States assaults temporarily penetrated Jackson’s line but did not hold the unfinished railroad. Longstreet’s wing joined Jackson and deployed across the exposed Federal left-front. The battle remained undecided until the Confederate counterattack on August 30
Episode scopeThe second day of Second Manassas, Friday, August 29, 1862Episode 176 covered the August 28 fight at Brawner Farm. This episode follows the attacks on Jackson’s railroad position, Longstreet’s arrival, and the evening action on the Warrenton Turnpike.
Battle namesSecond Manassas and Second Bull RunThe National Park Service also lists Manassas, Manassas Plains, Groveton, Gainesville, and Brawner’s Farm as associated names for the August 28–30 battle.
Pope’s objectiveConcentrate against Jackson before he could withdraw or receive supportPope interpreted Jackson’s movements as an attempted escape and directed converging movements toward Groveton and Gainesville.
Jackson’s positionStony Ridge and the unfinished Manassas Gap RailroadA. P. Hill held the Confederate left, Alexander Lawton commanded the center in place of the wounded Richard Ewell, and William E. Starke commanded the right in place of the wounded William B. Taliaferro.
Railroad terrainCuts, fills, embankments, and level stretches formed an irregular defensive lineThe railroad grade offered cover in many places, but deep cuts, steep fills, and unfinished gaps prevented Jackson’s infantry from using it as one continuous breastwork.
Opening Federal forceAbout 9,000 men under Franz Sigel in the episode’s accountSigel advanced west from Henry Hill across a front about two miles wide during the morning.
Morning attacksCarl Schurz and Robert H. Milroy struck Jackson’s left and centerSchurz engaged Maxcy Gregg’s South Carolinians near the rocky ridge. Milroy attacked across the Groveton–Sudley Road toward a gap in the unfinished railroad. Both movements were repulsed.
Longstreet arrivesLee and Longstreet reached Jackson at about 10 a.m.John Bell Hood’s division led Longstreet’s column into position astride the Warrenton Turnpike. By shortly after noon, most of Longstreet’s wing had deployed on Jackson’s right.
Federal fire grazes LeeA Federal bullet grazed Lee’s cheek during reconnaissanceThe episode places the incident while Lee examined the Federal line after the two Confederate wings reunited.
Confederate concentrationMore than 50,000 men available after the two wings reunitedThe episode’s figure describes Lee’s combined battlefield force, not the number simultaneously engaged on August 29.
Pope reaches the fieldAbout 1 p.m., still unaware that Longstreet was substantially deployedThe Confederate line then extended nearly three miles: Jackson faced southeast north of the turnpike, while Longstreet faced generally east from Jackson’s right.
Federal leftOpen ground east of Longstreet’s concealed positionWoodland screened Longstreet’s deployment. Porter and McDowell encountered this force south of the turnpike while trying to act on Pope’s orders.
Grover’s attackAbout 1,500 men charged at roughly 3 p.m.Cuvier Grover shifted through woods and struck an incompletely defended gap between Gregg’s and Edward L. Thomas’s brigades. Thomas’s Georgians gave way, but Dorsey Pender’s brigade helped close the breach.
Grover’s lossesMore than one-third of the brigade in the episode’s accountThe National Park Service summary gives about 25 percent. Strength and casualty tabulations vary by source and by the units or time span included.
Nagle’s penetrationThree IX Corps regiments crossed the railroad against Lawton’s divisionBradley T. Johnson’s Virginia brigade hit James Nagle’s exposed left. Nelson Taylor arrived too late to support the penetration, and a Confederate pursuit briefly threatened a Federal battery.
Federal battery fightTwo guns were lost before Hooker restored the lineThe episode describes a Confederate pursuit after Nagle and Taylor withdrew, followed by a Federal rally under Joseph Hooker.
A. P. Hill’s ammunitionAt least one brigade had exhausted its cartridges late in the dayHenry Kyd Douglas recalled details collecting ammunition from dead and wounded soldiers while Hill warned Jackson that another attack could be difficult to stop.
Largest assault of the dayKearny sent ten regiments forward at about 5 p.m.Kearny’s division broke through parts of Gregg’s and Thomas’s line. Two regiments from Isaac Stevens’s division also penetrated the Confederate defense.
Jackson’s last reserveJubal Early’s brigade restored the threatened sectorThe Federal penetrations were not reinforced in time, and Jackson’s counterattacks recovered the railroad position before dark.
Porter and McDowellTheir southward movement encountered Longstreet instead of Jackson’s open flankPope’s orders were contradictory and based on an inaccurate picture of the field. Porter did not make the attack against Jackson that Pope expected.
Hatch at duskMoved west on the Warrenton Turnpike and collided with Longstreet’s troopsMcDowell ordered John P. Hatch’s division toward Groveton. The resulting action ended in darkness without a decisive result.
Pope recognizes LongstreetAbout 7 p.m.Even after accepting that Longstreet was present, Pope concluded that Lee was reinforcing Jackson or preparing to withdraw rather than preparing a major offensive.
August 29 resultJackson held; Longstreet deployed; neither side launched a decisive general attackFederal assaults made several local breaches but failed to coordinate or exploit them. The full battle’s Confederate victory belongs to the August 28–30 result, not to August 29 alone.

What happened

The August 28 fight is summarized

The hosts identify Brawner Farm as the opening action and begin the account of Friday, August 29.

Pope renews the attempt to trap Jackson

After Jackson leaves Manassas Junction, the Brawner Farm fight reveals his position near the old Bull Run battlefield.

Federal columns concentrate around Groveton

King, Ricketts, Porter, Reynolds, Sigel, IX Corps, and Heintzelman’s III Corps receive movements intended to close on Jackson from several directions.

Jackson’s line is placed on Stony Ridge

A. P. Hill holds the left, Lawton the center, and Starke the right along the unfinished railroad north of the Warrenton Turnpike.

The railroad is described as an irregular defense

Cuts, fills, steep banks, and level gaps explain why Jackson’s infantry did not follow the railroad grade continuously.

Sigel begins the morning pressure

About 9,000 men advance west from Henry Hill; Schurz engages Gregg while Milroy attacks toward a gap in the railroad.

Lee and Longstreet reach the battlefield

Hood’s division begins deploying astride the Warrenton Turnpike on Jackson’s right at about 10 a.m.

A bullet grazes Lee

Lee is struck on the cheek while reconnoitering, then continues organizing the reunited Confederate army.

Thomas’s brigade describes a Federal penetration

Marion H. Fitzpatrick of the 45th Georgia recalls the regiment nearly being surrounded and Branch’s brigade helping drive the attackers back.

Nagle’s attack is described from the railroad ditch

Henry H. Pearson of the 6th New Hampshire recounts finding Confederate troops close to the opposite side of the grade and withdrawing under fire.

Pope arrives without an accurate picture of Longstreet

By about 1 p.m., Pope remains focused on Jackson even though Longstreet is substantially deployed to the south.

The Confederate line reaches nearly three miles

Jackson faces southeast north of the turnpike, and Longstreet faces east from Jackson’s right.

Fresh Federal formations replace Sigel’s troops

Elements of Heintzelman’s III Corps and Reno’s IX Corps prepare new attacks on the unfinished railroad.

Grover’s brigade attacks

At about 3 p.m., five regiments move through woods and charge a vulnerable gap between Gregg and Thomas.

Thomas gives way at the railroad

Grover’s men pass the grade, but Gregg shifts troops and Pender arrives before the Federal breach can be reinforced.

Nagle attacks Lawton’s division

Three IX Corps regiments penetrate the railroad position southwest of Grover’s attack.

Bradley Johnson hits Nagle’s flank

The Virginia brigade forces Nagle back; Taylor arrives too late and is also driven from the immediate front.

Hooker restores the Federal line

A Federal battery nearly falls during the Confederate pursuit, and two guns are lost before Hooker stops the advance.

A. P. Hill reports an ammunition crisis

Henry Kyd Douglas recalls a brigade without cartridges and Hill warning Jackson that another attack could be difficult to defeat.

Jackson’s line repulses another attack

Douglas’s account ends with Hill reporting that the new Federal assault has been driven back.

Kearny’s division begins the largest assault

Ten regiments attack the battered Confederate left at about 5 p.m., while two regiments from Stevens’s division join the penetration.

Early’s brigade restores Jackson’s position

Jackson commits his remaining reserve, and the unsupported Federal breakthrough is forced back before night.

Porter’s expected attack does not occur

Porter receives conflicting orders and encounters Longstreet’s large force on Jackson’s right.

Hatch moves west on the turnpike

McDowell directs Hatch toward Groveton, where the division collides with Longstreet’s troops in a confused dusk action.

Pope accepts that Longstreet is present

At about 7 p.m., Pope finally revises his estimate but still interprets Lee’s movements as reinforcement or withdrawal rather than preparation for an attack.

The book recommendation is given

The hosts recommend John J. Hennessy’s “Return to Bull Run: The Campaign and Battle of Second Manassas.”

Important leaders killed or wounded

United States

Col. James Cantwell

Commander, 82nd Ohio Infantry, Milroy’s brigade. Killed while leading his regiment in the morning attacks against Jackson’s railroad position.

United States

Capt. David Gibson

Commander, Company H, 3rd West Virginia Infantry, Milroy’s brigade. Killed by a bullet to the forehead while leading a charge toward the railroad embankment. Theodore F. Lang’s postwar account supplies the episode’s opening description.

Confederacy

Sgt. Henry Ruffner Paine

First Rockbridge Artillery. Mortally wounded by solid shot during a close artillery exchange and died about two hours later. Edward A. Moore’s memoir supplies the episode’s opening account; the transcript renders the surname as “Payne.”
