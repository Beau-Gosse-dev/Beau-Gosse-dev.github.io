Episode 154 · June 8–9, 1862 · Cross Keys and Port Republic, Rockingham County, Virginia, at the southern end of Massanutten Mountain and the junction of the North and South rivers

STONEWALL IN THE VALLEY: CROSS KEYS & PORT REPUBLIC (Part the Second)

Confederate victories at Cross Keys and Port Republic. Ewell stopped Frémont on June 8, then most of his division reinforced Jackson against Tyler and Carroll on June 9. Control and destruction of the river bridges kept the two United States columns from combining. McDowell subsequently recalled Shields toward Fredericksburg, Frémont also withdrew, and the 1862 Valley Campaign ended with Jackson free to reinforce Lee near Richmond
Episode scopeThe battles of Cross Keys and Port Republic, June 8–9, 1862The episode begins with a recap of Carroll’s June 8 action at Port Republic, then covers Cross Keys and the following day’s battle at Port Republic.
Operational problemKeep Frémont and Shields separated, then defeat their forces in turnJackson controlled the bridges around Port Republic. Massanutten Mountain and the rivers limited the routes by which the two United States columns could support one another.
Cross Keys resultConfederate victory on June 8Ewell held the approaches to Port Republic and preserved Jackson’s control of the crossings.
Cross Keys strengthNPS estimate: 11,500 United States; 5,800 ConfederateThe episode gives Frémont’s own figure of 10,500, Milroy’s estimate of about 12,000, and fewer than 5,000 under Ewell. Returns, availability, and rounding produce different totals.
Cross Keys casualtiesNPS battle detail: 684 United States; 288 ConfederateThe NPS campaign overview gives 664 and 287 instead. The episode rounds the losses to nearly 700 and slightly fewer than 300, so the exact aggregate remains source-dependent.
Ewell’s lineSteuart on the left, Elzey and artillery in the center, Trimble on the rightTrimble received permission to advance his brigade beyond the main line to the Pence farm, where his men used a rise and split-rail fence.
Frémont’s deploymentA broad Federal line formed without firm central direction at the frontAfter a limited reconnaissance, Frémont left detailed placement largely to staff officers identified in the episode as Colonel Albert and Lieutenant Colonel Pilson. The episode describes Frémont as remaining in the rear and receiving incomplete information.
Frémont’s mistaken estimateHe believed he faced Jackson’s entire army and was outnumberedIn fact his force outnumbered Ewell’s division by about two to one. This incorrect estimate shaped his cautious and fragmented attacks.
8th New YorkAbout 548 men attacked Trimble without effective supportThe 45th New York stopped short, two other Stahel regiments lost direction, and one remained with a battery.
Close-range volley43 killed, 134 wounded, and about 43 captured in the 8th New YorkNPS gives a total loss of about 40 percent after Trimble’s brigade waited behind the fence until the attackers were roughly 40–50 yards away.
Trimble’s counterattackPressed Stahel and Bohlen back on the Federal leftTrimble’s advance included the 15th Alabama, 16th Mississippi, and 21st Georgia, supported in part by regiments from Elzey’s brigade. The exact position and timing of each regiment varied during the movement.
Milroy’s sectorA developing move against the Confederate left was recalledMilroy was advancing through difficult ground and had sent the 25th Ohio toward the Confederate flank when Frémont ordered the army back.
Unused strengthSchenck’s brigade remained largely uncommittedMilroy later argued that placing Schenck on his right could have changed the action. That is a participant’s counterfactual judgment, not a demonstrable outcome.
Cross Keys firing endsAbout 6:15 p.m. on June 8Frémont withdrew to his earlier position under artillery protection rather than renew a coordinated assault.
Rejected night attackEwell denied Trimble permission to attack Frémont’s campsEwell prioritized Jackson’s plan to concentrate against the force east of Port Republic on the morning of June 9.
Confederate night movementMost of Ewell’s division marched from Cross Keys to Port RepublicTrimble and Patton remained temporarily as a rear guard with orders to retire and burn the North River bridge if pressed.
Port Republic resultConfederate victory by about 11 a.m. on June 9The fall of the Coaling forced Tyler’s line to withdraw. Confederate pursuit continued for roughly eight miles in the episode’s account.
Port Republic strengthNPS estimate: 3,500 United States; 6,000 ConfederateThe episode describes about 4,000 men from Tyler’s and Carroll’s brigades on the field. Shields and the other two brigades of his division were still many miles away.
Port Republic casualties1,002 United States; 816 ConfederateThese are the NPS campaign figures and match the episode’s 816 Confederates and just over 1,000 Federals. The United States total includes a substantial number captured or missing.
Federal positionRight on the South Fork; left anchored on the CoalingTyler’s line followed the Lewiston Lane area across open bottomland. Artillery on the approximately 70-foot rise could fire across much of the field.
Crossing bottleneckPort Republic streets and the makeshift South River crossing delayed supportThe episode describes a crossing built from wagons and sawmill boards. Congestion caused Jackson to commit units before his force was fully assembled.
Initial Confederate attackWinder’s attack against the Federal right was repulsedThe 5th and 27th Virginia and 7th Louisiana attacked before adequate reinforcements arrived. The Federal counterattack briefly endangered the Confederate line and forced the abandonment of artillery pieces.
The CoalingThe decisive point at Port RepublicThe first attack by parts of the Stonewall Brigade failed. Taylor’s Louisiana Brigade then fought repeated close-range actions for the Federal guns until Confederate reinforcements helped secure the height.
Frémont blockedArrived opposite Port Republic after the North River bridge was burnedHis batteries fired across the river but could not provide useful infantry support to Tyler and Carroll. The episode repeats William C. Oates’s later allegation that the fire endangered Confederate ambulances and parties assisting wounded men.
Command casualtiesNo senior leader was killed or mortally wounded in these two battlesBrigade commander Arnold Elzey was seriously wounded at Cross Keys, and 7th Louisiana commander Harry T. Hays was wounded in the shoulder at Port Republic. Neither wound was fatal, so the leader-loss list is empty.
End of the campaignMcDowell ordered Shields back toward Luray and FredericksburgA similar withdrawal direction went to Frémont. Jackson later crossed the Blue Ridge and joined Lee for the defense of Richmond.

What happened

Carroll’s June 8 action is reviewed

The episode resumes with Carroll’s failed attempt to hold the North River bridge at Port Republic and the arrival of Tyler’s brigade later that day.

Tyler decides to remain

Eight regiments and three batteries from Tyler’s and Carroll’s brigades take position northeast of Port Republic rather than withdraw.

The account turns to Cross Keys

While Jackson dealt with events at Port Republic, Frémont advanced from Harrisonburg against Ewell northwest of the town.

Strength estimates are compared

Frémont reported 10,500 men, Milroy estimated about 12,000, and the episode places fewer than 5,000 Confederates with Ewell.

Frémont’s column leaves Harrisonburg

The lead brigade marched at about 4:45 a.m., followed by the remaining infantry brigades and artillery.

Pickets make first contact

Cluseret’s skirmishers encounter two companies of the 15th Alabama north of Union Church Cemetery between 8 and 8:30 a.m.

Ewell forms his line

Steuart anchors the left, Elzey and four batteries occupy the center, and Trimble holds the right.

Trimble advances to the Pence farm

Concerned about the woods and his refused flank, Trimble moves forward of Ewell’s main line and uses a rise and rail fence.

The Confederate defense is ready

By about 10 a.m. Ewell’s brigades occupy the ground blocking Frémont’s route toward Port Republic.

Frémont describes the position

He reports a strong wooded ridge commanding the road junction and continues to believe the Confederate force is larger than his own.

Ewell’s artillery opens

Confederate batteries in the center begin firing as the Federal brigades deploy; Frémont’s artillery answers.

Frémont attacks Ewell’s right

Stahel’s brigade receives the first major assault assignment, but its regiments do not advance together.

The 8th New York advances alone

About 548 men continue toward Trimble while the 45th New York stops short and the remainder of the brigade fails to support the attack.

Trimble’s brigade fires at close range

The Confederates wait behind the fence until the New Yorkers are roughly 40–50 yards away, then deliver a concentrated volley.

The 8th New York is broken

The episode gives 43 killed, 134 wounded, and about 40 captured before the survivors retreat.

Trimble begins a counterattack

After recalling the immediate pursuit, Trimble moves against a Federal battery and Bohlen’s brigade with support from Elzey’s regiments.

Frémont orders the entire line back

Uncertain about events on his left, he directs a general withdrawal while separate Federal movements are still developing.

Milroy’s flank move is recalled

A courier reaches Milroy just as the 25th Ohio is moving toward the Confederate left; Milroy complies despite objecting to the order.

Schenck’s brigade remains idle

Milroy sees five regiments uncommitted and later argues that their use on his right could have strengthened his attack.

Cross Keys firing ends

The episode places the end of the action at about 6:15 p.m.; both armies remain near the field after the Federal withdrawal.

Trimble proposes a night attack

He wants to strike Frémont’s campfires but does not receive Ewell’s permission.

Jackson sets the June 9 plan

Ewell learns that the army will concentrate at Port Republic, attack the eastern Federal force first, and turn back toward Frémont only if time permits.

Ewell marches toward Port Republic

Most of his division leaves Cross Keys before dawn while Trimble and Patton temporarily watch Frémont as rear guard.

Winder starts across the rivers

At about 5 a.m. the Stonewall Brigade crosses through Port Republic and uses the makeshift South River span to approach Tyler’s line.

Tyler’s position is defined

Open fields provide clear fire, the South Fork protects the Federal right, and the Coaling anchors the left.

Fog covers the deployment

Winder places the 5th and 27th Virginia against the Federal right before the fog clears around 5:45 a.m.

The artillery duel begins

Poague’s guns exchange fire with the stronger Federal artillery position for at least an hour in some accounts.

The crossing becomes a bottleneck

Troops and wagons clog Port Republic and the makeshift bridge, delaying the reinforcements Winder needs.

Winder attacks before support is massed

The 5th and 27th Virginia and 7th Louisiana advance across open ground toward the Federal right along Lewiston Lane.

The first Confederate attack fails

Federal infantry stops the advance and counterattacks, driving back Winder’s line and forcing Confederate gunners to abandon pieces.

Virginia reinforcements stabilize the line

The 44th and 58th Virginia arrive, stop the Federal advance, and allow the Confederates to restore a front across the bottomland.

Jackson shifts attention to the Coaling

The failed attack on Tyler’s right demonstrates that the Federal artillery on the left must be neutralized.

The first Coaling assault is repulsed

The 2nd and 4th Virginia and a two-gun section cannot overcome musketry and canister from the height.

Taylor’s Louisiana Brigade is committed

The 8th and 9th Louisiana and Wheat’s Battalion move through dense woods and underbrush toward the Federal guns.

The Louisianans reach the battery

Taylor’s men emerge near the Coaling, cross a ravine, and overrun part of the gun line before Federal infantry counterattacks.

The first capture is reversed

After about fifteen minutes of close fighting, Ohio troops drive Taylor’s brigade back; only one recaptured gun can be removed.

Frémont advances too late at Cross Keys

At about 10 a.m. his lead troops find Ewell gone. Ewell’s brigades are already passing through Port Republic toward the June 9 battlefield.

Ewell strikes the shifting Federal left

Arriving Confederates fire into the 5th and 7th Ohio while those regiments are moving from Lewiston Lane toward the Coaling.

Taylor’s final attack takes the Coaling

After repeated repulses, Confederate pressure and reinforcements force the Federal defenders from the height.

Winder renews the attack

Seeing Federal units leave the Lewiston Lane line, Winder presses the 27th Virginia forward as the left flank gives way.

Jackson secures the field

By about 11 a.m. Tyler’s line has collapsed and the Confederates begin a pursuit that the episode places at roughly eight miles.

The burned bridge blocks Frémont

His troops reach the opposite side of the South Fork after the rear guard destroys the North River bridge, preventing direct support for Tyler.

The cost of Port Republic is assessed

The episode gives 816 Confederate casualties and just over 1,000 United States casualties after a four-hour battle.

Shields encounters the retreat

Still short of the battlefield, he rallies fugitives from Tyler’s and Carroll’s commands and prepares to continue the movement.

McDowell ends the pursuit

Orders direct Shields back to Luray for a return toward Fredericksburg; a similar direction goes to Frémont.

The book recommendation

The hosts recommend Donald C. Pfanz’s biography “Richard S. Ewell: A Soldier’s Life.”
