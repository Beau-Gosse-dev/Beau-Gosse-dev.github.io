Episode 154 · June 8–9, 1862 · Cross Keys and Port Republic, Rockingham County, Virginia, at the southern end of Massanutten Mountain and the junction of the North and South rivers

STONEWALL IN THE VALLEY: CROSS KEYS & PORT REPUBLIC (Part the Second)

Confederate victories at Cross Keys and Port Republic. Ewell stopped Frémont on June 8, then most of his division reinforced Jackson against Tyler and Carroll on June 9. Control and destruction of the river bridges kept the two United States columns from combining. McDowell subsequently recalled Shields toward Fredericksburg, Frémont also withdrew, and the 1862 Valley Campaign ended with Jackson free to reinforce Lee near Richmond

Division in Frémont’s armyBrig. Gen. Louis BlenkerIncluded Stahel, Bohlen, and Koltes; the principal June 8 attack began with Stahel’s brigade on the Federal left

Independent brigade in Frémont’s armyBrig. Gen. Robert H. MilroyAdvanced against the Confederate left and began a flank movement with the 25th Ohio before obeying Frémont’s withdrawal order
Brigade in Frémont’s armyBrig. Gen. Robert C. SchenckRemained largely uncommitted at Cross Keys; Milroy later criticized the failure to use the brigade
Division, Department of the RappahannockBrig. Gen. James ShieldsNot present at Port Republic; Tyler and Carroll were the two advance brigades while Shields remained with the rest of the division farther north

Confederacy

Valley District and combined Valley ArmyMaj. Gen. Thomas J. “Stonewall” JacksonControlled the Port Republic crossings, assigned Ewell to hold Frémont on June 8, and personally directed the June 9 attack against Tyler
Division at Cross KeysMaj. Gen. Richard S. EwellDeployed about 5,800 by the NPS estimate, repelled Frémont, left a rear guard, and led most of the division to reinforce Jackson at Port Republic
Left brigade, Ewell’s divisionBrig. Gen. George H. SteuartHeld the western portion of Ewell’s Cross Keys line against the sector approached by Milroy
Center brigade, Ewell’s divisionBrig. Gen. Arnold ElzeySupported the central artillery; regiments under Col. James A. Walker reinforced Trimble’s counterattack. Elzey was seriously wounded but survived
Right brigade, Ewell’s divisionBrig. Gen. Isaac R. TrimbleDestroyed the isolated 8th New York attack, counterattacked the Federal left, and served as part of the rear guard on June 9
Stonewall BrigadeBrig. Gen. Charles S. WinderLed the first main attack at Port Republic with elements of the 5th, 27th, 2nd, and 4th Virginia and later renewed pressure along Lewiston Lane
Louisiana Brigade, Ewell’s divisionBrig. Gen. Richard TaylorLed the 8th and 9th Louisiana and Wheat’s Battalion through the woods in repeated attacks that ultimately took the Coaling
Louisiana Tigers battalionMaj. Chatham Roberdeau WheatFought under Taylor in the close action around the Coaling and helped disable artillery horses so the guns could not be removed
Rockbridge ArtilleryCapt. William T. PoagueOpened the June 9 artillery action in support of Winder; Federal counterbattery fire held the advantage
