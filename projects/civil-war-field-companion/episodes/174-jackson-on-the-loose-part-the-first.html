Episode 174 · August 24–26, 1862 · Northern Virginia, from the upper Rappahannock River and Warrenton through Salem and Thoroughfare Gap to Gainesville, Bristoe Station, and the approaches to Manassas Junction

JACKSON ON THE LOOSE (Part the First)

Confederate operational success in progress. Jackson marched around Pope’s right, passed unopposed through Thoroughfare Gap, and cut the Orange and Alexandria Railroad at Bristoe Station. By the end of the episode, Trimble and Stuart were moving toward the lightly defended supply depot at Manassas Junction; its capture occurred after the episode’s stopping point

People and command

United States

General-in-Chief, U.S. ArmyMaj. Gen. Henry W. HalleckSupervised the transfer of Army of the Potomac units toward Pope but lacked effective coordination with the field headquarters and railroad system
Army of VirginiaMaj. Gen. John PopeHeld the upper Rappahannock from the Warrenton area to Waterloo Bridge and interpreted Jackson’s movement as a march toward the Shenandoah Valley
I Corps, Army of VirginiaMaj. Gen. Franz SigelDirected from the river line toward Fayetteville on August 25 in the episode’s account
II Corps, Army of VirginiaMaj. Gen. Nathaniel P. BanksMoved south past Bealeton toward Kelly’s Ford rather than toward Jackson at Salem
III Corps, Army of VirginiaMaj. Gen. Irvin McDowellRemained at Warrenton and was ordered to probe across White Sulphur Springs on August 26
III and V Corps, Army of the PotomacMaj. Gens. Samuel P. Heintzelman and Fitz John PorterTheir arriving corps were within supporting distance but had not yet given Pope the full reinforcement strength he expected
U.S. Military Railroads in VirginiaCol. Herman HauptManaged trains moving reinforcements and supplies through Alexandria amid competing demands and disrupted schedules

Confederacy

Army of Northern VirginiaGen. Robert E. LeeOrdered Jackson around Pope’s right while Longstreet threatened the Rappahannock front, then directed Longstreet to follow Jackson
Left Wing, Army of Northern VirginiaMaj. Gen. Thomas J. “Stonewall” JacksonDivisions of Richard S. Ewell, A. P. Hill, and William B. Taliaferro; directed the flank march and the strike against the railroad
Right Wing, Army of Northern VirginiaMaj. Gen. James LongstreetInitially held opposite Pope, then recommended and received orders to follow Jackson’s route toward Salem
Ewell’s divisionMaj. Gen. Richard S. EwellLed Jackson’s marching column; Henry Forno’s Louisiana brigade helped disrupt the railroad at Bristoe
Light DivisionMaj. Gen. A. P. HillMarched behind Ewell and ahead of Taliaferro in Jackson’s column
Taliaferro’s divisionBrig. Gen. William B. TaliaferroBrought up the rear of Jackson’s three-division column
Cavalry DivisionMaj. Gen. J. E. B. StuartRejoined Jackson on August 26 and was sent with cavalry to command the Manassas Junction expedition
2nd Virginia CavalryCol. Thomas T. MunfordSeized Bristoe Station and worked with Forno’s infantry to block the Orange and Alexandria Railroad
Detachment from Trimble’s brigadeBrig. Gen. Isaac R. TrimbleLed the 21st Georgia and 21st North Carolina toward Manassas Junction before Stuart was sent to take overall command
