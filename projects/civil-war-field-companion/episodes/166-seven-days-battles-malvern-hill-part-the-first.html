Episode 166 · July 1, 1862 · Malvern Hill, an open plateau north of the James River in Henrico County, Virginia, about fifteen miles southeast of Richmond

SEVEN DAYS' BATTLES: MALVERN HILL (Part the First)

This episode establishes the terrain, opposing deployments, and Confederate plan before the main artillery and infantry fighting. The Army of the Potomac occupied a compact position supported by massed artillery, while Robert E. Lee planned to suppress the Federal guns with artillery on two ridges and then attack. Misidentified roads, dispersed batteries, ammunition shortages, and an ambiguous attack order prevented the intended Confederate concentration. The full battle, covered in the following episode, ended in a United States victory
Episode scopeThe battlefield and command arrangements before the main fighting at Malvern HillThe narrative stops after explaining the failed Confederate artillery concentration. The gun duel, infantry assaults, and casualty totals belong to the following episode.
Campaign settingFinal battle of the Seven Days, one day after GlendaleBy July 1, the Army of the Potomac had concentrated on Malvern Hill during its movement toward the James River.
Battlefield formAn elevated, open plateau with long fields of fireThe episode describes the plateau as about one and one-quarter miles north to south, three-quarters of a mile wide, and approximately 130 feet above the James River. Published measurements vary according to the boundaries used.
Natural boundariesTurkey Run and Western Run framed the plateauMalvern Cliffs and Turkey Run protected the western side. Wooded, swampy Western Run bordered the east and crossed the northern approach below the open slope.
Main approachWillis Church Road crossed the plateau from north to southThe road divided the principal Federal position and became the central reference line for both armies.
Federal formationA loose inverted horseshoe facing north and eastPorter’s V Corps held the western and northern sectors; Couch’s division extended east of Willis Church Road. Sumner and Heintzelman stood behind the eastern side, and Franklin guarded approaches nearer the James.
V Corps frontageSykes west of Willis Church Road; Morell across the northern faceMorell’s division curved around the Crew house. Griffin held the forward field, with Martindale and Butterfield behind and to the east.
Couch’s deploymentThree brigades in depth near the West houseThe episode identifies the brigades of Innis N. Palmer, Albion P. Howe, and John J. Abercrombie. Palmer’s understrength brigade formed the forward line.
Federal artilleryArmy artillery and siege guns were available around the plateauThe episode gives 268 field pieces and 26 siege guns in the vicinity. It places 31 guns in seven batteries on the northern front, about 60 guns in ten batteries to the east, and roughly 50 more guns in reserve. These figures describe availability and deployment areas, not simultaneous fire from one line.
Artillery directionCol. Henry J. Hunt organized and sustained the Federal gun lineAs Army of the Potomac chief of artillery, Hunt selected positions and arranged for batteries to rotate through the firing line.
McClellan’s movementsInspected Harrison’s Landing before returning to Malvern Hill in mid-afternoonMcClellan traveled from Haxall’s Landing aboard USS Galena and later remained on the eastern side of the Federal position. Porter directed the sector facing the principal Confederate approach.
Lee’s conditionFatigued and ill after the preceding campaign daysThe episode reports that Lee asked Longstreet to remain with him in case Longstreet needed to assume command. The extent to which fatigue affected later decisions remains an interpretation rather than a measurable fact.
D. H. Hill’s warningAdvised against attacking if McClellan held Malvern Hill in strengthHill had questioned a local chaplain about the ground and relayed the warning to Lee near Willis Methodist Church.
Confederate assumptionLee and Longstreet believed the retreating Federal army was demoralizedD. H. Hill later identified this belief as a reason Lee risked an attack against a strong position.
Intended Confederate alignmentJackson east of Willis Church Road; Magruder west of itLee selected commands that had done little fighting at Glendale: Jackson, Huger, and Magruder.
Road-name errorLee called Willis Church Road the Quaker RoadConfederate maps applied the Quaker Road name to Willis Church Road, while local guides knew a different Quaker Road about two miles west. The mismatch sent Magruder away from his intended route until an aide redirected him.
Unmapped eastern roadA parallel road east of Willis Church Road did not appear on Lee’s mapsThe episode notes that Federal wagons had used it and that it might have offered Jackson a better flanking approach. That possible use is counterfactual and cannot be verified as an available attack route under battlefield conditions.
Longstreet’s proposalMass artillery on both sides of Willis Church Road to create converging fireLongstreet estimated that about 60 guns could fit on the plateau behind Armistead and Wright. Lee selected the Poindexter farm ridge for the corresponding position on the eastern side.
Armistead’s roleHis advance was named as the signal for a general infantry attackThe order said that if artillery broke the Federal line, Armistead was to charge with a yell and other commands were to do the same. Armistead did not receive the order because it went only to division commanders.
Chilton orderUndated and dependent on an audible yell across the battlefieldLee’s aide Robert H. Chilton drafted the order before 1 p.m. Its wording did not specify how commanders were to verify that the bombardment had broken the Federal line.
Confederate artillery problemThe two planned concentrations were never assembledAvailable batteries were dispersed among divisions, routes and orders were unclear, and several batteries never reached the intended ridges.
D. H. Hill’s batteriesSeven batteries lacked ammunition after White Oak SwampThe episode states that Hill’s artillery had exhausted its ammunition during the June 30 bombardment and was unavailable for the planned Malvern Hill concentration.
Artillery reserveSix batteries under Brig. Gen. William N. Pendleton remained idleThe episode finds no record of Lee summoning the reserve. Pendleton searched for Lee but did not establish contact in time to employ the batteries.
Battle result outside episode scopeUnited States victoryThe National Park Service estimates 8,355 casualties for the complete battle: about 3,000 United States and 5,355 Confederate. These losses occurred during fighting narrated in the following episode, not during this episode’s setup.
Senior leader losses in this episodeNoneNo senior commander is killed or mortally wounded during the preparatory events covered here.
Recommended book“Voices of the Civil War: The Seven Days” by the editors of Time-Life BooksThe episode recommends this collection of participant accounts, and the official podcast listing confirms the title.

What happened

Glendale is reviewed

The episode begins by assessing why Lee’s June 30 plan failed to trap the retreating Federal army.

Command failures at Glendale are assigned

Huger and Jackson are criticized, but the episode places primary army-level responsibility on Lee.

Lee’s June 30 inspection of Malvern Hill is recalled

Lee had examined Holmes’s sector and diverted Magruder toward the River Road before reversing part of that movement.

The Army of the Potomac concentrates

On July 1, the Federal army is assembled at Malvern Hill with the James River behind it.

The plateau is described

The episode gives the dimensions and elevation of the open ground above the James.

The watercourses frame the position

Turkey Run, Western Run, and the Malvern Cliffs define the principal approaches and flanks.

Porter evaluates the defensive ground

Porter considers Malvern Hill better suited to defense than earlier V Corps positions.

The Federal line is outlined

The army forms a loose inverted horseshoe facing the expected Confederate approach.

Sykes and Morell take the principal front

Sykes covers the western cliffs and River Road while Morell spans the northern face toward Willis Church Road.

Couch extends the line east

Couch places Palmer, Howe, and Abercrombie in depth near the West house.

The supporting corps are located

Sumner and Heintzelman stand behind the eastern side, with Franklin nearer the James.

Federal artillery becomes the central defensive advantage

The episode inventories the field batteries, siege guns, and reserve pieces available around Malvern Hill.

Hunt organizes the gun line

Henry J. Hunt places batteries and prepares to rotate them to sustain the fire.

McClellan leaves for the James

After visiting the lines, McClellan goes to Haxall’s Landing and inspects Harrison’s Landing aboard USS Galena.

McClellan returns in mid-afternoon

He comes back to Malvern Hill and remains on the army’s eastern flank while Porter directs the northern sector.

The position’s narrow front is assessed

The episode notes that only a limited number of defenders could occupy the 1,200-yard northern front at once, but nearby reserves could respond to a breakthrough.

Lee’s condition is considered

Lee is described as fatigued and ill after days of limited sleep and operational disappointment.

D. H. Hill warns against an attack

Near Willis Methodist Church, Hill reports local information about the strength of the ground and advises leaving McClellan alone if he holds it in force.

Longstreet judges the Federal army demoralized

Longstreet interprets the continuing retreat as evidence that the Army of the Potomac can be defeated by another attack.

Jackson crosses White Oak Swamp

Jackson moves south through Glendale and meets Magruder before both commanders report to Lee.

Lee assigns the approach columns

Jackson is to move east of Willis Church Road and Magruder is to follow and deploy west of it.

The road-name error redirects Magruder

Lee’s use of “Quaker Road” for Willis Church Road leads local guides toward the actual Quaker Road to the west.

Lee and Longstreet inspect opposite sides

Longstreet scouts west of Willis Church Road while Lee examines the eastern approaches.

Longstreet identifies an artillery ridge

He finds Armistead and Wright on high ground north of the Federal line and proposes massing about 60 guns there.

Magruder’s mistaken movement is discovered

Longstreet sees Magruder’s troops moving away from the intended battlefield route and reports the problem to Lee.

Lee approves the crossfire concept

Lee accepts Longstreet’s proposal, sends an aide to redirect Magruder, and expects batteries to assemble on the western ridge.

Lee selects the Poindexter ridge

The eastern ridge near the Poindexter farm is chosen for the second planned artillery concentration.

Chilton drafts the attack order

The order makes a break in the Federal line and Armistead’s charge with a yell the conditions for other commands to advance.

Armistead does not receive the order

Copies go to division commanders, leaving the brigadier named as the signal unaware of that role.

The artillery concentration fails

The episode identifies inadequate numbers, dispersed batteries, ammunition shortages, and poor communication.

D. H. Hill’s batteries are unavailable

Seven batteries have exhausted their ammunition at White Oak Swamp, while Jackson’s remaining batteries are scattered among divisions.

Pendleton’s reserve remains idle

The six-battery artillery reserve receives no effective direction and does not join the planned concentration.

The book recommendation begins

The hosts recommend “Voices of the Civil War: The Seven Days” by the editors of Time-Life Books.
