Episode 196 · September 17, 1862 · The center of the Antietam battlefield east of Sharpsburg, Maryland, from the Roulette farm south across the ridge to the Sunken Road between the Hagerstown Turnpike and the Roulette farm lane

ANTIETAM (Part the Eleventh)

Beginning at about 9:30 a.m., Brig. Gen. William H. French sent three brigade lines against Maj. Gen. D. H. Hill’s infantry in the Sunken Road. The road’s banks and piled fence rails protected the Confederates, who waited until the Federal lines crossed the ridge before firing at close range. Weber’s, Morris’s, and Kimball’s brigades were each stopped, and French’s division could not break the position before Israel Richardson’s division arrived. French reported 1,817 casualties; the episode uses 1,760. The first phase therefore ended with Hill’s line intact but under continuing pressure and with reinforcements approaching both sides

What happened

The northern attacks are over

Sedgwick has been routed and Greene has lost his foothold near the Dunker Church, shifting the fighting toward Lee’s center.

The Sunken Road becomes the new focus

D. H. Hill’s infantry holds a farm lane in the center of the Confederate line.

French reaches the East Woods without orders

Sumner has left no staff officer to direct French, and Sedgwick is already out of sight in the West Woods.

French surveys the visible Federal line

Greene’s troops near the Dunker Church are the only Federal formation he can see.

French moves south

He intends to form on Greene’s left and advances toward the Roulette farm instead of following Sedgwick.

The Sunken Road is located

The episode traces the defended lane east from the Hagerstown Turnpike and then southeast toward the Boonsboro Turnpike.

A farm lane provides natural cover

Erosion and wagon traffic have worn parts of the road several feet below the adjoining fields.

Lee and Hill see French approaching

The two generals ride along the center and recognize that the Federal column is moving toward Hill’s line.

John B. Gordon answers Lee

The 6th Alabama colonel assures Lee that his regiment will remain in position.

The senior Confederates move to higher ground

Lee and Hill join Longstreet on a ridge near the Hagerstown Turnpike to observe the Federal advance.

A Federal shell kills Hill’s horse

The shell severs the animal’s front legs while Hill remains mounted; Hill escapes injury.

French reaches the Roulette farm

His three brigades drive Confederate skirmishers away from the farm buildings.

French receives an urgent attack order

News of Sedgwick’s disaster finally reaches him, and he is told to attack quickly to reduce the pressure in the West Woods.

The division advances in three lines

French moves south over rising ground toward Confederates hidden beyond the crest.

Hill’s strength is reduced

Three of his five brigades have already fought on the northern field, leaving perhaps 2,500 men in the episode’s estimate.

Richard H. Anderson approaches

Lee sends another Confederate division toward the center while French closes on the road.

George B. Anderson holds the deepest road cut

His four North Carolina regiments occupy the eastern portion of the defended sector.

Rodes holds the bend

Five Alabama regiments extend west from the junction with the Roulette farm lane.

Battered brigades extend Hill’s left

The remnants under Alfred Colquitt and Duncan McRae connect Rodes with the Hagerstown Turnpike.

Gordon explains his fire plan

He orders the 6th Alabama to remain down and hold its fire until the Federals are at close range.

Tanner’s account records the 1st Delaware’s losses

He recalls 286 of 635 men and eight of ten company commanders killed or wounded in less than five minutes.

French’s division is described

The recently assembled command contains veteran units, new regiments, and three brigades that will attack separately.

Weber begins the first attack

The 1st Delaware, 5th Maryland, and 4th New York form the first Federal line.

Weber’s line bows inward

The 5th Maryland falls behind the two flank regiments during the advance.

The defenders continue to wait

Confederate officers prevent their men from firing as the Federal line crosses the ridge.

The Sunken Road erupts at close range

At about eighty yards, the Confederate line opens a coordinated volley on Weber’s exposed brigade.

Weber’s brigade is stopped

The episode estimates as many as 500 Federal casualties in five minutes, including the severely wounded Weber.

The 1st Delaware color guard falls

All nine members are down and the regiment’s colors remain near the Confederate position.

Charles Tanner saves the colors

Tanner retrieves the 1st Delaware flag, receives three wounds, and later earns the Medal of Honor.

Morris sends in the second brigade

About 1,000 men in three new regiments follow Weber toward the ridge.

Friendly fire adds to the confusion

Members of the 14th Connecticut fire into the rear of the 1st Delaware.

Morris can advance no farther

His brigade and Weber’s survivors withdraw behind the ridge and return fire from cover.

Samuel Fisk describes a first battle

The 14th Connecticut captain records disordered commands, retreating troops, and inexperienced soldiers firing under stress.

Confederates counterattack from the road

Small impromptu advances expose some defenders but help push Morris’s line back behind the crest.

Kimball receives the third attack

French commits his last brigade after the first two have been repulsed.

Kimball’s mixed experience is identified

Three regiments are veterans, while the 132nd Pennsylvania has only recently entered service.

A shell breaks beehives in the orchard

The 132nd Pennsylvania scatters briefly among the bees at the Roulette farm before rallying.

Kimball passes the first two brigades

His line shifts east and advances beyond Weber’s and Morris’s men on the reverse slope.

Oakford is killed

The 132nd Pennsylvania’s colonel falls in the first Confederate volley that strikes Kimball’s line.

Kimball’s veterans call it systematic killing

The third brigade cannot pass the same protected position that stopped Weber and Morris.

French’s offensive power is exhausted

Kimball falls back to regroup, leaving all three Federal brigades short of the Sunken Road.

The first phase ends

The episode gives French 1,760 casualties and notes that fresh formations are approaching both sides.

The book recommendation begins

The hosts recommend Richard Slotkin’s “The Long Road to Antietam.”

The campaign is connected to emancipation

The recommendation emphasizes how the military result at Antietam enabled Lincoln to issue the preliminary Emancipation Proclamation.

Important leaders killed or wounded

United States

Brig. Gen. Max Weber

Commander of 1st Brigade, French’s division. Severely wounded while leading the first assault. Col. John W. Andrews of the 1st Delaware assumed brigade command and ordered the shattered line to fall back.

United States

Col. Richard A. Oakford

Commander of the 132nd Pennsylvania. Killed while leading his new regiment in Kimball’s attack. His surname is incorrectly rendered as “Oakley” in the automated transcript.

Confederacy

Brig. Gen. George B. Anderson

Commander of Anderson’s North Carolina brigade. Wounded in the ankle during the Sunken Road fighting. The wound became infected, and he died in Raleigh, North Carolina, on October 16, 1862.

United States

Lt. Charles B. Tanner

1st Delaware Infantry. Wounded three times while recovering the regimental colors after the color guard fell. He survived and later received the Medal of Honor.
