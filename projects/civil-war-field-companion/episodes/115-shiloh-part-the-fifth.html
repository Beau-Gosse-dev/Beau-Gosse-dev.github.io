SHILOH (Part the Fifth)

Prentiss re-formed part of his routed division, and the divisions of W. H. L. Wallace and Stephen Hurlbut extended the new United States center. Johnston diverted Chalmers and Jackson toward Stuart’s isolated brigade after mistaking it for a full division. On the United States right, Sherman recovered from the initial surprise and held near Shiloh Church for more than two hours. What happened

The episode resumes after Prentiss’s defeat

The Confederate attack had driven Prentiss’s division from its camps, but it had not cut the United States army off from Pittsburg Landing.

Confederate troops stopped in the captured camps

Some formations became disorganized while soldiers searched the tents and collected food and equipment.

Johnston received an incorrect report about Stuart

A cavalry patrol saw Stuart’s detached brigade and reported a United States division moving south near the river.

Johnston diverted two brigades

He sent James Chalmers and John K. Jackson toward Stuart, leaving only one brigade to cover a large part of the Confederate center.

Two reserve brigades were redirected

Johnston changed earlier orders and sent two of Breckinridge’s brigades back toward the right to reinforce the weakened center.

Prentiss rebuilt part of his division

Survivors rallied along a farm lane about 1,200 yards north of their captured camps instead of counterattacking.

The 23rd Missouri joined Prentiss

The newly arrived regiment contributed about 1,000 fresh soldiers, probably more men than Prentiss had rallied from his original division.

Wallace and Hurlbut formed a new center

Their divisions took positions on either side of Prentiss between about 9 and 9:30 a.m.

MacArthur extended Hurlbut’s left

W. H. L. Wallace sent John McArthur’s brigade across the line, but a 200-yard wooded gap still separated it from Stuart.

A lull developed in the center

After Chalmers and Jackson moved toward Stuart, only Adams’s brigade faced the Wallace–Prentiss–Hurlbut line for most of the next hour.

Johnston believed the army was trapped

He thought the Confederate right had turned Grant’s left and that the rest of the United States army could be driven into the Owl and Snake Creek bottomlands.

Appler heard the opening fighting

The 53rd Ohio formed after its pickets reported heavy firing, but Sherman again dismissed the warning.

Cleburne’s brigade entered Ray Field

Its regiments crossed the southern end of the field while moving toward the United States camps.

Sherman confirmed the attack

Confederate skirmishers fired at him from about 75 yards. Buckshot struck his right hand and killed his orderly, Thomas D. Holliday.

The 53rd Ohio stopped the first assault

Appler’s regiment fired a close-range volley into two of Cleburne’s regiments and drove them back.

Terrain divided Cleburne’s brigade

Mud and broken ground split the six-regiment line. Two regiments moved against Appler while four attacked Hildebrand and Buckland.

Sherman stabilized his division

Although wounded and losing two horses, he directed Buckland and Hildebrand under sustained fire.

Appler ordered a retreat

Several hundred men of the 53rd Ohio ran toward Pittsburg Landing. Other companies remained until their exposed flank forced them back.

Raith reinforced Sherman’s left

Julius Raith brought four regiments from McClernand’s division and restored part of the line after the 53rd Ohio withdrew.

Seven Confederate brigades faced Sherman and Raith

By about 9:30, formations from Hardee, Bragg, and Polk had converged around Shiloh Church without a single coordinating commander.

Hildebrand’s brigade began to collapse

By about 10 a.m., the concentrated Confederate pressure had broken much of Sherman’s left after more than two hours of fighting.
