Episode 199 · September 17–22, 1862 · The southern and northern ends of the Antietam battlefield around Sharpsburg, Maryland; field hospitals across the Antietam Valley; and the roads through Middletown to Frederick, Maryland, and across the Potomac at Boteler’s Ford near Shepherdstown, Virginia

ANTIETAM (Part the Fourteenth)

A. P. Hill’s Light Division struck the exposed left flank of the Ninth Corps late on September 17. The 8th and 16th Connecticut, 4th Rhode Island, and supporting Ohio regiments were driven back, Isaac Rodman was mortally wounded, and Lawrence O’Bryan Branch was killed. Jacob Cox withdrew the Ninth Corps to defensible ground above the Lower Bridge, which remained in Federal hands. On the Confederate left, concentrated Federal artillery stopped Jackson and Stuart from turning the Federal right. Fighting ended near sunset. Neither army renewed the battle on September 18; Lee withdrew across the Potomac that night. Nearly 23,000 soldiers were casualties, and the Union strategic victory gave Lincoln the opportunity to announce the Preliminary Emancipation Proclamation on September 22
Episode scopeThe final fighting on September 17 and the immediate aftermath through September 20The episode begins with A. P. Hill’s counterattack, follows the close of combat and medical response, and ends with the Confederate withdrawal, limited Federal pursuit, and the battle’s strategic consequences.

Rodman mortally woundedShot through the chest while reacting to Hill’s arrivalBrig. Gen. Isaac P. Rodman was carried from the field and died on September 30, 1862.
8th Connecticut advanceSeveral hundred yards ahead of the rest of Harland’s brigadeThe regiment advanced with Fairchild’s brigade, captured a Confederate battery near the Harpers Ferry Road, and then lost about half its men when attacked in flank.
Otto’s 40-acre cornfieldHead-high corn and broken ground concealed the attackersThe 16th Connecticut and 4th Rhode Island could not see Gregg’s South Carolinians approaching from the south and west. Smoke, crops, and some Confederate clothing taken at Harpers Ferry added to the identification problem.

What happened

The Ninth Corps crisis is recapped

The corps has reached the outskirts of Sharpsburg, but its exposed left threatens the entire advance.

A. P. Hill arrives from Harpers Ferry

Hill’s Light Division reaches the battlefield at the point where it can strike the open Federal flank.

The forced march reduces Hill’s strength

The episode estimates that about 2,000 of the roughly 3,300 men who left Harpers Ferry are available at the first crisis.

Hill angles toward the Federal left

The position and surprise of the Confederate arrival multiply the effect of the veteran troops.

Harland’s regiments hold the flank

The 8th and 16th Connecticut and 4th Rhode Island form the vulnerable edge of Rodman’s division.

The 16th Connecticut’s inexperience is explained

The regiment had been in Federal service for less than a month and had almost no opportunity for field training.

Rodman sees the threat

He identifies Hill’s column moving toward his division from the south and rides forward to warn the line.

Rodman is mortally wounded

A Confederate bullet strikes the Ninth Corps division commander in the chest.

Harland loses his horse

The brigade commander’s horse is shot while he tries to reach the two trailing regiments.

The 8th Connecticut advances alone

A delay separates the regiment by several hundred yards from the 16th Connecticut and 4th Rhode Island.

The 8th Connecticut captures a battery

The regiment reaches Confederate guns near the Harpers Ferry Road before Hill’s counterattack strikes its flank.

Two regiments enter Otto’s cornfield

The 4th Rhode Island and 16th Connecticut move into head-high corn on broken ground half a mile behind the 8th Connecticut.

Gregg approaches from the south and west

The South Carolina brigade uses the concealed approach to reach the inexperienced Federal regiment.

Crossfire surprises the Federals

Gregg’s troops fire from two directions into the 16th Connecticut and then the 4th Rhode Island.

The 16th Connecticut collapses

Inexperienced officers and soldiers cannot execute the needed formation changes under close fire.

Clothing adds to the confusion

Some Confederates wear pieces of captured Federal clothing, making identification harder in the corn and smoke.

The Federal flank routs

The 16th Connecticut breaks and carries the 4th Rhode Island back with it.

The forward Ninth Corps line is endangered

Hill’s attack threatens troops that had advanced close to Sharpsburg and were unaware of the collapse to their left.

Cox orders the forward troops back

The corps commander gives up ground near Sharpsburg to prevent the advance from being cut off.

Three of Hill’s brigades are engaged

Gregg, Archer, and Branch continue the attack into the Ninth Corps left and supports.

Scammon’s Ohioans contest the cornfield

The supporting Kanawha brigade changes front but is outflanked and forced to withdraw.

Lawrence O’Bryan Branch is killed

The North Carolina brigade commander is shot in the head while riding forward and dies immediately.

The six general-officer fatalities are listed

The episode identifies three Federal and three Confederate generals killed or mortally wounded at Antietam.

Confederate guns concentrate south of town

More than forty field pieces in the episode support Hill and the remnants of D. R. Jones’s command.

Federal batteries west of the creek withdraw

The concentrated Confederate artillery outguns the Federal pieces that had crossed near the Middle Bridge.

Cox assembles a defensive line

The Ninth Corps falls back to preserve its west-bank foothold and control of the Lower Bridge.

McClellan watches the southern crisis

From the Pry House, the army commander observes the reversal on Burnside’s front.

New cannon fire erupts on the Federal right

The noise marks the opening of a separate Confederate effort on the northern end of the field.

Jackson’s counterattack plan is explained

Lee had asked Jackson to turn the Federal right and relieve pressure elsewhere on the Confederate line.

Stuart receives a mixed force

One infantry regiment, several batteries, and cavalry are assigned to move between the Federal flank and the Potomac.

Meade’s artillery blocks the route

Federal guns near the Joseph Poffenberger farm command the ground Stuart must cross.

Confederate batteries are suppressed

The Federal gun line silences the opposing artillery in about fifteen minutes in the episode’s account.

Stuart cancels the movement

The proposed turn of the Federal right ends before the Confederate infantry can advance.

George Smalley observes headquarters

The correspondent records McClellan watching the Ninth Corps and considering the V Corps reserve.

Porter’s infantry remains in reserve

McClellan and Porter decide that the V Corps troops cannot be spared while both flanks appear threatened.

Pleasonton’s request is recalled

Headquarters had already refused to release Porter’s infantry for an advance from the Middle Bridge.

McClellan and Porter ride toward the left

They leave the Pry House to inspect the known crisis on the Ninth Corps front.

Burnside asks for reinforcements

A courier reports that the Ninth Corps needs infantry and artillery to hold west of the creek.

McClellan orders the bridge held

He sends Miller’s battery but tells the courier that no infantry is available.

The Ninth Corps keeps its foothold

Hill’s exhausted men do not drive the Federals back across Antietam Creek or recapture the Lower Bridge.

The northern front remains static

Stuart’s abandoned movement leaves both armies holding their existing positions.

The firing declines

Artillery and musket fire diminish as the sun sets through battlefield smoke.

Combat on September 17 ends

Darkness closes the deadliest single day in American military history.

The night of the wounded begins

Stretcher-bearers and medical personnel search the fields and carry casualties to improvised hospitals.

Casualty estimates are presented

The episode gives category totals and emphasizes that exact Confederate figures remain uncertain.

Letterman’s ambulance corps is assessed

The new organized system assigns ambulances, drivers, and trained attendants to remove wounded soldiers.

Dozens of field hospitals receive casualties

The episode counts at least 71 Federal hospitals in buildings around the battlefield.

Clara Barton brings supplies

Barton’s wagons deliver urgently needed medical material to the northern battlefield.

The Sanitary Commission sends relief

The civilian organization uses its own transportation to bring additional supplies the day after the battle.

Official medical stores remain delayed

The episode places the arrival of substantial Army Medical Department supplies on the evening of September 20.

Evacuation to Frederick begins

Walking wounded and ambulances travel about twenty miles through Middletown to the larger hospital center.

Most movable wounded leave the battlefield

By September 25, about 600 severely wounded soldiers remain in two tent hospitals near Sharpsburg.

Frederick expands hospital capacity

Doctors add six temporary hospitals in churches and other buildings to the existing military barracks.

Lee leaves wounded soldiers behind

The Confederate withdrawal cannot carry all casualties, and about 2,000 remain in Federal hands in the episode’s estimate.

Confederate battlefield strength is discussed

The episode compares Lee’s estimate of no more than 35,000 engaged with Ezra Carman’s estimate of just over 38,000 effectives.

Federal losses concentrate in four corps

The episode states that about 96 percent of Federal casualties occurred in I, II, XII, and IX Corps.

A third of the Federal army did not fire

Large parts of V and VI Corps remained uncommitted or saw only limited action.

Lee decides to remain

That evening he orders the army reorganized and provisioned without issuing a retreat order.

McClellan predicts renewed fighting

On the morning of September 18 he tells Halleck and his wife that the battle will probably resume.

Franklin proposes another assault

The VI Corps commander again recommends attacking the Confederate left, but McClellan declines.

Lee withdraws after dark

A truce permits collection of wounded and burial of dead; the Army of Northern Virginia then crosses the Potomac.

Federal pursuit reaches the river

V Corps troops cross on September 19, and a larger force is repulsed by Hill at Shepherdstown on September 20.

The battle’s result is assessed

The episode distinguishes the tactical standstill from the strategic failure of Lee’s Maryland invasion.

Antietam becomes a decisive event

Lee’s withdrawal ends the invasion even though the Army of Northern Virginia is not destroyed.

Lincoln acts after the battle

The Union strategic victory supplies the occasion for announcing the Preliminary Emancipation Proclamation on September 22.

The book recommendations begin

The hosts recommend two Antietam essay collections edited by Gary W. Gallagher.

A Canadian connection is added

The bonus segment introduces Calixa Lavallée, a Quebec-born musician who served in the 4th Rhode Island.

Lavallée is wounded at Antietam

The future composer of the music for “O Canada” is wounded when Hill’s counterattack hits the regiment.
