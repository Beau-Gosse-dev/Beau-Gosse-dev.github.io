What happened

The narrative returned to early 1864

Both sides were repairing railroad supply systems and preparing for the campaign that would begin in May.

Johnston took command at Dalton

Jefferson Davis appointed Joseph E. Johnston to rebuild the Army of Tennessee despite their mutual distrust.

Johnston rebuilt the army without an agreed strategy

He improved supply, condition, and morale while Davis declined to engage with his strategic proposals.

Grant prepared the western flank

Before advancing into Georgia, Grant wanted to prevent Confederate forces in Mississippi from threatening river traffic or the railroads from Nashville.

Grant approved the Meridian expedition

The operation was intended to damage Mississippi railroads enough to prevent a large Confederate concentration in the state.

Sherman commanded the raid

William T. Sherman led the operation east from Vicksburg with the Army of the Tennessee.

Sherman warned Mississippi residents

Before departure, he ordered a public warning that U.S. forces were about to enter the interior of the state.

The columns left Vicksburg

Sherman departed on February 3, three days before the podcast narrative places his forty-fourth birthday.

Sherman commanded Grant’s former army

He had led the Army of the Tennessee since October 1863, after its victories at Donelson, Shiloh, Corinth, Champion Hill, and Vicksburg.

Sherman had failed in Kentucky

In late 1861 he overestimated Confederate strength, requested unrealistic reinforcements, and was replaced by Don Carlos Buell.

Halleck relieved Sherman in Missouri

A second command crisis led Halleck to judge him temporarily unfit for duty.

Sherman returned home to recover

Ellen Sherman took him to Ohio after newspaper reports described him as mentally ill and his confidence collapsed.

Halleck returned Sherman to limited duty

At the start of 1862, Sherman resumed service by drilling recruits in St. Louis.

His prewar career had been unstable

Sherman resigned from the Army in 1853, then failed in banking and real-estate work before finding success at a Louisiana military academy.

Sherman returned to military service in 1861

He was running a St. Louis streetcar company when the war began and received a commission with help from his brother John Sherman.

Halleck assigned Sherman to Grant

In February 1862, Sherman received a division under the general who had just captured Fort Donelson.

Sherman’s battlefield record remained mixed

He fought well after the surprise at Shiloh but later failed at Chickasaw Bluffs and in his attack at Missionary Ridge.

Grant and Sherman developed mutual trust

During twenty-two months of service together, Grant provided steady direction and Sherman became a dependable subordinate.

Grant gave Sherman the Army of the Tennessee

When Grant became commander of the Military Division of the Mississippi, he selected Sherman to lead his former army.
