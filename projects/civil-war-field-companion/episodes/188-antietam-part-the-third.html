Episode 188 · September 16, 1862 · Sharpsburg and Keedysville, Maryland, with the Army of Northern Virginia on the ridge between Antietam Creek and the Potomac River and the Army of the Potomac concentrating east of the creek

ANTIETAM (Part the Third)

The armies concentrated for battle without a general engagement on September 16. George B. McClellan postponed his main attack until September 17 while Robert E. Lee strengthened a four-mile defensive line west of Antietam Creek. Thomas J. “Stonewall” Jackson and John G. Walker reached Lee during the day; late in the day, elements of the United States I and XII Corps crossed the creek and made contact with the Confederate left

People and command

United States

Army of the PotomacMaj. Gen. George B. McClellanReconnoitered Lee’s line on September 16, concentrated the army east of Antietam Creek, and ordered the general attack for the next day
I CorpsMaj. Gen. Joseph HookerCrossed the upper Antietam late on September 16 and moved toward the Miller farm opposite Lee’s left
XII CorpsMaj. Gen. Joseph K. F. MansfieldFollowed I Corps across the upper creek late in the day to support the opening attack against the Confederate left
II CorpsMaj. Gen. Edwin V. SumnerConcentrated east of the Antietam; the corps had camped on and around the Pry farm during the approach
V CorpsMaj. Gen. Fitz John PorterFormed part of McClellan’s concentrated force and remained available near the army center and reserve
IX CorpsBrig. Gen. Jacob D. CoxExercised corps command after Jesse Reno’s mortal wound at South Mountain; waited east and southeast of the creek for the next day’s orders
VI CorpsMaj. Gen. William B. FranklinRemained in Pleasant Valley to watch Harpers Ferry until McClellan ordered him toward Sharpsburg at 6 p.m.

Confederacy

Army of Northern VirginiaGen. Robert E. LeeChose to stand west of Antietam Creek, placed Jackson on the left and Longstreet on the right, and called the remaining Harpers Ferry detachments to Sharpsburg
Left WingMaj. Gen. Thomas J. “Stonewall” JacksonArrived from Harpers Ferry on September 16 and took position from the Dunker Church area toward Nicodemus Heights
Right WingMaj. Gen. James LongstreetHeld the roads, ridges, and approaches around Sharpsburg and extended the defense south toward the lower Antietam
Hill’s Division, Confederate centerMaj. Gen. D. H. HillOccupied ground connecting Jackson and Longstreet near the Hagerstown Turnpike, Roulette farm, and roads north of Sharpsburg
Cavalry Division and horse artilleryMaj. Gen. J. E. B. StuartHeld Nicodemus Heights with two cavalry brigades and horse artillery on the extreme Confederate left
Early’s Brigade, Ewell’s DivisionBrig. Gen. Jubal A. EarlySupported the left near Nicodemus Heights as Lee’s position filled out on September 16
Toombs’s Brigade, D. R. Jones’s DivisionBrig. Gen. Robert ToombsHeld the forward position above Rohrbach Bridge to delay a United States crossing of the lower Antietam
Cavalry on the lower AntietamCol. Thomas T. MunfordGuarded the creek below Sharpsburg toward its junction with the Potomac and the route to Boteler’s Ford
