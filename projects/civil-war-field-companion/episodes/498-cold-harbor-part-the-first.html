What happened

Grant continued southeast after each battle

The Overland campaign did not end at the Wilderness, Spotsylvania, or the North Anna. Grant repeatedly moved around Lee’s right flank.

The armies fought along Totopotomoy Creek

By May 30, Lee’s lines appeared too costly to attack directly, and Early’s attempt to strike Warren near Bethesda Church had failed.

Confederate cavalry probed near Old Cold Harbor

Matthew Butler’s mounted brigade moved toward Matadequin Creek and met Alfred Torbert’s U.S. cavalry protecting the route to White House Landing.

Torbert drove Butler back

Three U.S. brigades forced Butler’s single brigade away from the creek and pursued it toward Old Cold Harbor.

Grant chose another turning movement

He planned to move beyond Lee’s right with William F. Smith’s XVIII Corps leading from White House Landing.

Sheridan received orders to clear the route

Grant directed Sheridan to move cavalry from the U.S. left toward Cold Harbor and open the way for Smith.

XVIII Corps began landing

About 10,000 men, with more following, disembarked at White House Landing after leaving the Bermuda Hundred lines.

Lee requested reinforcements from Beauregard

Lee warned Jefferson Davis of possible disaster. Beauregard then sent Robert Hoke’s division from Bermuda Hundred.

Both commands focused on Cold Harbor

The five-road crossroads controlled routes toward the Chickahominy crossings and the next U.S. movement around Lee’s right.

Fitzhugh Lee organized the Confederate defense

Butler’s brigade and Fitzhugh Lee’s two cavalry brigades built breastworks across the eastern approaches while waiting for Hoke’s infantry.

Clingman’s brigade arrived

Four North Carolina regiments reinforced the Confederate left shortly before U.S. cavalry attacked about 4 p.m. on May 31.

Merritt and Custer attacked dismounted

Two of Torbert’s brigades advanced with Sharps carbines and Spencer repeaters. The first attack identified the Confederate line and the presence of infantry.

Merritt turned the Confederate flank

Part of his brigade held the front while other troopers moved around Clingman’s exposed and poorly placed North Carolinians.

The 51st North Carolina was isolated

Placed about 500 yards ahead of the main line, the regiment was outflanked. Clingman was with it and could not direct the rest of his brigade.

The Confederate position collapsed

Clingman’s brigade broke, Fitzhugh Lee withdrew, and Custer’s mounted charge accelerated the retreat.

U.S. cavalry held the crossroads

Merritt and Custer had driven away four Confederate brigades with two U.S. brigades; Thomas Devin’s brigade did not join the attack.

Sheridan briefly abandoned Cold Harbor

Reports of more Confederate infantry and the distance to U.S. infantry support led Sheridan to order Torbert out after dark.

Wright received orders to march

At 9:45 p.m. on May 31, VI Corps left the Totopotomoy line for Cold Harbor.

Lee prepared an ambush

Richard Anderson’s First Corps marched toward Cold Harbor to join Hoke, clear the crossroads, and strike Smith’s column while it was stretched along the roads.

Meade ordered Sheridan back

Sheridan received the order about 1 a.m. and reoccupied the crossroads two hours later before the Confederates realized it had been vacant.

Kershaw’s division reached the area first

Only Kershaw’s troops were ready to attack at dawn. Hoke was supposed to advance when he heard the firing.

About 10,000 Confederates faced 5,000 cavalrymen

Kershaw and Hoke had roughly twice Sheridan’s manpower, but the U.S. cavalry had earthworks and repeating carbines.

The Confederate attack failed quickly

Lawrence Keitt advanced without skirmishers. Merritt’s dismounted cavalry held fire until close range and then broke the attack with rapid carbine fire.

A second advance also failed

Kershaw’s men regrouped but were repulsed again. Wright’s infantry then arrived and secured Sheridan’s position.

The operation had begun as a reconnaissance

Anderson ordered a reconnaissance in force rather than the coordinated attack Lee expected. Kershaw assigned it to Keitt’s inexperienced brigade.

Keitt was mortally wounded

He rode conspicuously ahead of the formation and was hit by the first U.S. volley. His 20th South Carolina then collapsed under its first major combat.

Hoke did not advance

The signal and coordination arrangements failed. Hoke remained in place, Kershaw did not renew the attack, and Anderson’s later arrivals began entrenching.

Lee’s counterstroke ended

By late morning on June 1, the Confederate effort to recover Cold Harbor and attack the U.S. turning movement had failed.
