SEVEN DAYS' BATTLES: GAINES'S MILL (Part the First)

Although Porter’s V Corps had repulsed the Confederate attacks at Beaver Dam Creek, McClellan ordered it four miles east to a new defensive position behind Boatswain’s Swamp and decided to abandon the Richmond & York River Railroad supply line in favor of a base on the James. Porter prepared to hold north of the Chickahominy while the rest of the Army of the Potomac remained south of the river. Lee pursued a plan based on the mistaken expectation that the Federals would defend Powhite Creek and the route to White House Landing; on the morning of June 27, he had to revise that plan after encountering Porter’s unanticipated position. The episode ends before the main Battle of Gaines’s Mill begins

V Corps north of the ChickahominyBrig. Gen. Fitz John PorterGeorge W. Morell’s division faced west; George Sykes’s division faced north; George A. McCall’s Pennsylvania Reserves formed the reserve
VI Corps south of the ChickahominyBrig. Gen. William B. FranklinHad long-range artillery able to support Porter; Henry W. Slocum’s division was initially checked at the bridge while McClellan assessed threats south of the river
II, III, and IV Corps south of the ChickahominyMaj. Gens. Edwin V. Sumner, Samuel P. Heintzelman, and Erasmus D. KeyesRemained on the Richmond front while reports of Confederate activity reinforced McClellan’s fear of an attack there

Confederacy

Army of Northern VirginiaGen. Robert E. LeePursued Porter while still expecting the Federals to defend Powhite Creek and the route to White House Landing
Left wing moving toward Old Cold HarborMaj. Gen. Thomas J. “Stonewall” JacksonLee sent an aide to find him after first light; his movement with D. H. Hill was intended to pass beyond the expected Federal right
Division supporting Jackson’s movementMaj. Gen. D. H. HillMoved toward Old Cold Harbor as part of the force Lee expected to place beyond the Federal flank
Pursuit east from the Mechanicsville sectorMaj. Gens. A. P. Hill and James LongstreetExpected to push a Powhite Creek defense toward Jackson and D. H. Hill; instead their advance would encounter Porter at Boatswain’s Swamp
