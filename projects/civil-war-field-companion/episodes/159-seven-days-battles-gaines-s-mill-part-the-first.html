SEVEN DAYS' BATTLES: GAINES'S MILL (Part the First)

Although Porter’s V Corps had repulsed the Confederate attacks at Beaver Dam Creek, McClellan ordered it four miles east to a new defensive position behind Boatswain’s Swamp and decided to abandon the Richmond & York River Railroad supply line in favor of a base on the James. Porter prepared to hold north of the Chickahominy while the rest of the Army of the Potomac remained south of the river. Lee pursued a plan based on the mistaken expectation that the Federals would defend Powhite Creek and the route to White House Landing; on the morning of June 27, he had to revise that plan after encountering Porter’s unanticipated position. Episode scopeThe decisions, terrain, commands, and faulty assumptions that set up Gaines’s MillThe opening eyewitness readings preview the late-afternoon fighting, but the main narrative stops before the battle begins.
Campaign dateFriday, June 27, 1862Gaines’s Mill was the third of the Seven Days Battles and is also called First Cold Harbor. This installment covers the preceding night and the opening movements that morning.
McClellan’s 1 a.m. orderPorter was directed to leave Beaver Dam Creek and fall back about four milesThe episode identifies roughly 1 a.m. on June 27 as the moment McClellan gave up the immediate effort to capture Richmond.
Three strategic choicesProtect the York River supply line, attack Richmond south of the Chickahominy, or change base to the JamesPorter and other officers urged an attack toward Richmond while V Corps held Lee north of the river. McClellan chose the third course.
Porter’s understandingHold north of the river “to the last extremity”According to the episode’s account of Porter’s later recollection, he believed McClellan meant to attack Richmond while V Corps held. McClellan instead intended the stand to cover the army’s withdrawal.
Change of baseAbandon White House Landing and move the army toward Harrison’s LandingMovable supplies at White House Landing were to go by water to the James. The phrase “change of base” was McClellan’s preferred description; in operational effect the army was retreating from Richmond.
Conflicting assessmentsLee anticipated McClellan’s caution; McClellan underestimated Lee’s appetite for riskThe episode treats the failed Confederate attack at Mechanicsville as sufficient to make McClellan surrender the initiative. Claims that a Federal attack would have captured Richmond are counterfactual, not established fact.
Porter’s positionA defensive arc about 1½–1¾ miles long behind Boatswain’s SwampNational Park Service and episode measurements differ slightly in wording. Both describe an arc on high ground north of the Chickahominy with difficult approaches and useful fields of fire.
TerrainA brush-lined, sluggish stream below a steep rise to the Turkey Hill plateauThe plateau fell toward the Chickahominy on the south. The Watt, McGehee, and Adams houses stood on the high ground.
Why “Gaines’s Mill”The Gaines property gave the battle its name, although most fighting occurred southeast of the millThe episode places Dr. William Gaines’s farm near Powhite Creek and his mill farther north, more than a mile from the principal battlefield.
Confederate map problemLee’s map showed Powhite Creek but omitted Boatswain’s SwampThe episode presents this omission as a major reason Lee expected a Federal line in the wrong place. Exact cartographic content varies among surviving maps; the diagram shows the episode’s stated contrast, not Lee’s original map.
Federal deploymentGeorge Morell faced west; George Sykes faced north; George McCall remained in reserveThe two forward lines followed the creek and slope, with artillery on the plateau crest. McCall’s Pennsylvania Reserves had fought heavily on June 26.
Episode strength estimateA little more than 27,000 men in nine brigades initially available to PorterThe National Park Service gives 27,000 before later reinforcements raised the force to about 34,000. Strength depends on time and counting method; this episode ends before tracing those later arrivals.
Federal artillery17 batteries and 96 guns with V CorpsThe episode also gives William B. Franklin’s VI Corps three long-range batteries south of the Chickahominy that could fire across the river. Battery and gun totals can vary with time and attachment.
Slocum checkedHenry W. Slocum’s division was initially stopped before crossing to reinforce PorterThe episode says McClellan recalled the division when reports suggested a Confederate attack south of the river. Elements of Slocum’s division did reinforce Porter later on June 27, beyond this installment’s endpoint.
Magruder’s demonstrationTroop movements, shouted orders, drums, and pressure along the Richmond frontJohn B. Magruder sought to convince the Federal corps south of the Chickahominy that they faced a serious attack, helping keep them from being used more aggressively.
Lee’s expected Federal responseDefend Powhite Creek and the supply route toward White House LandingMcClellan had instead decided to abandon that route. Porter occupied Boatswain’s Swamp, a stronger position east of where Lee expected to meet him.

Command uncertaintyLee began June 27 still trying to locate Jackson and interpret the Federal withdrawalThe episode says Lee sent an aide to find Jackson after first light. Confederate pickets then reported that the Beaver Dam Creek line was empty.
Leader casualties in scopeNone in the narrated setup to the battlePorter’s Mexican War wound is biographical background. The opening readings describe later combat at Gaines’s Mill but do not establish a killed or wounded senior commander for this episode’s losses section.

What happened

A cavalryman recalls the battle’s final crisis

Private William H. Hitchcock of the 5th U.S. Cavalry describes Philip St. George Cooke ordering a charge to help Federal artillery withdraw. This reading previews fighting covered in later installments.

A Texan recalls Hood’s breakthrough

John W. Stevens of the 5th Texas describes the assault over the Federal works and against the reserve guns. The excerpt is a participant’s later account, not the start of the chronological narrative.

Episode 159 begins

The hosts resume the Seven Days after Oak Grove and Mechanicsville and shift to the setup for Gaines’s Mill.

Lee gains the initiative despite defeat

McClellan orders Porter out of the Beaver Dam Creek position that had repulsed the Confederate attacks.

The 1 a.m. withdrawal order

The episode places McClellan’s decisive order around 1 a.m. on June 27: Porter will fall back four miles to Boatswain’s Swamp.

Jackson is still on Porter’s flank

Reports correct McClellan’s earlier belief that Jackson had been defeated and warn that the Valley command threatens V Corps from the north.

McClellan’s alternatives are stated

He can defend the railroad supply line or hold Lee north of the river while attacking Richmond with the army’s main body to the south.

Porter urges an attack on Richmond

Porter recommends enough reinforcement to hold north of the river while the larger Federal force attacks south of it.

Porter is told to hold

McClellan stresses the necessity of holding until arrangements across the river are complete, without clearly telling Porter that the army will retreat.

McClellan chooses to save the army

The episode says he rejects the proposed attack and informs his staff, but not his field commanders, that the army is retreating.

V Corps must buy one more day

Porter is to resist on June 27 and then cross to the south side of the Chickahominy that night.

White House Landing is abandoned

McClellan directs movable supplies to be loaded on ships for Harrison’s Landing on the James River.

The commanders read one another differently

Lee correctly expects caution; McClellan’s earlier view that Lee would avoid a bold movement has already failed.

Boatswain’s Swamp offers a strong defense

The episode describes the swampy stream, steep rise, plateau, and Chickahominy behind Porter’s new line.

Fitz John Porter’s career

The narrative reviews his naval family, West Point class of 1845, Mexican War service, prewar staff work, Texas mission, and rise to V Corps command.

The Watt House becomes headquarters

Porter uses the house on Turkey Hill; the episode notes that the seventy-seven-year-old Sarah Watt left for safety.

Lee’s map omits Boatswain’s Swamp

The map available to Lee showed nearby Powhite Creek but not the wooded watercourse screening Porter’s actual line.

Porter forms the archer’s bow

Morell’s division faces west and Sykes’s division north, with two infantry lines and a third artillery line along the crest.

Ninety-six guns support V Corps

Porter has seventeen batteries; Franklin’s guns south of the river can also reach Confederate approaches from the west.

Slocum’s reinforcement is stopped

McClellan checks the division at the bridge because he fears a simultaneous Confederate attack south of the Chickahominy.

Magruder sustains the deception

Movement, shouted orders, and drums strengthen Federal reports of a threat on the Richmond front.

Lee begins correcting the previous day’s failures

After first light he sends an aide to find Jackson and waits for news of the Federal position.

Beaver Dam Creek is empty

Confederate pickets discover the abandoned works, and Lee’s columns begin moving after Porter.

Lee’s plan rests on the wrong response

He expects McClellan to defend the supply line and Porter to make a stand at Powhite Creek.

The intended trap

Jackson is to pass beyond the Federal right while A. P. Hill and Longstreet drive the expected Powhite Creek line toward Jackson and D. H. Hill.

Two assumptions fail

McClellan will not protect White House Landing, and Porter is entrenched on stronger high ground behind a stream missing from Lee’s map.

Lee must revise in real time

The episode ends its operational narrative with the Confederate commander moving into a situation different from the one he planned for.

The book recommendation

The hosts recommend Angus Konstam’s Seven Days Battles 1862: Lee’s Defense of Richmond as an introduction to the campaign.
