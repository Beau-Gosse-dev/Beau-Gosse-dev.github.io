SEVEN DAYS' BATTLES: GAINES'S MILL (Part the First)

Although Porter’s V Corps had repulsed the Confederate attacks at Beaver Dam Creek, McClellan ordered it four miles east to a new defensive position behind Boatswain’s Swamp and decided to abandon the Richmond & York River Railroad supply line in favor of a base on the James. Porter prepared to hold north of the Chickahominy while the rest of the Army of the Potomac remained south of the river. Lee pursued a plan based on the mistaken expectation that the Federals would defend Powhite Creek and the route to White House Landing; on the morning of June 27, he had to revise that plan after encountering Porter’s unanticipated position. The episode ends before the main Battle of Gaines’s Mill begins
Episode scopeThe decisions, terrain, commands, and faulty assumptions that set up Gaines’s MillThe opening eyewitness readings preview the late-afternoon fighting, but the main narrative stops before the battle begins.
Campaign dateFriday, June 27, 1862Gaines’s Mill was the third of the Seven Days Battles and is also called First Cold Harbor. This installment covers the preceding night and the opening movements that morning.
McClellan’s 1 a.m. orderPorter was directed to leave Beaver Dam Creek and fall back about four milesThe episode identifies roughly 1 a.m. on June 27 as the moment McClellan gave up the immediate effort to capture Richmond.
Three strategic choicesProtect the York River supply line, attack Richmond south of the Chickahominy, or change base to the JamesPorter and other officers urged an attack toward Richmond while V Corps held Lee north of the river. McClellan chose the third course.
Porter’s understandingHold north of the river “to the last extremity”According to the episode’s account of Porter’s later recollection, he believed McClellan meant to attack Richmond while V Corps held. McClellan instead intended the stand to cover the army’s withdrawal.
Change of baseAbandon White House Landing and move the army toward Harrison’s LandingMovable supplies at White House Landing were to go by water to the James. The phrase “change of base” was McClellan’s preferred description; in operational effect the army was retreating from Richmond.
Conflicting assessmentsLee anticipated McClellan’s caution; McClellan underestimated Lee’s appetite for riskThe episode treats the failed Confederate attack at Mechanicsville as sufficient to make McClellan surrender the initiative. Claims that a Federal attack would have captured Richmond are counterfactual, not established fact.
Porter’s positionA defensive arc about 1½–1¾ miles long behind Boatswain’s SwampNational Park Service and episode measurements differ slightly in wording. Both describe an arc on high ground north of the Chickahominy with difficult approaches and useful fields of fire.
TerrainA brush-lined, sluggish stream below a steep rise to the Turkey Hill plateauThe plateau fell toward the Chickahominy on the south. The Watt, McGehee, and Adams houses stood on the high ground.
Why “Gaines’s Mill”The Gaines property gave the battle its name, although most fighting occurred southeast of the millThe episode places Dr. William Gaines’s farm near Powhite Creek and his mill farther north, more than a mile from the principal battlefield.
Confederate map problemLee’s map showed Powhite Creek but omitted Boatswain’s SwampThe episode presents this omission as a major reason Lee expected a Federal line in the wrong place. Exact cartographic content varies among surviving maps; the diagram shows the episode’s stated contrast, not Lee’s original map.
Federal deploymentGeorge Morell faced west; George Sykes faced north; George McCall remained in reserveThe two forward lines followed the creek and slope, with artillery on the plateau crest. McCall’s Pennsylvania Reserves had fought heavily on June 26.
Episode strength estimateA little more than 27,000 men in nine brigades initially available to PorterThe National Park Service gives 27,000 before later reinforcements raised the force to about 34,000. Strength depends on time and counting method; this episode ends before tracing those later arrivals.
Federal artillery17 batteries and 96 guns with V CorpsThe episode also gives William B. Franklin’s VI Corps three long-range batteries south of the Chickahominy that could fire across the river. Battery and gun totals can vary with time and attachment.
Slocum checkedHenry W. Slocum’s division was initially stopped before crossing to reinforce PorterThe episode says McClellan recalled the division when reports suggested a Confederate attack south of the river. Elements of Slocum’s division did reinforce Porter later on June 27, beyond this installment’s endpoint.
Magruder’s demonstrationTroop movements, shouted orders, drums, and pressure along the Richmond frontJohn B. Magruder sought to convince the Federal corps south of the Chickahominy that they faced a serious attack, helping keep them from being used more aggressively.
Lee’s expected Federal responseDefend Powhite Creek and the supply route toward White House LandingMcClellan had instead decided to abandon that route. Porter occupied Boatswain’s Swamp, a stronger position east of where Lee expected to meet him.
Confederate morning conceptJackson and D. H. Hill move toward Old Cold Harbor while A. P. Hill and Longstreet press the retreating Federals eastLee expected the outside columns to get beyond the Federal right and receive troops driven from Powhite Creek. The arrows in the diagram are generalized operational relationships, not exact routes.
Command uncertaintyLee began June 27 still trying to locate Jackson and interpret the Federal withdrawalThe episode says Lee sent an aide to find Jackson after first light. Confederate pickets then reported that the Beaver Dam Creek line was empty.
Leader casualties in scopeNone in the narrated setup to the battlePorter’s Mexican War wound is biographical background. The opening readings describe later combat at Gaines’s Mill but do not establish a killed or wounded senior commander for this episode’s losses section.
Recommended bookSeven Days Battles 1862: Lee’s Defense of Richmond by Angus KonstamThe episode describes it as an introductory volume in Osprey Publishing’s Campaign series. The automated transcript renders the author’s surname as “Constam.”
Transcript normalizationsFitz John Porter, Boatswain’s Swamp, Powhite Creek, McGehee House, and Philip St. George CookeThe automated transcript variously renders these as “Fitch,” “Boats/Boesens,” “Powhat,” “McGeehee,” and “Cook.”
