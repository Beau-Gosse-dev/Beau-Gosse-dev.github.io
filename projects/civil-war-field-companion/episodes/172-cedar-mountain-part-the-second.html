Episode 172 · August 7–11, 1862; battle fought August 9 · Cedar Mountain battlefield along the Culpeper–Orange Road south of Culpeper Court House, Culpeper County, Virginia

CEDAR MOUNTAIN (Part the Second)

Confederate victory. Banks’s outnumbered II Corps struck first and broke part of Jackson’s left, but arriving brigades from A. P. Hill’s Light Division restored the line and drove the Federals toward Culpeper. Jackson stopped the pursuit after dark and withdrew across the Rapidan on August 11 as the rest of Pope’s army concentrated
Episode scopeJackson’s August 7 advance through the August 11 withdrawal after Cedar MountainEpisode 171 established the opposing armies and the move toward Culpeper. This episode covers the approach march, deployment, battle, casualties, and immediate operational result.
CampaignNorthern Virginia CampaignThe National Park Service treats Cedar Mountain as the opening battle of the campaign that culminated at Second Manassas.
Confederate objectiveDefeat Banks before Pope’s Army of Virginia could concentrateJackson moved north from the Gordonsville area after learning that Banks’s II Corps was forward near Culpeper and separated from Pope’s other corps.
August 8 marchConfused, delayed, and broken up by severe heatJackson’s secrecy and changes to the order of march contributed to friction with A. P. Hill. The episode states that only about half of Jackson’s command crossed the Rapidan that day.
Banks’s decisionAdvanced south and attacked before Federal reinforcements arrivedBanks and Pope later disagreed about the meaning of Pope’s verbal orders. The episode assigns responsibility to Pope for failing to provide unambiguous written instructions.
Forces in the episodeAbout 9,000 Federals and about 15,000 Confederates brought into the fightThe National Park Service battlefield database lists 8,030 United States and 16,868 Confederate troops engaged. Differences reflect how cavalry, artillery, late arrivals, and troops actually committed are counted.
BattlefieldA roughly north–south front of fields and woods beneath Cedar MountainThe Federal and Confederate lines were generally separated by about half a mile. Woods screened the northern flanks; wheat, corn, pasture, and rolling ground shaped the attacks.
Other namesCedar Run, Slaughter’s Mountain, and Belle GroveThe episode explains that Cedar Mountain was also called Slaughter’s Mountain. The National Park Service battlefield database lists Belle Grove as another name.
WeatherExtreme heat, reaching about 98°F in the episode’s accountMen in both armies fell out from exhaustion and heat illness during the approach and deployment.
Early’s advanceSecured the road junction and formed near the Crittenden farmJubal Early detached part of his brigade to guard against cavalry, drove back Federal horsemen with artillery support, and deployed astride the Culpeper–Orange Road.
Confederate high groundEwell placed Trimble, Forno, and artillery on Cedar Mountain’s northern shoulderThe position protected the Confederate right and enabled artillery to fire across much of the Federal front.
Federal deploymentWilliams north of the road; Augur south of itCrawford’s and Gordon’s brigades formed in the wooded and open ground north of the road. Geary, Prince, and Greene formed in Augur’s sector south of it.
Artillery duelOpened at about 3 p.m. and continued for roughly two hoursConfederate guns near the road, behind the infantry line, and on Cedar Mountain created intersecting fields of fire against Federal batteries north of Cedar Run.
Winder mortally woundedStruck by a shell while directing artillery fireBrig. Gen. Charles S. Winder suffered devastating wounds to his left arm and side and died within hours. His loss disrupted command of the Confederate division immediately before the infantry battle.
Command successionWilliam B. Taliaferro took command of Winder’s divisionTaliaferro could not locate Jackson and had not been told the full plan. He concentrated on the sector around his own brigade and Early’s left.
Federal attackBanks attacked at about 5:30 p.m.Augur’s brigades advanced through the cornfield south of the road. Shortly before 6 p.m., three regiments of Crawford’s brigade crossed the wheat field north of the road.
Crawford’s penetrationBroke Garnett’s left and disordered part of the Stonewall BrigadeCrawford’s men struck a poorly aligned Confederate left, fought through the woods, and turned into Taliaferro’s flank. The advance was not supported by enough fresh Federal infantry to exploit the opening.
Jackson’s rallyJackson entered the broken sector and steadied retreating troopsThe episode repeats the account that his sword had rusted in its scabbard, so he brandished the sheathed weapon while urging the men to stand.
Hill’s counterattackBranch, Archer, and Pender struck Crawford’s front and rightThe arrival of A. P. Hill’s brigades supplied the reserves Jackson lacked. Edward L. Thomas also reinforced the Confederate right near Early.
Crawford’s lossesMore than half of the attacking brigade killed, wounded, or capturedThe episode describes the brigade retreating across the wheat field after its initial success and severe close-range fighting.
Federal retreatBanks withdrew toward Culpeper after darkThe 10th Maine, elements of Gordon’s brigade, Federal artillery, and a cavalry charge helped cover the retreat. A division from McDowell’s corps arrived too late to enter the battle.
Pegram’s batteryCaught in Federal crossfire during the pursuitCapt. William J. Pegram unlimbered on open ground against the retreating Federals. United States batteries silenced his guns, and Jackson halted the pursuit after roughly a mile.
Casualties in the episode2,403 Federal and 1,418 Confederate casualtiesThe episode gives 1,786 Federals killed or wounded and 617 captured; Confederate losses are 1,376 killed or wounded and 42 captured. The National Park Service rounds the totals to about 2,500 and 1,400.
Immediate aftermathBurials under flags of truce; Jackson withdrew on August 11Jackson could not remain against the rest of Pope’s concentrating army and retired toward Gordonsville across the Rapidan.
Operational effectPope lost the initiative despite Jackson’s withdrawalThe U.S. Army Center of Military History records that Jackson defeated Banks but did not pursue because Pope’s main body was nearby. Lee then shifted the broader campaign into northern Virginia.
Recommended book“Stonewall Jackson at Cedar Mountain” by Robert K. KrickThe hosts describe the book as detailed on the Confederate side and less complete on Federal movements.

What happened

William Warren describes five wounds

Lt. William P. Warren of the 28th New York recounts being wounded repeatedly during Crawford’s charge and remaining in the wheat field until Monday.

Edward Moore recalls Winder’s battery position

Pvt. Edward A. Moore of the Rockbridge Artillery describes Charles Winder directing guns near the road and Capt. Joseph Carpenter being mortally wounded.

The shell strikes Winder

Moore’s account records the shell tearing through Winder’s arm and side; the general dies a few hours later.

The battle date and campaign setting are established

The hosts place Cedar Mountain on August 9, 1862, south of Culpeper and at the beginning of the campaign leading to Second Manassas.

Lee’s two-army problem is reviewed

McClellan remains at Harrison’s Landing while Pope’s Army of Virginia threatens Confederate rail links in central Virginia.

McClellan is ordered off the Peninsula

The Army of the Potomac is to move by water and join Pope, producing a limited window for Jackson to attack.

Jackson identifies Banks as isolated

On August 7 Jackson learns that Banks’s II Corps is forward near Culpeper and begins moving to strike it.

John Gould describes close-range fire

Lt. John M. Gould of the 10th Maine describes the confusion, intermingled formations, and varied weapons in Crawford’s fight.

John Blue watches Jackson react

Lt. John M. Blue, a Confederate courier, recounts Jackson hearing the battle intensify and riding toward the threatened left.

Jackson enters the retreating line

Blue describes the disputed sword-rally episode amid smoke, dense foliage, noise, and men falling back through the woods.

The August 7 advance begins

Jackson intends to cross the Rapidan on August 8 and attack Banks near Culpeper on August 9.

Heat and confusion disrupt the march

Changes in marching order, limited communication, and extreme temperatures string out the Confederate column.

Only half the command crosses the Rapidan

Jackson’s divisions are not concentrated as planned, and many soldiers are exhausted before the battle day begins.

Banks advances to meet Jackson

Instead of waiting at Culpeper, Banks moves south under verbal orders whose later interpretations remain disputed.

Banks supports Crawford and Bayard

Banks moves the rest of his corps toward Crawford’s infantry brigade and George Bayard’s cavalry near Cedar Mountain.

The Federal strength is estimated

The episode gives Banks about 9,000 men at Cedar Mountain, including roughly 1,200 cavalry.

The battlefield terrain is described

The opposing lines run roughly north to south across wheat, corn, pasture, and wooded ground beneath Cedar Mountain.

The artillery duel begins

At about 3 p.m. the guns open; Confederate positions on and behind the line create a damaging crossfire.

Ewell sends Early forward

Early’s brigade advances from about 8 a.m., screens against Federal cavalry, and develops the position ahead.

Early meets Federal sentries

Confederate skirmishers make contact and pause while commanders observe the growing Federal force.

Early moves on the road junction

Ewell directs Early to secure the intersection where the Madison Court House road met the Culpeper–Orange route.

Slaughter’s Mountain is identified

The episode explains Cedar Mountain’s alternate name and its association with the Slaughter family.

Jackson and Ewell make the battle plan

Ewell will place Trimble and Henry Forno on Cedar Mountain while Early presses up the road and Winder comes in on the left.

Jackson’s engaged strength is estimated

The episode states that Jackson ultimately brings about 15,000 men into the Cedar Mountain fighting.

Early forms near the Crittenden farmhouse

His roughly 1,500 men cross the farm lane and take position before Federal artillery forces them below the crest.

Ewell occupies Cedar Mountain

Trimble and Forno move to the northern shoulder, giving Confederate artillery commanding ground over the southern battlefield.

Winder’s division arrives

The division begins deploying north of the road while A. P. Hill’s six brigades remain stretched along the route behind it.

Banks conceals the smaller Federal force

Augur is screened by the cornfield south of the road and Williams by woods north of it.

The artillery duel intensifies

The batteries exchange fire for about two hours while the Confederate infantry line remains imperfectly aligned.

Winder becomes the senior casualty

A Federal shell mortally wounds Winder while he is helping direct artillery fire near the road.

Taliaferro assumes division command

Without Jackson’s full plan and unable to find him, Taliaferro concentrates on the immediate center of the line.

Thomas reinforces Early

Edward L. Thomas’s Georgians, the first brigade of Hill’s division to arrive, strengthen the Confederate right.

Banks attacks first

At about 5:30 p.m., Augur sends Geary and Prince through the cornfield against the Confederate center and right.

Augur is wounded

George S. Greene replaces the wounded division commander as the attack disrupts Jackson’s intended advance south of the road.

Crawford crosses the wheat field

Three regiments emerge from the woods north of the road and strike the exposed Confederate left shortly before 6 p.m.

Garnett’s line breaks

After three volleys, part of Garnett’s brigade and the Stonewall Brigade fall back as Crawford drives into the woods.

Jackson rallies the left

Jackson rides into the disordered sector and urges the men to hold while the Federal advance loses cohesion and reserves.

Hill’s brigades counterattack

Branch attacks Crawford’s front while Archer and Pender move against his right, reversing the Federal breakthrough.

Crawford retreats

With more than half the brigade killed, wounded, or captured, the survivors withdraw across the wheat field.

Federal reserves cover the withdrawal

The 10th Maine and parts of Gordon’s brigade suffer heavily while trying to slow the Confederate counterattack.

Banks retreats toward Culpeper

Darkness settles as the Federal line gives way; a division from McDowell’s corps reaches the area too late to fight.

Pegram’s battery is silenced

Federal guns catch William J. Pegram’s exposed battery in crossfire during the pursuit.

Jackson halts the pursuit

After advancing about a mile beyond the battlefield, Jackson stops rather than press into Pope’s approaching army after dark.

The casualty totals are given

The episode counts 2,403 Federal and 1,418 Confederate killed, wounded, or captured.

Jackson withdraws after the burials

Following flags of truce to bury the dead, the Confederates begin retiring across the Rapidan on August 11.

The operational result is assessed

Although Jackson’s tactics were costly and he left the field, the battle checked Pope and transferred the initiative to Lee.

The book recommendation is given

The hosts recommend Robert K. Krick’s “Stonewall Jackson at Cedar Mountain.”
