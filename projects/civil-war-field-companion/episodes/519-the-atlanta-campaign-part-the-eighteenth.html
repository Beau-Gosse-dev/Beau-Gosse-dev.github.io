What happened

The U.S. assault began on June 27

Federal artillery opened on the Kennesaw line. Samuel G. French’s Confederate division held Little Kennesaw and Pigeon Hill.

McPherson assigned XV Corps the main northern attack

Skirmishers pressed Big Kennesaw while John A. Logan’s corps attacked Little Kennesaw and Pigeon Hill.

Morgan L. Smith selected three brigades

Charles C. Walcutt attacked on the left, Giles A. Smith in the center, and Joseph A. J. Lightburn south of Burnt Hickory Road.

The assault force numbered more than 5,500

The three brigades had marched several miles during the night to reach their starting positions.

Most soldiers learned the plan at 7 a.m.

Officers received their final instructions one hour before the assault. Giles Smith described his brigade’s assignment as a forlorn hope.

Walcutt attacked between the two heights

The 46th Ohio led as skirmishers, followed by the 103rd Illinois and 6th Iowa, with three regiments behind them.

Confederate lines created a crossfire

William W. Berry’s and Francis M. Cockrell’s brigades bent toward each other between Little Kennesaw and Pigeon Hill.

The 46th Ohio captured about 120 skirmishers

The regiment overran a Mississippi skirmish line and continued onto the southern slope of Little Kennesaw.

Dense vegetation broke up Walcutt’s formation

The leading regiments pushed through tangled timber and undergrowth before Confederate fire stopped them on the slope.

Parts of the brigade came close to the works

The 103rd Illinois stopped about 60 yards away. An officer of the 46th Ohio reported reaching about 15 yards before steep rocks blocked further movement.

Walcutt withdrew

The brigade fell partway down the slope, stayed under cover for several hours, and then returned to its starting line.

Giles Smith attacked Pigeon Hill

Six regiments advanced north of Burnt Hickory Road against Cockrell’s Missouri brigade.

Smith’s men drove in the skirmish line

The U.S. regiments crossed thick woods, reformed at the base, and climbed until concentrated Confederate fire stopped them.

Cockrell requested reinforcements

At 9 a.m., French sent several regiments from Matthew D. Ector’s brigade because Cockrell faced both Walcutt and Smith.

The 57th Ohio colors were recovered

Captain Alva Skilton retrieved the fallen flag while under fire and carried it down the slope.

William Porter was killed

The 55th Illinois captain was killed on Pigeon Hill one month after his marriage.

Americus V. Rice was severely wounded

The 57th Ohio colonel suffered several wounds. His right leg was amputated that evening, and he survived.

Lightburn attacked south of Burnt Hickory Road

Six regiments in two lines advanced against Hugh W. Mercer’s brigade on the right of W. H. T. Walker’s division.

Rough ground disrupted Lightburn’s lines

A creek, woods, ravines, swampy ground, and thickets slowed the brigade before it reached the Confederate skirmish pits.

The 63rd Georgia was driven from its rifle pits

Lightburn’s lead regiments fought at close range and overwhelmed the outnumbered Confederate skirmishers.

Lightburn entered open ground

Mercer’s infantry had a clear field of fire, while artillery on Pigeon Hill fired into the U.S. flank.

Lightburn ended the attack

Infantry and artillery fire stopped the brigade before the main Confederate works, and it withdrew.

Osterhaus added a supporting demonstration

A heavy skirmish line advanced south of Lightburn but did not press a full assault against Walker’s line.

The XV Corps attack was over by noon

All three assault brigades had been repulsed. None secured a position in the main Confederate works.
