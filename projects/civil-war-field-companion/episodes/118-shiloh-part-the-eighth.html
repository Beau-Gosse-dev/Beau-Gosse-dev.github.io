Episode 118 · April 6, 1862, about 10:30 a.m.–2 p.m. · Locust Grove Run, the peach orchard, Duncan Field, Davis Wheat Field, and the farm lane later called the Sunken Road at Shiloh, Tennessee

SHILOH (Part the Eighth)

Stuart’s isolated brigade held near the river for about two hours despite being outnumbered, while MacArthur’s brigade absorbed part of Jackson’s attack. The Wallace–Prentiss–Hurlbut line used woods, thickets, and the farm lane to stop several separate Confederate assaults. Shaver withdrew after first contact at Duncan Field; Cheatham’s attack failed at about 11 a.m.; Hindman’s larger attack lost coordination before noon; and Gibson’s brigade made repeated unsuccessful attacks until it was exhausted by about 2 p.m. The position later called the Hornet’s Nest held because the Confederate center remained thin and the attacks arrived separately

What happened

The episode returns to Stuart’s isolated brigade

One Illinois and two Ohio regiments guarded the far United States left near the Tennessee River.

Chalmers and Jackson reached Stuart

At about 11 a.m., nine Confederate regiments and two batteries deployed against Stuart’s three regiments behind Locust Grove Run.

The 71st Ohio broke

Colonel Rodney Mason fled, part of the regiment followed, and the unit disintegrated after its second-in-command was mortally wounded.

Stuart rebuilt a shorter line

The 55th Illinois rallied about 200 yards back and joined the 54th Ohio and part of the 71st Ohio, leaving about 800 men with a ravine behind them.

The Confederate attack slowed

Chalmers’s men needed ammunition, soldiers left the ranks for captured camps, and Stuart’s reduced line continued firing.

MacArthur drew off part of Jackson’s brigade

Three Illinois regiments held about a quarter mile west of Stuart. The 9th Illinois lost 361 killed and wounded, 62 percent of its strength.

The entire United States line is identified

From east to west it ran through Stuart, MacArthur, Hurlbut, Prentiss, W. H. L. Wallace, then the retreating McClernand and Sherman.

The Confederate center was nearly empty

At about 11 a.m., eight brigades faced the United States right and two attacked Stuart, while only one faced Hurlbut, Prentiss, and Wallace.

Bragg and Polk improvised a new command arrangement

With corps intermingled, Bragg took the right, Polk the center, and Hardee the left. Johnston was acting locally near the front.

Shaver made first contact at the Hornet’s Nest

At about 10:30 a.m., his Arkansas brigade entered Duncan Field and withdrew after Tuttle’s Iowans opened fire.

Stevens’s brigade made the first organized assault

At about 11 a.m., Benjamin Cheatham led roughly 1,000 men toward Wallace’s position after Stevens was injured in a fall.

The Iowans fired at thirty yards

The 12th and 14th Iowa delivered two close volleys, then William Shaw led a short counterattack before returning to the farm lane.

Cheatham’s brigade withdrew with heavy losses

All twelve members of the 6th Tennessee color guard fell; the regiment lost fourteen officers and 226 enlisted men.

Grant ordered the line held

Around 11 a.m., he visited Hurlbut, Prentiss, and Wallace. Prentiss understood the order to hold at all hazards literally.

Hindman assembled the largest assault

About 3,700 men from parts of four brigades advanced around 11:30 with artillery support.

Hindman’s attack lost command and momentum

Hindman was disabled when his horse was hit, Shaver had no horse, Wood was already injured, and the attacking formations withdrew after moving about 80 yards.

Two organized attacks had failed by noon

The United States center had not yet faced the kind of combined pressure that broke Sherman and McClernand.

Bragg sent Gibson’s brigade forward

One Arkansas and three Louisiana regiments advanced across Davis Wheat Field shortly after noon.

United States infantry fired from the thicket

The defenders waited until Gibson’s men were only yards away, then delivered a point-blank volley from concealed positions.

A bayonet charge failed in the brush

The 19th Louisiana entered the thicket but could not move through it and withdrew under fire.

Gibson renewed the attack several times

Accounts differed between three and four assaults. Each was repulsed, and the brigade was exhausted by about 2 p.m.
