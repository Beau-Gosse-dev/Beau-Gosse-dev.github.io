PERRYVILLE (Part the First)

The initiative passed to the United States. Braxton Bragg paused at Bardstown, failed to obtain the large body of Kentucky recruits he expected, and did not promptly unite with Edmund Kirby Smith. Don Carlos Buell retained command, reorganized the Army of the Ohio into three corps, and marched from Louisville on October 1 with about 55,000 soldiers in three main columns while a fourth column feinted toward Frankfort and Lexington. No battle occurs within this episode; it ends with the armies moving toward the Perryville campaign’s decisive phase

People and command

United States — Army of the Ohio

Army of the OhioMaj. Gen. Don Carlos BuellRetained command after Halleck suspended his relief, reorganized the army into three corps, and opened the central Kentucky offensive on October 1.
Second in command, Army of the OhioMaj. Gen. George H. ThomasAsked that Buell be retained rather than immediately assuming command; received no corps in the reorganization.
First CorpsMaj. Gen. Alexander McD. McCookSecond Division—Joshua W. Sill; Third Division—Lovell H. Rousseau; Tenth Division—James S. Jackson. Sill was detached for the Frankfort feint.
Second CorpsMaj. Gen. Thomas L. CrittendenFourth Division—William Sooy Smith; Fifth Division—Horatio P. Van Cleve; Sixth Division—Thomas J. Wood.
Third CorpsActing Maj. Gen. Charles C. GilbertFirst Division—Albin F. Schoepf; Ninth Division—Robert B. Mitchell; Eleventh Division—Philip H. Sheridan.
Frankfort–Lexington feintBrig. Gen. Joshua W. Sill and Col. Ebenezer DumontSill’s detached Second Division and Dumont’s new units formed an eastern column of about 20,000 soldiers.

Confederacy — forces in Kentucky

Army of the Mississippi; senior commander in KentuckyGen. Braxton BraggHeld roughly 30,000 men near Bardstown, then went to Lexington and assumed formal command over the combined Confederate forces in the state.
Army of the Mississippi at BardstownMaj. Gen. Leonidas PolkReceived command at Bardstown when Bragg departed to meet Kirby Smith in Lexington.
Army of KentuckyMaj. Gen. Edmund Kirby SmithHeld roughly 18,000 men near Lexington, supplied Bragg, and resisted moving promptly into a field junction that would place him under Bragg.
Kentucky recruiting effortBrig. Gen. Simon B. BucknerSent to Camp Breckinridge, formerly Camp Dick Robinson, to organize Confederate recruits.
Division, Army of the MississippiMaj. Gen. Patrick R. CleburneReturned from detached service with Kirby Smith and rejoined Bragg at Bardstown.
Chief of staff, Army of the MississippiCol. George W. BrentVirginia officer appointed in late September after Bragg had attempted to perform both command and chief-of-staff duties.

Important leaders killed or wounded

United States

William “Bull” Nelson

Major general and intended commander of the Army of the Ohio’s Third Corps. Shot in the chest by Brig. Gen. Jefferson C. Davis at Louisville’s Galt House on September 29, 1862; died about an hour later.

United States

Robert L. McCook

Brigadier general and older brother of First Corps commander Alexander McCook. Mortally wounded during an encounter with Confederate guerrillas in Tennessee in August 1862; mentioned while introducing the “Fighting McCooks.”
