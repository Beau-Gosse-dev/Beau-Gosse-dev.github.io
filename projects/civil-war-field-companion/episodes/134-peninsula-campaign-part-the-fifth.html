PENINSULA CAMPAIGN (Part the Fifth)

The engagement at Eltham’s Landing did not interrupt Joseph E. Johnston’s withdrawal toward Richmond. A separate United States operation directed from Fort Monroe occupied Norfolk and forced the Confederates to destroy CSS Virginia. The opened James River then allowed Commander John Rodgers’s five-vessel flotilla to approach Richmond, but Confederate artillery, infantry, naval personnel, and river obstructions repulsed it at Drewry’s Bluff on May 15. McClellan meanwhile established a major supply base at White House Landing and deployed the Army of the Potomac on both sides of the Chickahominy while expecting Irvin McDowell’s force to approach from Fredericksburg. The Peninsula Campaign remained unresolved at the end of the episode

Major general commanding the Department of Virginia and Fort MonroeJohn E. WoolLed approximately 5,000 soldiers ashore on May 10 and occupied Norfolk after the Confederate evacuation; his command was separate from McClellan’s Army of the Potomac
Secretary of the NavyGideon WellesExercised civilian authority over naval operations; Flag Officer Louis M. Goldsborough commanded the North Atlantic Blockading Squadron and Commander John Rodgers led the James River flotilla

Confederacy

President and commander in chiefJefferson DavisConsulted his cabinet and military adviser Robert E. Lee as Richmond faced threats from the James River and the Army of the Potomac
General commanding the field army defending RichmondJoseph E. JohnstonContinued the withdrawal from the Peninsula and positioned the army east of Richmond near the Chickahominy
Major general commanding the Department of NorfolkBenjamin HugerEvacuated the Norfolk-Portsmouth position and ordered military property at the Gosport Navy Yard destroyed before Wool’s arrival
Flag officer commanding CSS Virginia and Confederate naval forces in Hampton RoadsJosiah TattnallOrdered Virginia destroyed when evacuation of Norfolk left the deep-draft ironclad without a viable base or route to the upper James
