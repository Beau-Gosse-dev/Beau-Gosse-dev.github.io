Episode 165 · June 30, 1862 · The Glendale crossroads and adjoining farms, woods, and roads between White Oak Swamp and Malvern Hill, east and southeast of Richmond, Virginia

SEVEN DAYS' BATTLES: GLENDALE (Part the Second)

Indecisive. Longstreet’s and A. P. Hill’s attacks broke George A. McCall’s Pennsylvania Reserves and captured artillery, but United States reinforcements held the Willis Church Road. Lee did not cut the Army of the Potomac’s line to the James River, and the Federal army withdrew to Malvern Hill during the night
Episode scopeThe June 30 fighting at Glendale and the withdrawal to Malvern HillEpisode 164 covered the approach to Glendale and the separate action at White Oak Swamp. This episode completes the battle and follows the Army of the Potomac’s night movement south.
Battle namesGlendale, Frayser’s Farm, Nelson’s Farm, and Charles City CrossroadsThe National Park Service records several additional contemporary names, including New Market Crossroads, Riddell’s Shop, and White Oak Swamp. United States and Confederate accounts did not use one uniform name.
Confederate objectiveSever the Willis Church Road and divide the retreating Federal armyThe crossroads controlled the principal route south to Malvern Hill and the James River. Lee attempted to converge separate columns on that bottleneck.
Huger’s actionStopped opposite Slocum on the Charles City RoadAfter clearing the road obstructions, Benjamin Huger brought up only two guns for an unequal artillery exchange and did not order an infantry assault. Slocum later sent George W. Taylor’s brigade toward Glendale.
Holmes at Malvern HillArtillery fire failed to disrupt the Federal wagon routeUnited States batteries and gunboats on the James River drove off Theophilus H. Holmes’s guns and disordered his inexperienced infantry. Holmes did not attack the strong Malvern Hill position.
Magruder’s movementsRepeated orders kept 13,500 men out of the main Glendale fightingThe episode describes seven sets of orders and about 20 miles of marching and countermarching. Magruder reached the Glendale front after the battle, at about 3 a.m. on July 1.
Main Confederate striking forceLongstreet’s division and A. P. Hill’s Light DivisionThese formations approached from the west by the Long Bridge Road and carried out the principal infantry attacks against McCall, Kearny, Hooker, and arriving elements of Sedgwick and Slocum.
Opening of the main assaultMicah Jenkins attacked at about 4 p.m.Longstreet ordered Jenkins to silence James H. Cooper’s battery. Jenkins advanced his whole brigade, beginning a general engagement before Lee or Longstreet had ordered the coordinated assault they intended.
Jenkins’s attackThree charges against Cooper’s battery and Seymour’s brigadeJenkins’s South Carolinians reached the gun line and briefly took the battery before a Federal counterattack forced them away. The episode gives more than 500 casualties from about 1,200 men.
Palmetto Sharpshooters254 casualties from 375 engaged in the episode’s accountThe episode gives 44 killed and 210 wounded, or about 68 percent of the regiment engaged. Campaign and battle casualty reports do not always separate losses in the same way.
Kemper’s advanceA rapid, unsupported charge reached the Whitlock clearingKemper’s brigade crossed about 1,200 yards of woods and clearings, broke part of Seymour’s left, and reached ground near the Whitlock house. Its speed disrupted its formation and separated it from supporting brigades.
Kemper’s lossesMore than 400 of roughly 1,400 men in the episode’s accountA Federal counterattack drove the unsupported Virginians back. The total includes killed, wounded, and captured soldiers.
Near-breakthroughBranch and Strange advanced within a few hundred yards of Willis Church RoadLawrence O. Branch’s brigade and George Pickett’s brigade, led by Col. John B. Strange while Pickett recovered from a wound, retook the Whitlock clearing and pursued the Federal line eastward.
Sedgwick’s reinforcementDana’s and Sully’s brigades reached the threatened sector in timeThe two brigades returned from the White Oak Swamp sector and entered the fight as they arrived. Their fire, followed by pressure from Hooker’s direction, stopped Branch and Strange before they reached the road.
Fight for Randol’s batteryWilcox attacked Battery E and G, 1st U.S. ArtilleryCapt. Alanson M. Randol’s guns were supported by George G. Meade’s brigade. Prolonged close combat cost the two attacking Alabama regiments more than 250 casualties in the episode’s account, and Meade was wounded.
Pryor and FeatherstonTheir attacks stalled in wooded terrain against Kearny’s divisionRichard Pryor fed regiments forward against artillery and John C. Robinson’s infantry. Winfield S. Featherston extended the line but could not advance, and Maxcy Gregg committed only part of his brigade in support.
McCall capturedTaken by soldiers of the 47th VirginiaWhile trying to organize another line, the wounded division commander rode into Confederate troops and was captured. His Pennsylvania Reserves had already fought at Beaver Dam Creek and Gaines’s Mill.
Kearny’s escapeRode out of a group of Field’s Confederates after being mistaken for a Confederate officerKearny was unarmed and used a verbal bluff to leave the group. The episode distinguishes this escape from his death two months later at Chantilly under different circumstances.
Captured artilleryMore than a dozen guns in the main breakthrough; 18 in the episode’s totalThe National Park Service states that Confederates captured more than a dozen cannon. The episode counts 14 retained near the Cooper and Randol battery line and 18 across the battle.
Final Confederate attacksField, Pender, Archer, and Anderson did not reach Willis Church RoadField withdrew after Taylor threatened his flank. Pender met Sedgwick’s last defensive line, Archer stalled against Hooker, and Joseph R. Anderson’s after-dark advance lost cohesion in the woods.
Battle casualtiesApproximately 3,500 on each sideRichmond National Battlefield Park emphasizes that June 30 totals are uncertain. The episode uses about 3,800 United States and 3,700 Confederate casualties; another widely used compilation gives 3,797 and 3,673, respectively.
Senior command casualtiesMcCall captured; Simmons killed; Meade and several generals woundedCol. Seneca G. Simmons, commanding a Pennsylvania Reserves brigade, was fatally wounded or killed during the counterattack. NPS also identifies United States generals Meade and Sumner and Confederate generals Joseph R. Anderson, Pender, and Featherston as wounded.
Immediate resultThe Willis Church Road remained openThe Confederate penetration did not become a sustained breakthrough. Federal formations withdrew south after the fighting rather than being cut off west or north of the crossroads.
Night withdrawalThe Army of the Potomac moved to Malvern HillThe movement was poorly coordinated and some pickets were left behind, but the main body reached the strong position a little more than two miles south of Glendale before the July 1 battle.
Forces actually engagedFewer than 20,000 Confederates against more than 23,000 Federals in the episode’s estimateThese figures describe troops that entered the main fighting, not all soldiers present in the broader June 30 operational area. Jackson, Huger, Holmes, and Magruder did not add infantry to Longstreet and Hill at Glendale.
Recommended book“A Glorious Army: Robert E. Lee’s Triumph, 1862–1863” by Jeffry D. WertThe official episode page and podcast book list identify this title as the recommendation.
Transcript normalizationsHuger, Kearny, Heintzelman, Randol, Maxcy Gregg, and Jeffry D. WertThe automated transcript produces forms including “U.J.,” “Carney,” “Heinzelman,” “Randall,” “Maxie,” and “Jeffrey D. Wirt.” “U.J.” refers to Benjamin Huger.

What happened

The battle date and crossroads are reviewed

The episode returns to Glendale on Monday, June 30, and the Federal line of retreat toward the James River.

Lee’s operational objective is restated

Lee seeks to break the Federal line at the bottleneck and cut off part of McClellan’s army.

The three converging movements are identified

Jackson is north at White Oak Swamp, Huger approaches by Charles City Road, and Longstreet and A. P. Hill approach from the west.

Federal brigades leave Jackson’s front

The episode states that four brigades, nearly 10,000 men, move from the White Oak sector and later reinforce Glendale.

Huger reaches Slocum’s position

Between about 2:30 and 3 p.m., Huger finds Federal infantry and artillery on a ridge across the creek.

Huger’s two guns are suppressed

Federal counterbattery fire overwhelms the small Confederate artillery deployment, and no infantry assault follows.

Slocum releases Taylor’s brigade

Huger’s failure to attack allows one VI Corps brigade to reinforce the main Glendale line.

Longstreet and Hill reach the Federal pickets

Their columns halt on the Long Bridge Road at about 11 a.m., roughly one mile short of Glendale.

Artillery fire is mistaken for the attack signal

Lee and Longstreet hear firing to the north and assume that Huger has begun the planned coordinated action.

Lee inspects the Malvern Hill sector

A report of Federal troops and wagons moving over Malvern Hill prompts Lee to ride toward the River Road.

Holmes opens against Malvern Hill

About six rifled guns shell the Federal position and wagon route before land batteries and gunboats reply.

Federal fire drives off Holmes’s force

Gunboat and artillery shells scatter the Confederate guns and cause inexperienced infantry to retreat in disorder.

Magruder is redirected toward Holmes

Lee diverts Magruder from supporting Longstreet and Hill because he believes added force may be needed at Malvern Hill.

Magruder’s marches are summarized

Successive changes send the division between the River Road and Glendale; it reaches the front only after the battle.

The main battle begins from a misunderstood order

Longstreet’s direction to silence a battery leads Micah Jenkins to advance his full South Carolina brigade.

Jenkins attacks Cooper’s battery

At about 4 p.m., the South Carolinians move against Cooper’s guns and Truman Seymour’s Pennsylvania Reserves.

Three charges reach the gun line

Jenkins’s brigade enters hand-to-hand fighting, briefly takes the battery, and then loses the guns to a Federal counterattack.

Jenkins’s losses are counted

The episode gives more than 500 brigade casualties and 254 casualties in the Palmetto Sharpshooters.

Longstreet orders the other brigades forward

The wider Confederate attack begins at about 5 p.m. after Jenkins has already opened the engagement.

Kemper charges toward the Whitlock house

The Virginia brigade moves rapidly through woods and clearings, loses formation, and strikes Seymour’s exposed left.

A Federal counterattack drives Kemper back

Three regiments attack the unsupported Virginians, who withdraw with more than 400 casualties in the episode’s account.

Branch and Strange renew the advance

Their brigades cross the Whitlock clearing and move within a few hundred yards of Willis Church Road.

Sedgwick’s brigades reach the threatened line

Dana’s and Sully’s troops enter the action and help stop the Confederate movement toward the road.

Wilcox attacks along the Long Bridge Road

Two Alabama regiments support Jenkins while two others attack Randol’s battery and Meade’s brigade.

Meade is wounded

Close combat around Randol’s gun line forces Meade to leave the field; Wilcox’s attacking regiments later withdraw.

Pryor attacks Kearny’s sector

Wooded terrain breaks up the advance, and Federal canister and Robinson’s infantry stop the Confederate regiments.

Featherston and Gregg extend the Confederate left

Featherston joins Pryor but cannot advance; Gregg commits one regiment and does not make a general attack.

Field enters near the batteries

Field’s brigade advances as Wilcox withdraws and presses toward Randol’s and Cooper’s positions.

McCall is captured

The division commander rides among soldiers of the 47th Virginia while trying to place another Federal line.

Kearny escapes from Field’s troops

Mistaken for a Confederate officer, Kearny gives an order and rides away before his identity is recognized.

Confederates retain the battery line

Field overruns Randol’s guns while other regiments retake Cooper’s battery; the episode counts 14 guns held in this sector.

Taylor threatens Field’s flank

The New Jersey brigade from Slocum’s division attacks from the left and leads Field to withdraw to a safer position.

Pender strikes the last Federal line

Pender advances beyond the Whitlock house but cannot dislodge Sedgwick’s troops near Willis Church Road.

Archer’s attack stalls against Hooker

Archer holds ground south of the Whitlock house without breaking the Federal division to his front and right.

Anderson advances after dark

Smoke, woods, and failing light prevent the final A. P. Hill brigade from maintaining a coherent attack.

Battle casualties are assessed

The episode uses about 3,700 Confederate and 3,800 United States casualties while noting the difficulty of separating campaign losses.

Captured guns are totaled

The episode counts 18 Federal guns taken across the June 30 fighting.

The Confederate objective remains unmet

Lee’s attacks have not severed Willis Church Road or cut off part of the Army of the Potomac.

McClellan reaches Malvern Hill

At about 8:30 p.m., McClellan arrives at Fitz John Porter’s headquarters as the Glendale fighting ends.

The troops actually engaged are compared

The episode estimates fewer than 20,000 Confederate attackers against more than 23,000 Federal defenders.

The Federal withdrawal resumes

The Glendale defenders leave their lines during the night and move south on Willis Church Road.

The column reaches Malvern Hill

The retreating troops climb the hill before dawn, where the final major battle of the Seven Days will occur on July 1.

The episode’s book recommendation is given

The hosts recommend Jeffry D. Wert’s “A Glorious Army: Robert E. Lee’s Triumph, 1862–1863.”

Important leaders killed or wounded

United States

Col. Seneca G. Simmons

Commander, 1st Brigade, Pennsylvania Reserves division. Fatally wounded or killed while leading the counterattack against Kemper’s brigade. Contemporary and later records differ on whether he died on the field or during the following night; the official army register records his death at the June 30 battle.

United States

Capt. Henry J. Biddle

Assistant adjutant general, Pennsylvania Reserves division. Mortally wounded at Glendale and captured. He was taken to Chimborazo Hospital in Richmond and died on July 20, 1862.
