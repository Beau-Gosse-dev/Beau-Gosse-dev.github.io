What happened

The episode turns to the Dead Angle

Cheatham’s division held the line south of Cleburne. The XIV Corps attack would strike a bend in that line at Cheatham Hill.

Cheatham occupied the Kennesaw line

Vaughan’s brigade moved into position on June 19, and the rest of Cheatham’s division followed on the night of June 20.

Maney had to bend his brigade line

The ridge ended beside a branch of John Ward Creek. Maney placed part of his brigade on a low hill and the remainder along ground facing south.

A gap and bend created a shallow salient

Cheatham’s two wings were separated by about 150 yards, and the connecting line projected west toward the U.S. position.

Engineers placed the works on the natural crest

The line stood a few yards behind the military crest. Parts of the slope therefore remained hidden from the Confederate defenders.

Visibility was restricted at the salient

Men on Vaughan’s left and Maney’s right could see only 25 to 40 yards. Farther north, visibility improved to about 75 yards.

The problem became clear on June 21

Daylight exposed the mistake, but U.S. artillery fire made rebuilding the line difficult. Cheatham kept the position and strengthened it.

A forward ridge covered the approach

A creek valley and ridge lay about 400 yards west of the works. Cheatham sent skirmishers forward to occupy the ridge.

U.S. troops captured the forward ridge

On June 23, Stanley’s division drove off the Confederate skirmishers and captured about 40 prisoners.

The 1st and 27th Tennessee counterattacked

Cheatham called for volunteers, but the consolidated regiment advanced together. It drove U.S. skirmishers back without retaking the ridge.

U.S. artillery struck the angle on June 24

Fire damaged the works and partly enfiladed Vaughan’s left. Confederate artillery remained concealed and did not answer.

The defenders rebuilt their earthworks

The Tennesseans added earth, rocks, timber, head logs, and firing steps. Fence rails inside the trench helped keep displaced head logs from falling on the men.

Obstacles were unevenly distributed

Vaughan’s front had abatis about 30 yards from the works. Maney’s position at the salient had less obstruction and more open ground.

Some Confederate regiments were very small

Several depleted Tennessee regiments had been consolidated. The combined 1st and 27th Tennessee probably had no more than 200 men present.

Confederate generals inspected the position

On June 26, Johnston, Hardee, and Cheatham visited the line. Cheatham and Hardee told the defenders the position had to be held.

George Gordon coordinated supporting fire

Gordon arranged for adjacent regiments to fire obliquely across one another’s fronts where the ground blocked each unit’s direct view.

Eight guns covered the attacking route

Cheatham placed artillery south of the salient so it could fire along the right flank of troops attacking the Dead Angle.

Two XIV Corps brigades received the assault assignment

McCook was to attack the northern shoulder and apex; Mitchell was to attack the southern shoulder. Morgan’s brigade remained in support.

U.S. troops prepared before dawn

The men were awakened at 4 a.m., ate breakfast, left their knapsacks, and carried haversacks, canteens, and 60 rounds.

The bombardment began at 8 a.m.

XIV Corps artillery and guns from the XX Corps sector opened on Cheatham Hill, warning the defenders that the assault was beginning.
