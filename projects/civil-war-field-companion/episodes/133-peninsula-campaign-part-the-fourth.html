Episode 133 · May 4-7, 1862 · The Virginia Peninsula from Yorktown through Williamsburg to Eltham's Landing near West Point, Virginia

PENINSULA CAMPAIGN (Part the Fourth)

The Battle of Williamsburg on May 5 and the fight at Eltham's Landing on May 7 were tactically inconclusive. Operationally, the Confederate rear guard achieved its purpose: it delayed and contained the United States pursuit while Joseph E. Johnston's army, artillery, and trains continued toward Richmond. Hancock repulsed Early's poorly coordinated attack at Williamsburg, but the opportunity against the Confederate flank was not exploited. Franklin established a bridgehead at Eltham's Landing but did not reach the retreat road in time to block Johnston's column
Battle of WilliamsburgMay 5, 1862Also called the Battle of Fort Magruder; the National Park Service classifies the result as indecisive.
Rear-guard objectiveProtect Johnston's withdrawal toward RichmondLongstreet held the prepared Williamsburg line while Confederate trains struggled over rain-soaked roads.
Williamsburg defensive positionFort Magruder and a line of smaller redoubtsCreeks, swampy ground, felled timber, and the narrow Peninsula restricted the routes of approach. Contemporary accounts and later summaries describe 13 smaller redoubts in addition to Fort Magruder.
Williamsburg casualtiesAbout 2,300 United States and 1,600 ConfederateThe NPS battle database gives 2,283 and 1,560. Other authoritative summaries give approximately 2,300 and 1,600, while a commonly cited Confederate total of 1,682 includes the May 4 cavalry fighting. The 5th North Carolina return was omitted from one Official Records-based tabulation.
Hooker's divisionFought unsupported for much of May 5Kearny's arriving division stabilized the United States left after Confederate attacks pushed Hooker's line toward the Lee's Mill Road.
Hancock's flanking forceAbout 3,400 infantry and eight gunsHancock crossed Cub Creek, occupied two empty redoubts on the Confederate left, and repulsed elements of Early's brigade.
Early's brigade losses508 reported in the episodeThe episode says the 5th North Carolina alone lost 302 killed, wounded, or captured. Published tabulations differ because returns are incomplete and may separate that regiment from the rest of the brigade.
Senior officers killed at WilliamsburgAt least four regimental commandersUnited States Lieutenant Colonel John P. Van Leer and Confederate Colonel George T. Ward, Lieutenant Colonel Thomas E. Irby, and Colonel Christopher H. Mott were killed.
Hancock becomes "the Superb"McClellan praised Hancock's performanceNewspaper use of the phrase helped establish the nickname after the battle.
Battle of Eltham's LandingMay 7, 1862Also called Barhamsville or West Point; the National Park Service classifies it as indecisive.
Eltham's Landing casualtiesApproximately 186 United States and 48 ConfederateThe NPS battle database gives a total of 234. Some secondary accounts give a slightly higher United States figure.
Operational outcome at ElthamThe bridgehead did not cut the Confederate retreat routeHood's brigade pushed the United States picket line back before disengaging; Johnston's column continued toward Richmond.
