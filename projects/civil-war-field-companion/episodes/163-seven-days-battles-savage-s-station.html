SEVEN DAYS' BATTLES: SAVAGE'S STATION

The Battle of Savage’s Station was tactically indecisive. John B. Magruder attacked the United States rear guard late on June 29 but committed only part of his command and did not stop the withdrawal. Edwin V. Sumner’s troops held until darkness, then crossed White Oak Swamp. The Confederates occupied the field and captured the sick and wounded left at the station, but Robert E. Lee failed to trap the retreating army north of the swamp
Episode scopeThe June 29 pursuit and Battle of Savage’s StationThe episode follows the Army of the Potomac’s withdrawal after Gaines’s Mill, the battle at Savage’s Station, and the night march through White Oak Swamp.
Federal objectiveMove the army and supply trains to a new base on the James RiverMcClellan called the operation a change of base. Two corps moved toward Malvern Hill while the remaining corps covered the roads and White Oak Swamp crossings.
Withdrawal beginsEarly June 28Men, artillery, livestock, and wagons moved south toward the limited crossings through White Oak Swamp.
Lee identifies the routeBefore daylight on June 29Lee concluded that McClellan was moving toward the James and issued pursuit orders to his division commanders.
Lee’s June 29 planFix the rear guard north of White Oak Swamp while other columns moved toward GlendaleMagruder was to advance east on the Williamsburg Road, Jackson was to cross the Chickahominy on Magruder’s left, and Huger was to use the Charles City Road. Longstreet and A. P. Hill had the longer march toward Glendale for the following day.
Savage’s Station functionRail depot, supply point, headquarters site, and field hospitalThe Richmond & York River Railroad connected the station to the former Federal supply base at White House Landing.
Hospital populationMore than 3,000 sick and wounded before the retreatThe episode states that about 2,500 men who could not be transported were left at the hospital and became prisoners. These patients were separate from the battle’s casualty count.
Destroyed materielFood, equipment, rolling stock, and ammunition that could not be movedUnited States troops burned large stores and railroad cars. The episode also describes an ammunition train sent toward the destroyed Chickahominy bridge.
Rear-guard commandNo single commander was formally designated by McClellanSumner, Heintzelman, and Franklin received withdrawal instructions, but coordination depended on communication among three corps commanders after McClellan moved south of White Oak Swamp.
Allen’s Farm positionSumner stopped about two miles west of Savage’s StationHe resisted withdrawing to the assigned line until Franklin personally explained the danger to William F. Smith’s exposed division. Sumner fell back at about 11 a.m.
Unified Federal lineEstablished near Savage’s Station by about 1 p.m.The line contained Sumner’s II Corps and elements of Franklin’s VI Corps while other troops and trains continued toward White Oak Swamp.
Heintzelman’s withdrawalIII Corps left before the late-afternoon battleHeintzelman judged that the area contained more troops than could be used and marched south without informing Sumner or Franklin, leaving the Federal left unexpectedly exposed.
Magruder’s effective strengthAbout 14,000 men available; only about 3,000 engagedThe episode states that nine Confederate regiments entered the main fight. Strength totals are approximate and vary with the units and time counted.
Federal force late in the dayAbout 16,000 men under Sumner after other divisions departedThis is the episode’s estimate for Sumner’s remaining command before Smith’s nearest brigade returned. Battlefield strength estimates vary by source.
Magruder’s delayHe waited for support that Lee had not intended him to require before advancingMagruder expected Huger on his right and Jackson in direct cooperation on his left. Lee intended Magruder to initiate the pursuit and Jackson to support it after crossing the river.
Temporary reinforcementTwo of Huger’s brigades were offered only until 2 p.m.Because no major fighting had begun by the deadline, Huger recalled the brigades for the march toward Glendale.
Jackson’s delayHis command remained north of the Chickahominy and did not fight at Savage’s StationBridge repairs progressed slowly. Jackson then interpreted a forwarded contingency message as a reason to remain north of the river, although Lee later said that interpretation was mistaken.
Confederate attack orderIssued by Magruder at about 4 p.m.The principal engagement began near 5 p.m., after the best opportunity to catch a larger portion of the Federal rear guard had passed.
Confederate deploymentKershaw near the railroad, Semmes across the Williamsburg Road, and part of Griffith’s brigade farther southWilliam Barksdale directed Griffith’s brigade after Griffith was wounded. Dense woods disrupted the Confederate right, and some regiments fired on friendly troops before finding the Federal line.
Railroad gunA 32-pound naval gun mounted on a protected railroad carOften called the “Land Merrimac,” it supported the Confederate left along the railroad. The episode describes it as visually imposing but ineffective.
Federal responseSumner committed nearby regiments and recalled William T. H. Brooks’s brigadeBrooks’s brigade returned from its movement toward White Oak Swamp and reinforced the threatened sector south of the Williamsburg Road.
Brooks’s brigade lossesNearly 450 casualties in the episode’s accountThe episode states that the 5th Vermont lost nearly half its strength. Regimental and brigade totals differ among compilations.
Battle durationAbout 5 p.m. to 9 p.m.The fighting extended across the railroad and Williamsburg Road sectors and ended in darkness as rain began.
Battle casualtiesUnited States 919; Confederacy 444These are the National Park Service estimates. The episode gives ranges of 600–1,000 United States casualties and 375–475 Confederate casualties.
Important leader lossBrig. Gen. Richard Griffith mortally woundedGriffith was struck during the Confederate attack and died shortly afterward in Richmond. Sources differ on whether his death occurred late June 29 or on June 30.
Tactical resultIndecisiveThe Confederates occupied the field after the Federal night withdrawal, but neither side broke the opposing battle line during the main engagement.
Operational resultThe Army of the Potomac escaped north of the swampThe rear guard withdrew after dark, and the last Federal troops crossed White Oak Swamp shortly before 10 a.m. on June 30.
Lee’s assessmentThe pursuit had made too little progressLee rebuked Magruder that night and emphasized the need to move before McClellan escaped. The episode also assigns army-level responsibility to Lee for unclear orders and inadequate staff supervision.
Recommended book“To the Gates of Richmond” by Stephen W. SearsThe episode closing identifies Sears’s study of the Peninsula Campaign as its recommendation.

What happened

The retreat after Gaines’s Mill is introduced

Lee waits for confirmation of McClellan’s route after the June 27 battle.

Lee identifies the movement toward the James

By the morning of June 29, Lee is convinced that the Army of the Potomac is moving south.

The Federal withdrawal begins

The episode dates the first large movements toward White Oak Swamp to early June 28.

Lee’s June 29 plan is outlined

Magruder and Jackson are to fix the rear guard while Huger, Longstreet, and A. P. Hill move toward Glendale.

Jackson receives the crossing assignment

He is to rebuild a Chickahominy bridge, cross, and support Magruder’s left.

Speed is identified as the plan’s requirement

Lee needs rapid movement to reduce the advantage created by McClellan’s earlier start.

Longstreet’s engineers examine Golding’s Farm

They find the fieldworks empty shortly after dawn and confirm that the Federal withdrawal is underway.

The Savage’s Station hospital is described

Wounded and sick men arriving from II Corps bring the hospital population above 3,000.

Supplies are destroyed

United States troops burn food, equipment, and railroad rolling stock that cannot be taken south.

Wounded soldiers are left behind

The episode states that nearly 2,500 wounded men remain at the Savage’s Station field hospital.

McClellan moves south of the swamp

He leaves withdrawal orders but appoints no single officer to command the rear guard.

Sumner stops at Allen’s Farm

The II Corps commander refuses to withdraw immediately to Savage’s Station.

The morning action begins

At about 9 a.m., Sumner’s troops engage Confederate elements west of Savage’s Station.

Franklin identifies an exposed gap

William F. Smith’s division is isolated between the Chickahominy and Sumner’s position.

Sumner agrees to fall back

After Franklin reaches him in person, Sumner breaks contact at about 11 a.m.

The rear-guard line is unified

By about 1 p.m., Federal troops occupy the assigned line near Savage’s Station.

Heintzelman withdraws III Corps

He marches toward White Oak Swamp without notifying Sumner or Franklin.

A witness describes the railroad gun

The Confederate 32-pounder opens from a protected railroad car before infantry and field artillery advance.

Lee briefs Magruder in person

The episode explains that Magruder nevertheless misunderstands the intended relationship among the Confederate columns.

Magruder reaches Sumner at Allen’s Farm

Limited skirmishing leads Magruder to overestimate the threat in his front.

Magruder’s delaying role is explained

Lee expected him to force the Federal rear guard to stop while other Confederate columns moved toward Glendale.

Huger recalls the temporary brigades

The 2 p.m. deadline passes without a major engagement, and Huger resumes his assigned march.

Jackson receives a forwarded message

He interprets Lee’s contingency instructions to Stuart as a reason to remain north of the Chickahominy.

The bridge-repair delay is detailed

Work on the Grapevine and Alexander bridges proceeds slowly before Jackson sends an engineer officer to assist.

Magruder orders the advance

At about 4 p.m., he directs his commanders to attack the Federal force they encounter.

Kershaw makes contact

Kershaw’s South Carolina brigade leads the Confederate approach from the west.

Kershaw forms near the railroad

His left rests on the railroad and his right extends north of the Williamsburg Road.

The “Land Merrimac” is described

A 32-pound naval gun on a shielded railroad car supports the Confederate left.

The Confederate line expands

Semmes arrives on Kershaw’s right and extends across the Williamsburg Road.

Barksdale directs Griffith’s brigade

Dense woods disrupt its movement on the Confederate right, and only part of the brigade reaches the Federal line.

Brooks’s brigade returns

Sumner recalls the nearest brigade of Smith’s division to reinforce the sector south of the road.

Brooks’s losses are counted

The episode gives nearly 450 brigade casualties and states that the 5th Vermont lost nearly half its strength.

Darkness ends the battle

The opposing lines remain in place until about 9 p.m., when the fighting subsides.

Lee rebukes Magruder

Lee writes that the pursuit has made too little progress and that further delay may allow McClellan to escape.

Confederate command failures are assessed

The episode attributes the lost opportunity to Magruder’s delay, Jackson’s failure to cross, unclear orders, and insufficient staff supervision.

The rear guard begins the night withdrawal

The troops at Savage’s Station march down the White Oak Swamp Road after the battle.

The last troops cross the swamp

The episode places completion of the crossing shortly before 10 a.m. on June 30.

Important leaders killed or wounded

Confederacy

Brig. Gen. Richard Griffith

Commander of a Mississippi brigade in Magruder’s force. Mortally wounded by artillery fire during the late-afternoon attack at Savage’s Station and died shortly afterward in Richmond. The National Park Service identifies him as mortally wounded in the battle; Library of Congress cataloging for his surviving letter gives June 30 as the date of death, while some later accounts give June 29.
