Episode 194 · September 17, 1862 · The northern Antietam battlefield near Sharpsburg, Maryland, from Pry Ford and the East Woods west across Miller’s Cornfield and the Hagerstown Turnpike into the West Woods and the ground around the Dunker Church

ANTIETAM (Part the Ninth)

At about 9 a.m. the United States held much of the ground east of the Hagerstown Turnpike, but Joseph Hooker’s wound removed the northern attack’s senior commander as Confederate reinforcements arrived. Edwin V. Sumner then pushed John Sedgwick’s division into the West Woods without reconnaissance, a skirmish line, close support, or coordination with William French. McLaws’s, Early’s, and other Confederate commands struck Sedgwick’s exposed flank while artillery fired from Hauser Ridge. The division broke after roughly twenty minutes and lost about 2,200 men by the National Park Service estimate. George S. Greene retained a small foothold near the Dunker Church, but the Federal opportunity for a decisive breakthrough on Lee’s left had passed

What happened

The battlefield at 9 a.m. is recapped

After three hours of combat and about 8,000 losses, Federal troops have cleared the ground east of the Hagerstown Turnpike.

Jackson’s line reaches the breaking point

Wrecked Confederate formations try to rally while the XII Corps advance creates a chance for a Federal breakthrough.

Hood sends an urgent warning

Stephen D. Lee carries Hood’s message that the Confederate left may collapse unless Robert E. Lee sends reinforcements.

Lee builds a fallback position

Lee plans an artillery line on Hauser Ridge and points to McLaws’s approaching division as support for Jackson.

Hooker sees victory within reach

With Greene near the church and the Confederate front disordered, Hooker believes Lee’s army can be driven toward the Potomac.

Hooker is shot in the foot

A Confederate bullet strikes Hooker while he rides his white horse near the front and personally places troops and guns.

Hooker gives a final order

As he leaves the field, Hooker tells the XII Corps troops to carry and hold the woods.

Williams asks headquarters for help

The acting XII Corps commander reports Mansfield and Hooker wounded, Sumner approaching, and the field still in Federal hands.

McClellan finally releases II Corps

At 7:20 a.m. McClellan orders Sumner to support the northern attack but initially holds one division east of the creek.

Morell’s relief march consumes ninety minutes

The episode faults the V Corps division for moving about one mile without the urgency needed to free Sumner’s third division.

Sedgwick reaches the East Woods

A lull has settled over the brightening northern field when Sumner arrives with his leading division.

Williams briefs Sumner

Williams explains that I Corps is spent, XII Corps formations are scattered, and Greene needs ammunition near the church.

Williams later condemns the method

The XII Corps commander criticizes generals who attacked without consulting officers who knew the ground or reconnoitering first.

A warning cannot find Sumner

Army headquarters cautions Sumner to advance carefully, but the courier fails to locate him before the attack.

Sumner fixes on a sweeping maneuver

He plans to move west through the woods, wheel left, and roll the Confederate line south toward Sharpsburg.

Sedgwick’s left flank is offered to Jackson

The division advances straight across the field without the reconnaissance and support that might have exposed the danger.

Fresh Confederates enter the West Woods

George T. Anderson’s brigade and three brigades under McLaws add roughly 3,000 men to Jackson’s battered defense.

Sumner orders the advance

Shortly after 9 a.m., he personally leads Sedgwick’s roughly 5,400 men west and does not wait for French.

French reaches an empty rendezvous

With no staff officer left to guide him, French turns away from Sedgwick’s route and heads toward a different fight.

Roland Bowen describes the approach

The 15th Massachusetts soldier recalls wading the creek, crossing ground covered with casualties, and entering the woods under shellfire.

“We are flanked”

Bowen sees the Confederate line falling back, then realizes enemy troops have reached the division’s flank and the Federal formation dissolves.

William Andrews enters the fight

The 1st Georgia soldier recalls vaulting a fence into close-range fire and joining the Confederate charge.

Hauser Ridge guns find a dense target

Confederate artillery fires on three Federal brigade lines compressed within a few dozen yards of each other.

Sedgwick crosses the trampled Cornfield

The division moves past wounded men, crosses the turnpike and clover field, and enters the West Woods.

Gorman reaches the far side of the woods

The leading brigade engages Grigsby’s Confederates near the Alfred Poffenberger farm while Dana and Howard halt close behind.

The 125th Pennsylvania is hit

Fire reaches Jacob Higgins’s isolated regiment near the Dunker Church from both front and flank.

Kershaw pursues toward the East Woods

South Carolinians follow retreating Federals until Union canister breaks the pursuit near the batteries.

Jackson turns on Sedgwick’s flank

Early, Barksdale, and Semmes spearhead the attack against a formation that has almost no room to face south.

Sumner tries to reverse the disaster

His first signals are mistaken for an order to charge before he gets close enough to tell the troops to move back.

Friendly fire strikes the 15th Massachusetts

The 59th New York overlaps the line ahead and fires into fellow soldiers until Sumner forces the regiment to stop.

Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr. is wounded

The future justice is hit in the neck while the 15th and 20th Massachusetts suffer heavily in the collapse.

Sedgwick is hit three times

The division commander is carried from the field as all three brigade lines break toward the north.

The twenty-minute cost is counted

The episode places more than 2,300 Federal casualties in Sedgwick’s division and the 125th Pennsylvania, against fewer than 1,000 Confederate losses in Jackson’s immediate counterstroke.

Sumner signals for reinforcements

He warns McClellan that the troops are giving way and asks headquarters to locate French’s missing division.

Norwood Hallowell reaches the Nicodemus house

Wounded men crowd the farmhouse while the ground outside shifts between Federal and Confederate control.

Clara Barton treats the wounded

Barton recalls a bullet passing through a soldier she is helping and removing another man’s lodged ball with a pocketknife.

Greene defeats Walker’s second brigade

Resupplied Federal infantry drives off the attack and gains a small lodgment behind the Dunker Church.

“Things look blue”

A staff officer’s brief report captures the Federal situation after four hours of fighting without a decisive result.

The northern attacks are spent

I Corps, XII Corps, and Sedgwick’s division have all been badly damaged, with their commanders wounded or killed.

The initiative passes from McClellan

The episode concludes that piecemeal commitment and poor coordination allowed Lee to stop each Federal attack in turn.

The field guide closes the episode

The hosts recommend Carol Reardon and Tom Vossler’s stop-by-stop guide to the Antietam battlefield.

Important leaders killed or wounded

United States

Maj. Gen. Joseph Hooker

Commander of I Corps and the northern wing. Wounded in the foot while placing troops and artillery near the front; blood loss and pain forced him from the field and removed the commander intended to coordinate Sumner’s arrival.

United States

Maj. Gen. John Sedgwick

Commander of 2nd Division, II Corps. Hit three times—in the wrist, leg, and shoulder—during the West Woods repulse and carried from the field; Brig. Gen. Oliver O. Howard assumed division command.

United States

Brig. Gen. Napoleon J. T. Dana

Commander of 3rd Brigade, Sedgwick’s division. Wounded while leading the second brigade line in the West Woods. Sumner’s official report lists Dana among the brigade commanders wounded during the battle.
