Episode 194 · September 17, 1862 · The northern Antietam battlefield near Sharpsburg, Maryland, from Pry Ford and the East Woods west across Miller’s Cornfield and the Hagerstown Turnpike into the West Woods and the ground around the Dunker Church

ANTIETAM (Part the Ninth)

At about 9 a.m. the United States held much of the ground east of the Hagerstown Turnpike, but Joseph Hooker’s wound removed the northern attack’s senior commander as Confederate reinforcements arrived. Edwin V. Sumner then pushed John Sedgwick’s division into the West Woods without reconnaissance, a skirmish line, close support, or coordination with William French. McLaws’s, Early’s, and other Confederate commands struck Sedgwick’s exposed flank while artillery fired from Hauser Ridge. The division broke after roughly twenty minutes and lost about 2,200 men by the National Park Service estimate. George S. Greene retained a small foothold near the Dunker Church, but the Federal opportunity for a decisive breakthrough on Lee’s left had passed
Episode scopeAbout 9 a.m. to shortly after 10 a.m. on the northern battlefieldThe episode begins with the opening attacks nearly spent, follows Hooker’s wounding and Sedgwick’s advance into the West Woods, and closes with Greene still holding near the Dunker Church.
Cost before Sumner arrivedAbout 8,000 men already fallen in the episode’s estimateThe episode describes the losses from three hours of fighting around the Cornfield, East Woods, and Hagerstown Turnpike as divided about evenly between the armies.
Federal opportunity at 9 a.m.Confederates driven from the ground east of the turnpikeHooker and Mansfield had attacked separately, but their successive blows left Jackson’s and D. H. Hill’s formations disordered and created a short-lived chance to break Lee’s left.
Lee’s emergency responseA fallback gun line and fresh infantry for JacksonLee directed artillery toward Hauser Ridge and sent Lafayette McLaws’s division and George T. Anderson’s brigade north. The episode presents these reinforcements as arriving just before Sedgwick’s attack.
Hooker woundedShot in the foot near the frontHooker was positioning troops and batteries when a bullet struck him. He was carried from the field after ordering XII Corps troops to take and hold the woods.
Williams’s message“We hold the field at present”After taking command of XII Corps from the mortally wounded Mansfield, Alpheus Williams reported Hooker’s wound, Sumner’s approach, and the need for immediate assistance.
II Corps order time7:20 a.m.Sumner’s official report agrees with the episode that he received the order to march at 7:20. Sedgwick led, French followed, and Richardson was released later.
A division held east of the creekMcClellan initially retained one of Sumner’s three divisionsThe episode says the division could not move until George Morell’s V Corps division replaced it; Morell took about ninety minutes to cover roughly one mile.
Northern command vacuumMansfield mortally wounded and Hooker removedBy the time Sumner reached the East Woods, neither of the commanders who had directed the I and XII Corps attacks could coordinate him. Williams briefed Sumner on the scattered Federal positions.
Sumner’s conceptDrive west, then wheel south through the Confederate lineSeeing Greene near the Dunker Church, Sumner treated that advanced position as the army’s flank and planned to sweep Sedgwick through the West Woods toward Sharpsburg.
Precautions omittedNo effective reconnaissance, skirmish screen, or close supportThe episode emphasizes that Sumner advanced without establishing the Confederate position in the broken, wooded ground and did not wait for French’s division.
Confederate recoveryAbout 3,000 fresh troops added before Sedgwick arrivedThe episode says Jackson had only about 1,400 effectives immediately after Hood’s repulse, then received Anderson’s brigade and three brigades under McLaws.
Sedgwick’s strengthAbout 5,400 men in the episode; 5,300 in the NPS overviewThe small difference reflects the uncertainty common in battlefield strength estimates. Both figures describe a large division committed in a single unsupported thrust.
Attack formationThree brigade lines on a front about 500 yards wideGorman led, Dana followed, and Howard commanded the third line. The episode places the lines only about thirty yards apart; Howard’s official report gives sixty to seventy paces.
French separatedAbout twenty minutes behind SedgwickSumner left no staff officer to direct French when that division reached the East Woods. French moved south toward the Sunken Road instead of reinforcing Sedgwick.
Hauser Ridge artilleryConfederate shells struck the compact Federal formationStuart had shifted guns west of the West Woods, where they could fire over or through openings toward Sedgwick’s closely spaced brigade lines.
125th PennsylvaniaAn isolated new regiment near the Dunker ChurchJacob C. Higgins withdrew when Confederate fire reached his front and flank. He reported that remaining two minutes longer would have cost his entire command.
Friendly-fire crisis59th New York fired into the 15th MassachusettsAs the rear formations tried to face the attack, units overlapped in the smoke and woods. Sumner rode into the confusion and stopped the New Yorkers’ fire.
15th Massachusetts loss318 casualties in the episode; 330 on the NPS monument pageThe monument records 75 killed and 255 wounded among 606 present. Different historical tabulations explain why the episode and some NPS pages give nearby but nonidentical totals.
72nd Pennsylvania loss237 casualties in the episodeThe Philadelphia Brigade regiment occupied the exposed left of Howard’s line and was among the first Federal units to collapse under the flank attack.
Sedgwick’s divisionAbout 2,200 casualties in roughly twenty minutesThe National Park Service gives 2,200 losses out of 5,300 men. The episode combines Sedgwick’s division with the 125th Pennsylvania and states a total exceeding 2,300.
Senior Federal woundsHooker, Sedgwick, and Dana woundedHooker left the field with a foot wound. Sedgwick was hit three times and carried away. Dana was wounded while commanding Sedgwick’s second brigade line.
Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr.Wounded in the neck with the 20th MassachusettsThe future Supreme Court justice reached the Nicodemus farmhouse, where wounded men from the shattered division gathered as the ground changed hands.
Greene’s footholdA small Federal force behind the Dunker ChurchThe episode estimates roughly 700 men in the immediate lodgment after Greene repulsed John G. Walker’s attack. Greene’s official report gives 2,504 for his full division before its detachments and losses.
Northern battle after four hoursNo decisive Federal breakthroughI Corps could no longer attack, XII Corps was badly reduced, and Sedgwick’s II Corps division was wrecked. Greene still held advanced ground but lacked the reinforcements needed to exploit it.

Important leaders killed or wounded

United States

Maj. Gen. Joseph Hooker

Commander of I Corps and the northern wing. Wounded in the foot while placing troops and artillery near the front; blood loss and pain forced him from the field and removed the commander intended to coordinate Sumner’s arrival.

United States

Maj. Gen. John Sedgwick

Commander of 2nd Division, II Corps. Hit three times—in the wrist, leg, and shoulder—during the West Woods repulse and carried from the field; Brig. Gen. Oliver O. Howard assumed division command.

United States

Brig. Gen. Napoleon J. T. Dana

Commander of 3rd Brigade, Sedgwick’s division. Wounded while leading the second brigade line in the West Woods. Sumner’s official report lists Dana among the brigade commanders wounded during the battle.
