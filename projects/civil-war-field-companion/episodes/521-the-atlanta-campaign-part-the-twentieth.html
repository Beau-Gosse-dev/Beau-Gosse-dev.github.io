What happened

Sherman assigned each army a role

Schofield was to press the Confederate left, McPherson was to attack near Pigeon Hill and demonstrate against the mountain, and Thomas was to make the main assault.

Newton prepared to attack Cleburne

John Newton’s IV Corps division faced Patrick Cleburne’s entrenched Confederate division south of Kennesaw Mountain.

Wagner’s brigade started around 9 a.m.

George D. Wagner’s five regiments advanced about an hour after the appointed time and immediately received artillery and musket fire.

The assault reached the Confederate obstacles

Some soldiers reached the stakes and abatis but could not clear them under close-range fire. Wagner’s men made two attempts.

Kimball moved up in support

Nathan Kimball’s brigade advanced on Wagner’s left shortly after 9 a.m. Rough ground and Confederate fire also stopped this brigade.

Newton ordered a withdrawal

By 10 a.m., Wagner and Kimball were pinned down and unable to enter the works. Confederate soldiers fired heavily as the U.S. troops fell back.

Wagner and Kimball lost about 400 men

The two brigades failed to penetrate Cleburne’s line and withdrew after less than two hours of fighting.

Harker’s brigade attacked farther south

Charles G. Harker led eight regiments against the junction of Cleburne’s and Cheatham’s divisions.

Opdyke led the skirmish line

Emerson Opdyke and the 125th Ohio formed part of a strong skirmish screen ordered to clear the front and enter the Confederate works if possible.

Harker advanced around 9 a.m.

The skirmishers drove in Confederate pickets but stopped at the abatis and stakes under heavy fire. The brigade crowded behind them.

U.S. flags briefly reached the works

Accounts place the flags of the 51st and 27th Illinois on the parapet. Too few men reached the line to hold a position there.

Harker was shot near the works

Harker rode to within about 15 yards of the Confederate line. A bullet passed through his right arm and entered his body.

Luther Bradley took command

Bradley concluded the brigade could not remain under the works and ordered it back. Confederate fire caused additional losses during the retreat.

Crossfire struck Harker’s brigade

Harker attacked where Lucius Polk’s and Alfred Vaughan’s brigades joined at an angle. Fire from both directions hit the U.S. formation.

A Confederate soldier captured the 27th Illinois flag

A sergeant in the 29th Tennessee took the colors during the close fighting at the works.

Gunfire ignited dry vegetation

Muzzle blast and burning debris set fires in front of the Confederate line, trapping wounded U.S. soldiers near the works.

A temporary truce allowed wounded men to escape

Colonel William Martin displayed a white handkerchief and firing stopped locally. Soldiers from both sides helped remove U.S. wounded from the fire.

Newton’s division lost more than 650 men

The episode contrasts the U.S. loss with Johnston’s claim that Cleburne’s division suffered only about a dozen casualties.

Harker died at the field hospital

Harker died around 1 p.m. with David Stanley and John Newton present. He had received his brigadier general’s commission only 20 days earlier.
