What happened

Atlanta had become central to the 1864 campaign

The campaign in Georgia affected military planning and public opinion while the U.S. presidential election approached and fighting continued in Virginia.

George Petty warned his family about the U.S. advance

The Confederate surgeon wrote from a field hospital outside Atlanta and told his wife how to prepare if U.S. troops reached their home.

Louise Wigfall described evacuation by hospital train

Wigfall and her sister left Atlanta for Macon in a crowded train carrying sick, wounded, and dying Confederate soldiers.

Richmond ordered Johnston to transfer command

The order arrived on the night of July 17 and directed Joseph E. Johnston to turn the Army of Tennessee over to John B. Hood.

Hood asked Johnston to remain temporarily

Hood rode to Johnston’s headquarters before dawn and asked him to stay until the immediate crisis passed. Johnston refused.

Davis refused to delay the transfer

Hood, Hardee, and Stewart asked Jefferson Davis to postpone the change until Atlanta’s fate was decided. Davis kept the order in effect.

Johnston left for Macon

Johnston departed on July 18. Soldiers passing his headquarters removed their hats, and some officers left formation to shake his hand.

Hood had not commanded an army before

Hood was 33 and had a strong combat record at brigade and division level. Robert E. Lee and W. H. T. Walker questioned whether he had the qualities needed for army command.

Atlanta residents learned of the change

Samuel Richards recorded the news in his diary and expected Hood’s appointment to bring a battle before Atlanta was abandoned.

The episode reviews Hood’s military career

Hood graduated from West Point in 1853, served in the 2nd U.S. Cavalry, and rose from regimental to corps command in Confederate service.

Hood returned after two severe wounds

A wound at Gettysburg disabled his left arm. A wound at Chickamauga led to the amputation of his right leg, but he returned to field command in 1864.

Davis chose an offensive commander

Davis rejected Johnston’s continued withdrawals and selected Hood from within the army. Hardee was the only other practical internal candidate discussed in the episode.
