SEVEN DAYS' BATTLES: GAINES'S MILL (Part the Second)

By the endpoint of this installment, Porter’s reinforced V Corps still held behind Boatswain’s Swamp. D. H. Hill had checked his advance near Old Cold Harbor; A. P. Hill and Ewell had made costly, unsupported attacks; and Longstreet was preparing to turn a diversion into a full assault. The final Confederate breakthrough and Federal retreat occur in the next episode; the complete Battle of Gaines’s Mill was a Confederate victory

Confederacy

Army of Northern VirginiaGen. Robert E. LeeRevised a plan built around Powhite Creek and tried to bring separated divisions into a general assault
Light Division in the Confederate centerMaj. Gen. A. P. HillBrigades of Gregg, Branch, Anderson, Archer, Pender, and Field attacked in the central action
Left wing approaching Old Cold HarborMaj. Gen. Thomas J. “Stonewall” JacksonHis column lost time to an unclear route and arrived too late to coordinate the afternoon attacks described here
Division on the Confederate leftMaj. Gen. D. H. HillMet Sykes, withdrew after an artillery exchange, and waited for Jackson without promptly informing Lee
Division redirected into A. P. Hill’s sectorMaj. Gen. Richard S. EwellBrigades of Elzey, Trimble, and Taylor—commanded that day by Seymour—entered separately
Division on the Confederate rightMaj. Gen. James LongstreetFirst ordered to demonstrate, then told to prepare a full assault
