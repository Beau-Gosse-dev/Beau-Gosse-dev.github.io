Episode 160 · June 27, 1862, approximately 10 a.m.–5 p.m. · Hanover County, Virginia, north of the Chickahominy River: Walnut Church, Powhite Creek, New and Old Cold Harbor, Boatswain’s Swamp, and Porter’s line on the Turkey Hill plateau

SEVEN DAYS' BATTLES: GAINES'S MILL (Part the Second)

By the endpoint of this installment, Porter’s reinforced V Corps still held behind Boatswain’s Swamp. D. H. Hill had checked his advance near Old Cold Harbor; A. P. Hill and Ewell had made costly, unsupported attacks; and Longstreet was preparing to turn a diversion into a full assault. The final Confederate breakthrough and Federal retreat occur in the next episode; the complete Battle of Gaines’s Mill was a Confederate victory
Episode scopeThe approach march and failed Confederate attacks before the final evening assaultThe decisive breakthrough is outside this installment.
Battle and campaignGaines’s Mill, the third of the Seven Days BattlesThe fighting occurred on Friday, June 27, 1862, and is also called First Cold Harbor. The 1864 Battle of Cold Harbor was a separate engagement.
Lee’s mistaken premisePorter would defend Powhite Creek and the route to White House LandingMcClellan had decided to abandon that supply route. Porter instead occupied stronger ground about half a mile east, behind Boatswain’s Swamp.
Planned hammer and anvilA. P. Hill and Longstreet drive east while Jackson and D. H. Hill turn the Federal rightThe concept became obsolete when Confederate troops found Porter facing west and north behind Boatswain’s Swamp.
Morning movementsLee’s army advanced east on four roads north of the ChickahominyThe episode follows Jackson and D. H. Hill toward Old Cold Harbor, A. P. Hill toward Walnut Church and New Cold Harbor, and Longstreet toward River Road. Map routes are generalized.
Lee and Jackson conferApproximately 10–11 a.m. near Walnut ChurchJackson’s guns had mistakenly fired toward A. P. Hill’s troops nearby. Their staffs did not hear the later conversation; the episode’s claim that Lee restated Jackson’s role is an inference.
D. H. Hill meets SykesA substantial Federal line blocks the approach south of Old Cold HarborAfter a sharp and unfavorable artillery exchange, Hill fell back, deployed near the crossroads, and waited for Jackson.
Information failureLee did not promptly learn of D. H. Hill’s contactThe episode cites an acoustic shadow and notes that Hill sent no messenger. The atmospheric explanation is the podcast’s interpretation; the absence of a timely report is the direct command problem.
Gregg finds the actual lineAcross Powhite Creek, through New Cold Harbor, and down toward Boatswain’s SwampGregg pushed aside skirmishers near Gaines’s Mill, then met heavy artillery fire farther east and reported the previously unknown defensive line.
Porter requests SlocumAbout 2 p.m.McClellan had recalled Slocum because he feared an attack south of the river. After Porter’s request, Slocum was again sent north.
Strengths in the episodeAbout 55,000–60,000 Confederates; Porter rose from roughly 27,000 to 34,000–35,000The episode uses nearly 55,000 Confederates and about 35,000 Federals after Slocum’s 8,500 arrived. The National Park Service uses approximately 60,000 and 34,000. Totals vary by time and counting method.
Confederate artilleryBatteries entered separately and were generally overmatchedThe piecemeal guns could not sustain a concentration against Porter’s abundant artillery and strong fields of fire.
A. P. Hill attacksAbout 2:30 p.m. across more than a mile of Boatswain’s SwampPrecise timing and individual brigade locations are disputed. The episode emphasizes that Hill attacked without the support Lee expected.
Gregg and BranchTheir brigades gained no lasting breach in the Federal centerThe episode gives 315 casualties for the 1st South Carolina Rifles. Branch sent the 7th and 37th North Carolina first, then his other regiments. Col. Reuben P. Campbell was killed carrying the 7th’s colors.
Anderson and ArcherRepeated frontal efforts failed at the creek and slopeAnderson attacked without artillery support, aided by two of Field’s regiments. Archer crossed exposed ground and neared the Federal line before withdrawing; one Alabama regiment lost its colors.
Pender’s brief penetrationThe 16th and 22nd North Carolina temporarily entered the Federal lineFederal reinforcements drove them back. Pender’s men rallied in the swamp but could not renew the advance successfully.
Field ends Hill’s first effortRepulsed north of the Watt House after nearly two hours of attacksThe brigade’s retreat closed A. P. Hill’s opening assault.
Jackson’s wrong turnA misunderstood route forced a countermarch toward Old Cold HarborJackson had not clearly told his guide that he intended to pass around Gaines’s Mill. The episode estimates that the error left him about ninety minutes late; exact timing is uncertain.
Longstreet’s changing ordersFirst a diversion, then preparation for a full assaultFour brigades crossed Powhite Creek as a demonstration. Lee soon asked for more, and Longstreet brought Anderson’s and Kemper’s brigades forward.
Ewell redirectedLee sent Ewell’s three brigades toward A. P. Hill’s fight at about 3:30 p.m.Elzey’s, Trimble’s, and Richard Taylor’s brigades entered separately. Taylor was ill, so Col. Isaac G. Seymour commanded his brigade.
Ewell’s attack failsSeymour and Roberdeau Wheat were killed while leading the LouisianansThe episode calls Wheat’s wound mortal; other accounts state that he died on the field. A bullet struck Ewell’s boot, but he was not reported wounded. The attack stalled by about 5 p.m.
Porter’s changing reports“All was well” shortly after 4 p.m.; urgent need for reinforcements at 5 p.m.By 5 p.m. Porter saw Longstreet and more of Jackson’s command arriving while his tired troops had expended substantial ammunition.
Historical outcome beyond the episodeConfederate victory after a coordinated assault near sunsetThe National Park Service summarizes the complete battle as a Confederate breakthrough followed by Porter’s withdrawal that night. Episode 161 narrates that climax.
Recommended bookThe Seven Days: The Emergence of Lee by Clifford DowdeyThe official podcast page confirms the title and author. The automated transcript renders the surname as “Dowdy.”

What happened

Episode 160 begins

The hosts resume the Seven Days after Porter’s withdrawal from Beaver Dam Creek.

Confederates begin the pursuit

Lee’s columns find the Beaver Dam Creek line empty and move east after V Corps.

Lee’s plan is already wrong

He assumes McClellan will protect the York River supply route and Porter will defend Powhite Creek.

Porter occupies Boatswain’s Swamp

The Federal line lies east of Powhite Creek on high ground screened by a watercourse outside Lee’s expected scheme.

Four Confederate columns move east

The episode lays out the broad routes for Jackson, D. H. Hill, A. P. Hill, and Longstreet.

Jackson’s guns fire on A. P. Hill

Confederate artillery near Walnut Church mistakes A. P. Hill’s advancing column for the enemy.

Lee, Jackson, and A. P. Hill meet

Hill leaves after a short exchange; Lee and Jackson confer until about 11 a.m.

D. H. Hill finds the Federal right

South of Old Cold Harbor, Hill unexpectedly meets Sykes’s division and strong artillery.

Hill waits without informing Lee

D. H. Hill falls back toward Old Cold Harbor and awaits Jackson; Lee receives no prompt report.

Gregg crosses at Gaines’s Mill

Federal skirmishers yield Powhite Creek, and Gregg continues toward New Cold Harbor.

Boatswain’s Swamp stops Gregg

Heavy artillery fire checks the brigade and reveals Porter’s actual center.

Porter asks for Slocum

At about 2 p.m., Porter requests the division McClellan had earlier ordered north and then recalled.

Lee commits to the new battlefield

Lee moves forward and decides to break Porter’s unexpected position.

The artillery remains dispersed

Confederate batteries enter separately instead of massing against Porter’s guns.

A. P. Hill’s assault begins

The episode places the start at about 2:30 p.m. while acknowledging uncertainty over timing and locations.

Gregg gains a foothold

The South Carolinians enter the swamp but cannot advance through Federal fire.

Branch attacks in sections

The 7th and 37th North Carolina go first; the other regiments follow after they retreat.

Anderson attacks without artillery

Anderson and two of Field’s regiments make repeated efforts that do not cross the defended stream.

Archer crosses open ground

Archer’s brigade advances about 500 yards, approaches the Federal position, and breaks under heavy fire.

Pender commits the reserve

Pender attacks after Branch and briefly gets two regiments into the Federal line.

Field makes the last effort

Field attacks north of the Watt House and is repulsed, ending A. P. Hill’s first major assault.

Jackson’s guide takes the direct road

Because the flanking purpose was not explained, the column heads toward Gaines’s Mill rather than around it.

Jackson orders a countermarch

The column retraces part of its route while A. P. Hill fights without expected support.

Lee continues the pressure

Lee sends for Jackson and Longstreet and commits to taking the plateau.

Longstreet begins a diversion

Four brigades cross Powhite Creek and engage opposite Butterfield and Morell.

The diversion becomes an assault order

Lee asks for a stronger effort, and Longstreet brings all six brigades forward.

Jackson reaches Old Cold Harbor

The episode places his arrival at about 3 p.m., ahead of Whiting and Winder and out of contact with Ewell.

Lee and Jackson send messengers

Lee’s aide looks for Jackson while Jackson’s engineer heads toward Lee, illustrating the fragmented information flow.

Lee redirects Ewell

At about 3:30 p.m., Lee orders Ewell’s brigades to assist A. P. Hill.

Seymour’s Louisianans attack

The brigade advances into intense fire; Seymour and Roberdeau Wheat are killed.

Ewell’s effort stalls

By about 5 p.m., his brigades fall back among A. P. Hill’s formations.

Porter reports that all is well

Shortly after 4 p.m., Slocum’s arrival gives Porter confidence that he can keep holding.

Porter urgently asks for help

At 5 p.m., Confederate strength is building, the Federal troops are tired, and ammunition is running low.

The book recommendation

The hosts recommend Clifford Dowdey’s The Seven Days: The Emergence of Lee.
