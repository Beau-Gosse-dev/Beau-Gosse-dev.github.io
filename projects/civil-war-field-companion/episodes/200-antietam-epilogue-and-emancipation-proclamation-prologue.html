ANTIETAM (Epilogue) & EMANCIPATION PROCLAMATION (Prologue)

Lee’s withdrawal ended the Maryland Campaign without destroying either army. The United States treated Antietam as a strategic victory because the Confederate invasion had failed. The result weakened prospects for immediate European mediation and gave Abraham Lincoln the military occasion he had awaited to issue the Preliminary Emancipation Proclamation on September 22. McClellan then delayed a new advance until late October; Lincoln relieved him on November 5 and replaced him with Ambrose Burnside

Important leaders killed or wounded

United States

Maj. Gen. Joseph K. F. Mansfield

XII Corps commander. Mortally wounded near the East Woods on September 17 and died the next day.

United States

Maj. Gen. Israel B. Richardson

1st Division, II Corps commander. Mortally wounded after the Sunken Road breakthrough and died at the Pry House on November 3.

United States

Brig. Gen. Isaac P. Rodman

3rd Division, IX Corps commander. Mortally wounded while trying to meet A. P. Hill’s attack and died on September 30.

Confederacy

Brig. Gen. William E. Starke

Stonewall Division commander. Mortally wounded while leading a morning counterattack and died within the hour.

Confederacy

Brig. Gen. George B. Anderson

North Carolina brigade commander. Mortally wounded at the Sunken Road and died on October 16 after his infected foot was amputated.

Confederacy

Brig. Gen. Lawrence O’Bryan Branch

North Carolina brigade commander, Light Division. Killed while leading his brigade during A. P. Hill’s late-afternoon counterattack.
