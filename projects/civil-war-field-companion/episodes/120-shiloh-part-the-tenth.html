Episode 120 · April 6, 1862, about 1:30 p.m. through late afternoon · The eastern and central Shiloh battlefield: Stuart’s ravine, MacArthur’s position, Bell Field, the peach orchard, and the Hamburg–Savannah Road, Tennessee

SHILOH (Part the Tenth)

Johnston concentrated Statham, Bowen, and Jackson against MacArthur and Hurlbut at about 1:30 p.m. Stuart’s isolated brigade withdrew through a ravine under fire, exposing MacArthur’s flank; MacArthur then retreated. Additional Confederate brigades attacked Prentiss while Johnston personally led Statham forward. Hurlbut abandoned the peach orchard but formed another line immediately north of it. Johnston died at about 2:30 p.m.; Beauregard assumed army command from the rear and Bragg took direct control along the Hamburg–Savannah Road. The United States line continued to hold temporarily, while renewed fighting also pushed Sherman toward another withdrawal on the west

What happened

The western and central fronts were temporarily stable

Sherman and McClernand paused near Jones Field, while Wallace and Prentiss had repulsed attacks at the Hornet’s Nest.

Stuart remained isolated on the far left

About 800 men had no artillery, little ammunition, a deep ravine behind them, and no direct contact with MacArthur.

Johnston assembled fresh brigades

Statham’s six regiments deployed west of the Hamburg–Savannah Road and Bowen’s four regiments east of it; Jackson was redirected to support Bowen.

The attack opened at about 1:30 p.m.

Bowen and Jackson advanced against MacArthur while Statham moved toward Hurlbut in the peach orchard.

Johnston moved Bowen forward

When Bowen stopped at a shallow ravine under heavy fire, Johnston rode along the line and urged the men to resume their advance.

Hurlbut and MacArthur ran low on ammunition

The 31st Illinois was ordered to stop firing and prepare to use bayonets; Stuart’s men were searching casualties for cartridges.

Stuart ordered a withdrawal

Confederates fired down the ravine and from its rim, turning the attempted orderly movement into a rout.

MacArthur’s flank was exposed

Jackson struck the 50th Illinois from the side while Bowen pressed from the front. The 50th, 9th, and 12th Illinois withdrew in succession.

Hurlbut shifted troops toward his open left

With Stuart and MacArthur gone, Hurlbut reinforced the threatened end of his line and continued defending the peach orchard.

More Confederate brigades entered the attack

Zachariah Deas led Gladden’s former brigade and George Maney led Stephens’s former brigade against Prentiss.

The 45th Tennessee refused another advance

The regiment had been stopped by the 41st Illinois. Breckinridge and Tennessee governor Isham Harris could not immediately get it moving.

Johnston personally led Statham forward

His intervention produced a coordinated multi-brigade attack that continued through heavy infantry and artillery fire.

Hurlbut abandoned the peach orchard

The United States infantry withdrew to the woods north of the orchard and stopped the Confederates again with heavy fire.

Johnston died as the advance paused

At about 2:30 p.m., Johnston died in a ravine south of Bell Field after bleeding from the leg wound described in episode 117.

Beauregard assumed army command

Isham Harris delivered the news. Beauregard remained at the rear headquarters, while Bragg moved to direct the fighting along the Hamburg–Savannah Road.

The Confederate left renewed its attack

Cleburne’s damaged brigade and Preston Pond’s nearly fresh Louisiana brigade attacked Sherman and McClernand, prompting another planned withdrawal.

Pressure shifted toward the center

Patton Anderson attacked across Duncan Field. The assault failed but began the final sequence against the Hornet’s Nest.
