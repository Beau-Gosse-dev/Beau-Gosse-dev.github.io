Episode 185 · September 14, 1862 · Fox’s Gap, Turner’s Gap, and the high ground toward Frosttown Gap on South Mountain between Middletown and Boonsboro, Maryland

SOUTH MOUNTAIN (Part the Third)

United States tactical victory on the northern South Mountain front. IX Corps held Fox’s Gap, and I Corps drove back the Confederate left north of Turner’s Gap. Confederate troops still occupied Turner’s Gap at dark, but both flanks had been turned and Robert E. Lee ordered the mountain line abandoned during the night

People and command

United States

Army of the PotomacMaj. Gen. George B. McClellanDirected the army’s advance across South Mountain; observed the northern battle from high ground near Bolivar and reported the victory to Henry W. Halleck that night
Right Wing, Army of the PotomacMaj. Gen. Ambrose E. BurnsideCoordinated Joseph Hooker’s I Corps and Jesse Reno’s IX Corps in the double envelopment of the Turner’s Gap position
IX CorpsMaj. Gen. Jesse L. RenoJacob D. Cox’s division held the morning gains; Orlando B. Willcox’s and Samuel D. Sturgis’s divisions renewed the attack at Fox’s Gap. Reno was mortally wounded at dusk
I CorpsMaj. Gen. Joseph HookerGeorge G. Meade’s division attacked on the right, John P. Hatch’s on the left, and James B. Ricketts’s began in reserve north of the National Road
Pennsylvania Reserves Division, I CorpsBrig. Gen. George G. MeadeTruman Seymour, Albert L. Magilton, and Thomas F. Gallagher led its three brigades in the successful attack on Rodes’s exposed line
1st Division, I CorpsBrig. Gen. John P. HatchAdvanced on the spur nearer the National Road; after Hatch was wounded, Brig. Gen. Abner Doubleday assumed command

Confederacy

Army of Northern VirginiaGen. Robert E. LeeChose to contest South Mountain to protect the Harpers Ferry operation; remained near the western foot of the mountain and ordered withdrawal after dark
Right Wing, Army of Northern VirginiaMaj. Gen. James LongstreetMarched reinforcements from Hagerstown; took battlefield command by seniority near 5 p.m. and warned Lee to prepare for retreat
Hill’s Division and initial command at Fox’s and Turner’s GapsMaj. Gen. D. H. HillPlaced Robert Rodes north of the National Road and assigned Roswell Ripley to coordinate the intended four-brigade counterattack at Fox’s Gap
Rodes’s Brigade, Hill’s DivisionBrig. Gen. Robert E. RodesAbout 1,200 men from five Alabama regiments defended the long, broken front north of Turner’s Gap; Col. John B. Gordon commanded the 6th Alabama
Drayton’s Brigade, David R. Jones’s DivisionBrig. Gen. Thomas F. DraytonHeld an exposed position at Fox’s Gap after the larger counterattack failed to form; the brigade lost about half its strength
Hood’s Division, Longstreet’s WingBrig. Gen. John Bell HoodReturned to command when Lee suspended his arrest and led two veteran brigades toward the final fighting at Fox’s Gap
