STALEMATE ALONG THE RAPPAHANNOCK

Indecisive along the Rappahannock. Pope escaped Lee’s planned turning movement at the Rapidan and held the north and east bank of the Rappahannock. Confederate probes did not establish a permanent crossing, but Stuart’s raid captured intelligence that confirmed Pope’s dispositions and the approaching Federal reinforcements, helping Lee plan the next phase of the Northern Virginia Campaign

What happened

The road to Second Manassas is reviewed

The episode places the Rappahannock operations between Cedar Mountain and the two-day battle at Second Manassas.

The strategic setting after the Seven Days

McClellan remains at Harrison’s Landing while Pope’s new Army of Virginia operates in northern Virginia.

Lee faces two Federal armies

Lee cannot ignore McClellan near Richmond, but Pope’s movement against Confederate rail lines requires a response.

Cedar Mountain changes the local balance

A. P. Hill’s arrival lets Jackson defeat Banks on August 9, but Jackson retires when more of Pope’s army approaches.

The Peninsula withdrawal opens Lee’s opportunity

Halleck’s order to remove the Army of the Potomac frees Lee to reinforce Jackson and move north against Pope.

Army of the Potomac units leave slowly

McClellan receives the withdrawal order on August 3; the first units leave on August 14, and McClellan departs on August 23.

Lee identifies a window against Pope

Reports that McClellan is withdrawing lead Lee to send Longstreet’s ten brigades toward Jackson on August 13.

Lee joins the concentration

Lee arrives near Gordonsville on August 15 and assumes direct control over Longstreet and Jackson.

Lee selects a movement around Pope’s left

The plan calls for an east-northeast march across the Rapidan to sever Pope’s connection with Washington.

The Confederate advance is delayed

Difficulties with the cavalry postpone the movement, first intended to support an August 18 attack.

Federal cavalry surprises Stuart

Stuart escapes, but the patrol captures his adjutant and a copy of Lee’s orders for the movement against Pope.

Pope orders an immediate retreat

After informing Halleck, Pope prepares to withdraw northeast behind the Rappahannock and receives orders to hold that line.

Campfires conceal the night march

Pope’s men maintain their normal routine, then quietly leave before midnight on August 18.

The Army of Virginia reaches safety

Despite confusion and delays, Pope’s full command is behind the Rappahannock by noon on August 20.

Lee discovers the withdrawal late

Observers on Clark’s Mountain identify Federal wagon trains and dust columns around midday on August 19.

Cavalry contests the approaches

On August 20 Confederate cavalry pushes toward the river crossings and meets Federal screening forces, including at Brandy Station.

The Confederate wings approach the river

Longstreet heads for Kelly’s Ford while Jackson moves toward Rappahannock Station; heat and dust slow Jackson’s column.

The defended crossings block Lee

On August 21 Lee finds Federal troops strongly posted at the limited crossing points along the river.

Jackson shifts north from Freeman’s Ford

After an artillery duel ends around 3 p.m., Jackson moves toward White Sulphur Springs.

Bohlen crosses to investigate

Sigel sends three regiments across Freeman’s Ford after the Confederate batteries become silent.

Trimble attacks the Federal detachment

Trimble’s concealed brigade strikes Bohlen’s regiments, and Hood’s arriving troops add to the pressure.

Bohlen is killed in the rout

Bohlen is shot through the heart as his force breaks toward the ford; soldiers are shot or drown during the recrossing.

Jackson reaches White Sulphur Springs

Jackson finds the ford lightly guarded five miles upstream and prepares Ewell’s division to secure it.

The 13th Georgia crosses under rain

Supported by two batteries, the regiment wades across and captures the Federal mounted sentries.

Early crosses an old dam

Early’s brigade uses a crossing about a mile downstream to turn the small Federal position.

The river rises behind Ewell’s men

A severe downpour turns the Rappahannock into a torrent and isolates the Confederate advance on the Federal bank.

Pope weighs a counterstroke

Pope considers concentrating against Jackson but recognizes that Longstreet could threaten his rear if he moves north too soon.

Halleck authorizes an attack on Jackson

The planned support includes approaching formations from Heintzelman’s III Corps and Porter’s V Corps.

Floodwater closes every ford

Pope cannot cross to attack Jackson’s rear, but he recognizes that the same water has isolated Ewell’s bridgehead.

Pope marches toward the bridgehead

Early on August 23 the Army of Virginia turns north while Jackson orders engineers to rebuild the destroyed bridge.

Confederate guns cover Early

Jackson masses artillery on the west bank; Jackson and A. P. Hill cross the floodwater to help arrange Early’s defense.

Rain delays the Federal vanguard

Milroy’s brigade and part of Buford’s cavalry arrive too late in the day to mount an attack.

Jackson reinforces instead of withdrawing

After engineers finish the bridge, Jackson sends the remainder of Lawton’s brigade across after dark.

Ewell orders the bridgehead evacuated

After inspecting Early’s exhausted troops and the Federal force to their front, Ewell withdraws Early and Lawton before dawn on August 24.

Hartsuff holds the railroad bridgehead

At Rappahannock Station, Hartsuff’s brigade occupies the Confederate side so Pope can retain the railroad bridge.

A major artillery duel begins

Nearly 50 Confederate guns open on the morning of August 23 and Federal batteries answer from across the river.

Hartsuff withdraws before the infantry attack

Longstreet’s soldiers find the south-bank position abandoned but are stopped at the bridge by Federal cannon fire.

The railroad bridge is burned

Tower’s rear guard sets the span on fire after artillery fails to demolish it; the bridge collapses around noon.

Stuart proposes a raid on Pope’s rear

The target is the railroad bridge over Cedar Run near Catlett Station, whose destruction could cut Pope’s supply line.

Stuart rides with 1,500 cavalrymen

Lee approves the operation on August 22, and Stuart crosses the river and reaches Warrenton without opposition.

A downpour covers the approach to Catlett

Rain slows the raid but helps the lead Confederates seize Federal pickets and locate Pope’s headquarters baggage.

Stuart divides the raiding force

The 1st and 11th Virginia Cavalry move against the station, the 9th Virginia targets Pope’s baggage, and another detachment tries to burn the bridge.

Pope’s dispatch book is taken

The 9th Virginia captures the headquarters baggage, including Pope’s dress uniform and papers.

Wet timbers defeat the raid’s main objective

The Confederates cannot burn the Cedar Run railroad bridge, but the captured dispatch book proves more valuable than expected.

The papers disclose Pope’s dispositions

Marching orders, strength returns, unit locations, and information about Army of the Potomac reinforcements confirm Lee’s estimates.

Lee’s opportunity is measured in days

The intelligence underscores that Lee must force a major battle before the two Federal armies combine.

The episode’s book recommendation

The hosts recommend David G. Martin’s “The Second Bull Run Campaign: July–August 1862.”
