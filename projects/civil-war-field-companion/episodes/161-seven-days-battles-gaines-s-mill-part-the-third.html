Episode 161 · June 27, 1862 · Gaines’s Mill battlefield north of the Chickahominy River, near Mechanicsville and Richmond in Hanover County, Virginia

SEVEN DAYS' BATTLES: GAINES'S MILL (Part the Third)

Confederate victory. A late assault by Robert E. Lee’s army broke Fitz John Porter’s reinforced V Corps line behind Boatswain’s Swamp. Porter’s survivors withdrew across the Chickahominy after dark. The victory cost Lee about 8,750 casualties and came too late in the day to destroy Porter’s command

Figures reported in this guide
ScopeReported figure
Final attack strength in the episodeAbout 32,000 Confederates against roughly 32,000–34,000 United States troopsThese figures describe forces available along the late-day front, not total forces present during the whole battle. The National Park Service lists 34,214 United States and 57,018 Confederate personnel engaged by its broader accounting.
20th North Carolina losses272 casualties in the episode’s accountThe regiment, part of Samuel Garland’s brigade, was in its first battle. The episode gives the loss as about 40 percent of its strength.
Lawton’s brigadeAbout 3,600 men in its first combatLawton’s large Georgia brigade crossed the swamp after a prolonged struggle, gained the Federal side, and paused to restore alignment in smoke and fading light.
Federal capturesNearly 1,000 men from two regiments of George W. Taylor’s brigade were capturedOther pockets of United States troops were also cut off during the retreat, especially near the breakthrough on the Federal left.
Battle casualtiesUnited States 6,837; Confederacy 8,750These are the National Park Service battle-summary figures. The episode rounds Porter’s loss to nearly 7,000 and Richmond National Battlefield Park rounds the Confederate loss to approximately 9,000. Counts vary by source and method.

Important leaders killed or wounded

Confederacy

Col. John Marshall

Commander, 4th Texas Infantry. Killed by a long-range shot during the approach to Boatswain’s Swamp. The National Park Service trail guide places the event about 550 yards north of the creek.

United States

Col. Jesse A. Gove

Commander, 22nd Massachusetts Infantry. Killed while his regiment fought in Martindale’s brigade. His body was not recovered by the regiment after the Federal retreat.
