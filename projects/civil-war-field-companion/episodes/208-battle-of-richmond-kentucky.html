BATTLE OF RICHMOND (KENTUCKY)

Decisive Confederate victory. Edmund Kirby Smith’s veterans broke three United States positions on August 30, and John S. Scott’s cavalry blocked the road north, turning a retreat into a mass capture. The victory opened Lexington and Frankfort to Smith, but it did not produce the large, immediate recruiting surge Confederate leaders expected or decide the wider Kentucky campaign

People and command

United States — Department and Army of Kentucky

Department of the OhioMaj. Gen. Horatio G. WrightDirected the emergency defense of the region and sent newly raised troops toward Lexington.
Army of KentuckyMaj. Gen. William “Bull” NelsonSuperseded Lew Wallace at Lexington, dispersed the available force south of the Kentucky River, and took command of the final position at Richmond.
1st Brigade; field commander at RichmondBrig. Gen. Mahlon D. Manson16th, 55th, 69th, and 71st Indiana Infantry with artillery; held the initial line near Mount Zion and commanded east of the road at the second line.
2nd BrigadeBrig. Gen. Charles Cruft12th and 66th Indiana, 18th Kentucky, and 95th Ohio Infantry with artillery; reinforced Manson and held west of the road at the second line.
United States cavalry detachmentLt. Col. Reuben MundayObserved the Confederate concentration around Big Hill and was driven north during the August 29 approach.

Confederacy — Army of Kentucky

Army of KentuckyMaj. Gen. Edmund Kirby SmithConcentrated Cleburne and Churchill against Richmond, directed the three-stage attack, and sent Scott around the United States rear.
Cleburne’s divisionBrig. Gen. Patrick R. CleburneLed the advance and the attack east of the road; Col. Benjamin J. Hill and Col. Preston Smith commanded his brigades. Preston Smith took divisional command after Cleburne was wounded.
Churchill’s divisionBrig. Gen. Thomas J. ChurchillReinforced Cleburne and attacked west of the road; Col. Thomas H. McCray led the brigade that broke the United States right.
13th and 15th Arkansas InfantryCol. Lucius E. PolkManeuvered on the Confederate right and helped enfilade the United States left before being wounded.
Cavalry brigadeCol. John S. ScottScreened the advance, fought the August 23 and 29 actions, and cut the Lexington road north of Richmond during the final rout.

Important leaders killed or wounded

Confederacy

Col. Edward Fitzgerald

Commander, 154th Tennessee Infantry. Shot in the head and killed during the regiment’s advance against the first United States position near Mount Zion Church.

Confederacy

Brig. Gen. Patrick R. Cleburne

Division commander. Wounded through the left cheek and mouth during the pursuit from the first position; survived and returned to command.

Confederacy

Col. Lucius E. Polk

Commander, 13th and 15th Arkansas Infantry. Wounded during the first-stage fighting after his troops helped turn the United States left; survived.

United States

Maj. Gen. William “Bull” Nelson

Commander, Army of Kentucky. Struck in the leg by two spent balls during the final stand and escaped. On September 29, 1862, Brig. Gen. Jefferson C. Davis fatally shot him in an unrelated confrontation at Louisville’s Galt House.
