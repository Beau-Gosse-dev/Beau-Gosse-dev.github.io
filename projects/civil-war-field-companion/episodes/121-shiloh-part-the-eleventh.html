Episode 121 · April 6, 1862, about 4 p.m. to 5:30 p.m.; W.H.L. Wallace died April 10 · Duncan Field, the Hornet’s Nest, the Corinth Road, and the final United States line above Pittsburg Landing, Tennessee

SHILOH (Part the Eleventh)

Patton Anderson’s frontal attack failed. Daniel Ruggles then assembled 11 Confederate batteries with as many as 53 guns and opened a sustained bombardment. At about 4 p.m., Sherman and McClernand withdrew from the west while Hurlbut withdrew from the east, leaving W.H.L. Wallace and Benjamin Prentiss exposed on both flanks. Wallace ordered a retreat but was shot in the head while directing it. Several regiments escaped; most of the 58th Illinois, 12th Iowa, 14th Iowa, and Prentiss’s remaining command were surrounded. Prentiss and about 2,200 United States soldiers surrendered at about 5:30 p.m. Wallace was recovered alive the next day and died on April 10
