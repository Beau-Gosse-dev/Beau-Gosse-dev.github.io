SHILOH (Part the Eleventh)

Patton Anderson’s frontal attack failed. Daniel Ruggles then assembled 11 Confederate batteries with as many as 53 guns and opened a sustained bombardment. At about 4 p.m., Sherman and McClernand withdrew from the west while Hurlbut withdrew from the east, leaving W.H.L. Wallace and Benjamin Prentiss exposed on both flanks. Wallace ordered a retreat but was shot in the head while directing it. Several regiments escaped; most of the 58th Illinois, 12th Iowa, 14th Iowa, and Prentiss’s remaining command were surrounded. Prentiss and about 2,200 United States soldiers surrendered at about 5:30 p.m. Wallace was recovered alive the next day and died on April 10

What happened

The attack shifted to the United States center

Patton Anderson took four regiments and two battalions across Duncan Field against W.H.L. Wallace’s line.

Anderson’s attack was repulsed

Wallace’s infantry waited in the thickets and along the farm lane, then stopped the advance with close-range volleys.

Beauregard assigned Ruggles to the center

Daniel Ruggles took responsibility for the Confederate attacks against Wallace and Prentiss.

Ruggles decided to mass artillery

After earlier frontal attacks failed, he sent his staff to collect batteries from across the battlefield.

The Confederate batteries assembled

The podcast describes all or parts of 12 batteries and about 60 guns; the NPS identifies 11 batteries and as many as 53 guns.

The bombardment began

The guns opened at about 4 p.m. and continued arriving until about 5 p.m. Many rounds passed over infantry sheltering close to the ground, but some defenders were killed or wounded.

Sherman and McClernand withdrew

At about 4 p.m., the two divisions moved northeast and formed along the Hamburg–Savannah Road, roughly one mile from the river.

Hurlbut withdrew from the United States left

Confederates moved between his flank and the river. His attempt to form at Cloud Field failed, and parts of the division ran to the final ridge above Pittsburg Landing.

Hurlbut rallied troops around the siege guns

Units that retained organization became rally points, and field batteries were placed beside the 24-pounder siege guns.

Prentiss’s left flank became exposed

Hurlbut’s courier was killed before delivering notice of the withdrawal. Prentiss bent his line back but did not immediately retreat.

Grant’s earlier order remained in effect

Grant had told Wallace and Prentiss to hold. Wallace now had to decide when continued resistance would result in encirclement.

Sweeney reported that the right could not hold

Only the 7th and 58th Illinois remained with his brigade; Confederates were already moving around them.

Wallace ordered a withdrawal

Artillery left first. The 7th Illinois and the 2nd and 7th Iowa moved toward the Corinth Road as Confederate forces closed in.

W.H.L. Wallace was mortally wounded

A bullet entered the back of his head and exited near the left eye. His aides left him beside the road when Confederate fire made it impossible to carry him farther.

Several regiments were trapped

Most of the 58th Illinois and the 12th and 14th Iowa were cut off north of the farm lane. Most of Prentiss’s remaining command was also surrounded.

The Hornet’s Nest surrendered

Confederates captured Prentiss and about 2,200 men from the 2nd and 6th Divisions at about 5:30 p.m.

Wallace was recovered alive

On April 7, Cyrus Dickey found Wallace still breathing. He was taken to Cherry Mansion in Savannah, where he died four days after being wounded.
