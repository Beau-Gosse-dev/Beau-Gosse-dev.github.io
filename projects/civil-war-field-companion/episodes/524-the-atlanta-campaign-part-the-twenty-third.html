What happened

The episode shifts to Mitchell’s brigade

Jefferson C. Davis sent Daniel McCook Jr. and John G. Mitchell against the Dead Angle. The previous episode covered McCook; this one follows Mitchell.

John G. Mitchell commanded the second attacking brigade

The 25-year-old Ohio lawyer had led troops at Chickamauga but had seen limited combat during the Atlanta campaign.

Mitchell formed a column of regiments

The 113th Ohio led, followed by the 121st and 98th Ohio and 78th Illinois. The 34th Illinois deployed ahead as skirmishers.

The brigade was to deploy into line at the works

Henry Banning’s 121st Ohio was to move right when the leading regiment reached the defenses. The remaining regiments would extend the line farther right.

George Maney’s brigade held the Dead Angle

Maney’s Tennessee troops occupied the tip and south face of the salient. Benjamin Cheatham ordered them to hold the works.

The attack began with artillery fire

At 8 a.m., U.S. guns opened and the 34th Illinois overran the Confederate skirmish line. The brigade moved into the stream valley below the works.

Confederate soldiers saw the column approaching

Captain W. D. Kelly of the 1st Tennessee alerted the defenders as Mitchell’s troops climbed the slope.

Abatis and enfilading artillery broke up the formation

Cut saplings disrupted the U.S. column. Eight Confederate guns south of the salient fired along the attacking line.

The leading regiments reached the works

The 34th Illinois stopped 12 to 15 yards short. Men of the 113th Ohio reached the parapet, where some were killed and others captured.

The 113th Ohio lost many officers

Lieutenant Colonel Darius B. Warner was badly wounded. Ten of the regiment’s 19 officers were killed or wounded.

The 121st Ohio moved right as ordered

Banning’s regiment cleared the 113th but suffered heavy losses from the Confederate battery.

About 180 Confederates held the tip

Maney placed the consolidated 1st and 27th Tennessee in the salient while additional regiments held the adjoining works.

Hiram Field fought from the parapet

The regimental commander fired weapons passed up from the trench until a U.S. bullet grazed his head and temporarily paralyzed his left side.

The defenders fired rapidly at close range

Sam Watkins said he fired 120 rounds and exchanged overheated muskets for weapons taken from dead comrades.

William Hughes was mortally wounded

Hughes grabbed the barrel of a U.S. soldier’s musket while protecting Watkins and was shot through the hand and arm.

The 6th and 9th Tennessee reinforced the salient

They moved through the trench and added their fire as the 1st and 27th Tennessee ran low on ammunition.

Mitchell’s assault stalled

The 98th Ohio and 78th Illinois could not complete the planned deployment. The 113th and 121st Ohio and 34th Illinois were the units that closed on the works.

The U.S. regiments fell back to dead ground

Mitchell’s men joined McCook’s survivors behind a slight rise 40 to 100 yards from the Confederate line and dug a low parapet.

Cheatham sent reinforcements too late to engage

Jesse Finley’s brigade ran about a mile but arrived after the U.S. attack had fallen back.

The closest fighting lasted about 20 minutes

Participant estimates varied, but the exposed position near the parapet made a much longer assault unlikely.

Available U.S. reserves were not committed

James D. Morgan’s brigade, Absalom Baird’s division, and nearby XX Corps divisions prepared to move but received no order to join the assault.

Earthworks allowed a smaller force to hold

McCook and Mitchell brought about 4,000 men. One Confederate estimate placed no more than 1,000 defenders in action in the attacked sector at one time.

The XIV Corps attack did not break the line

Mitchell’s and McCook’s brigades came closer to the works than other attacks but did not penetrate Cheatham’s position.
