PENINSULA CAMPAIGN (Part the Third)

The Army of the Potomac prepared a formal siege rather than forcing the Yorktown–Warwick line. The delay allowed Joseph E. Johnston to reinforce the position and organize his army, but the United States siege artillery made the line untenable. Johnston evacuated during the night of May 3–4. United States troops occupied Yorktown and Gloucester Point, captured dozens of heavy guns, and began a pursuit toward Williamsburg. The Confederates gave up the lower Peninsula but preserved their field army and gained nearly a month for the defense of Richmond. The National Park Service classifies the Yorktown operation as indecisive

United States

President and constitutional commander in chiefAbraham LincolnRequired the protection of Washington and continued to press McClellan for prompt action at Yorktown
Secretary of WarEdwin M. StantonDirected the War Department and received McClellan’s reports on the occupation and pursuit
Commander, Army of the PotomacGeorge B. McClellanDirected the siege, the May 4 occupation, the land pursuit, and the planned water movement toward West Point
Division commander and director of siege operationsFitz John PorterSupervised construction of approaches and batteries and observed the Confederate position from balloons
Division commander, formerly assigned to McDowell’s I CorpsWilliam B. FranklinReached the Peninsula on April 22 and was assigned to the water movement toward West Point after Yorktown fell
Chief of cavalry, Army of the PotomacGeorge StonemanLed the cavalry and horse artillery pursuit toward Williamsburg on May 4

Confederacy

President and commander in chiefJefferson DavisDirected national strategy and ordered Johnston to continue defending the lower Peninsula after the April 14 conference
Secretary of WarGeorge W. RandolphServed as the civilian head of the Confederate War Department and participated in the April 14 strategy conference
Military adviser to President DavisRobert E. LeeArgued that the Peninsula offered advantages to an outnumbered defender and supported holding the Yorktown line
Department of Northern Virginia and principal field army on the PeninsulaJoseph E. JohnstonAssumed overall field command, opposed a prolonged defense at Yorktown, and ordered the May 3–4 withdrawal
Senior commanders in Johnston’s retreat organizationJames Longstreet, D. H. Hill, Gustavus W. Smith, and David R. JonesLed the four principal commands moving from the Yorktown–Warwick line; Jones temporarily replaced the ill John B. Magruder
Cavalry commander and rear guardJ. E. B. StuartCovered the Confederate withdrawal and skirmished with Stoneman’s pursuit on May 4
Brigade commander at YorktownGabriel J. RainsPromoted the use of explosive “torpedoes”; mines were left around the evacuated position and caused United States casualties
