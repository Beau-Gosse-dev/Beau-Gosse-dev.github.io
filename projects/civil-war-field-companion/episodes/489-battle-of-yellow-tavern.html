What happened

Grant’s supporting operations were ending

The episode follows the Confederate victory at New Market, which released about 2,500 troops for Lee. Butler and Sheridan still threatened Richmond from opposite directions.

Sheridan received permission to seek Stuart

After arguing with George Meade about cavalry performance, Sheridan said he could defeat J. E. B. Stuart. Grant supported the proposal and Meade issued the order.

The cavalry corps departed May 9

Sheridan’s force left the Spotsylvania area at daybreak in a thirteen-mile column of about 12,000 men and 32 guns.

Stuart kept four brigades with Lee’s army

He pursued with Fitzhugh Lee’s two brigades and later James B. Gordon’s brigade. The remaining cavalry stayed near Lee in case Grant moved around another flank.

Stuart tried to catch the column at the North Anna

He sent Wickham directly behind Sheridan while taking Gordon and Lomax upstream. Sheridan guarded the crossing and moved the full column safely south of the river.

U.S. cavalry destroyed the Beaver Dam depot

The raid destroyed two locomotives, about one hundred railcars, medical stores, and roughly 1.5 million rations, and released between three hundred and four hundred U.S. prisoners.

Stuart divided his pursuit

Gordon stayed behind Sheridan while Stuart and Fitzhugh Lee moved southeast to Telegraph Road, attempting to pass the U.S. column before it reached Richmond.

The Confederate march continued through exhaustion

Stuart’s troopers halted near Taylorsville after thirty-two hours in the saddle, rested six hours, and resumed the march at 3 a.m. on May 11.

Sheridan stopped to rest at Ground Squirrel Bridge

His objective was to fight Stuart rather than win a race to Richmond. The U.S. cavalry bivouacked south of the South Anna River on May 10.

Ten thousand U.S. cavalrymen resumed the advance

Sheridan left John I. Gregg’s 2,000-man brigade to guard the rear while the main force rode south on May 11.

Gordon attacked the U.S. rear guard

Confederate cavalry crossed at a nearby ford and began a running fight. Henry Davies’s U.S. brigade also damaged the Richmond, Fredericksburg and Potomac Railroad near Ashland.

The opposing columns converged on Yellow Tavern

Sheridan used Mountain Road while Stuart came down Telegraph Road. The roads met at an abandoned inn north of Richmond.

Lomax blocked the crossroads

His brigade reached Yellow Tavern around 8 a.m. and formed along Telegraph Road. Stuart had arrived ahead of the U.S. column.

Wickham formed a second wing on the ridge

Lomax faced west near the junction while Wickham stood about half a mile north, facing south above Turner’s Run and positioned to fire into an advancing U.S. flank.

Stuart had about 3,000 troops immediately available

The odds were worse than three to one. Stuart expected the Richmond garrison to join the battle and help close Sheridan’s route of withdrawal.

Merritt opened the battle

Wesley Merritt’s division dismounted, crossed a cornfield, and attacked with seven-shot Spencer carbines. The first assault captured about two hundred Confederates and drove Lomax north.

U.S. scouts reached Richmond’s outer works

Two regiments found the works unmanned and heard alarm bells in the city. They saw no large force preparing to march against Sheridan.

Stuart asked Richmond for an infantry attack

Braxton Bragg reported that 4,000 reservists and government clerks held the city while three veteran brigades came from Petersburg. Stuart requested that Eppa Hunton’s brigade strike Sheridan’s flank.

Sheridan attacked again at 4 p.m.

Three U.S. divisions moved against Fitzhugh Lee’s two brigades during a thunderstorm. The Confederate infantry Stuart expected had not arrived.

The Confederate line broke within thirty minutes

Most units fought dismounted, but mounted U.S. attacks penetrated the position. Fitzhugh Lee withdrew the survivors across the Chickahominy River.

John A. Huff mortally wounded Stuart

The 5th Michigan Cavalry private shot Stuart in the abdomen while the general tried to rally the center of his line. Fitzhugh Lee assumed command.

Stuart was taken to Richmond

An ambulance made a six-hour trip to the home of Stuart’s brother-in-law, Dr. Charles Brewer. Stuart died there at 7:38 p.m. on May 12 before his wife arrived.

Sheridan tried to pass east of Richmond

Flooded water and the city defenses forced the U.S. cavalry toward the damaged Meadow Bridges over the Chickahominy River.

Confederate forces converged at Meadow Bridges

Fitzhugh Lee held the far bank, Gordon attacked from the west, and Archibald Gracie’s infantry advanced from Richmond while U.S. troops crossed the railroad bridge and repaired the road bridges.

The U.S. cavalry cleared the crossings

By about 4 p.m. on May 12 the Meadow Bridges were repaired and Confederate forces had withdrawn. Sheridan crossed and moved toward Haxall’s Landing on the James.

Sheridan reached the James River

The cavalry encamped at Haxall’s Landing on May 14, contacted U.S. gunboats, and reported the raid’s results to Grant.

The raid cost about 1,200 casualties

The episode estimates roughly six hundred casualties on each side. It also caused major supply losses, temporary railroad damage, and the death of Stuart.
