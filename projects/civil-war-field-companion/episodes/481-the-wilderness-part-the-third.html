THE WILDERNESS (Part the Third)

Grant and Meade initially believed they faced only part of Lee’s army, partly because U.S. cavalry had not located the Confederate columns. Warren attacked Ewell at Saunders Field before his flanks were covered; the attack broke down in the woods and was repulsed. Three miles south, Getty reached the Brock Road intersection just before Heth and stopped the Confederate advance. What happened

The armies made contact on May 5

Ewell’s corps met U.S. troops after Grant’s army crossed the Rapidan and entered the Wilderness.

Meade underestimated the Confederate force

Initial reports described a small enemy body, leading Meade and Grant to order an attack against what they believed was an isolated part of Lee’s army.

Sheridan’s cavalry had not found Lee’s columns

Orders called for scouting on the main roads, but James Wilson mostly picketed them and withdrew as U.S. infantry arrived.

Wilson operated on the wrong road

Most of his division moved near Catharpin Road, the one route Confederate infantry was not using, and skirmished with cavalry instead of locating Hill and Ewell.

Ewell formed at Saunders Field

Edward Johnson and Robert Rodes held the front line behind fieldworks while Jubal Early’s division remained in reserve.

Warren prepared the V Corps attack

Charles Griffin would attack west, John Robinson remained mostly in reserve, and James Wadsworth and Samuel Crawford were ordered toward Griffin’s left.

The Wilderness delayed Wadsworth

His division took four hours to cross roughly one mile of thickets, vines, fallen timber, hills, and swampy ground.

Crawford refused to leave the Tuning farm

He saw A. P. Hill approaching on Orange Plank Road and feared that moving north would uncover the Brock Road intersection and isolate Hancock.

U.S. commanders recognized Lee’s strength

Reports placed Hill on Orange Plank Road and Confederate cavalry north of Orange Turnpike, showing that more than an isolated force was advancing.

Meade ordered the army to consolidate

He sent George Getty’s VI Corps division to hold the critical Brock Road–Orange Plank Road intersection.

Warren found Griffin’s right exposed

The Confederate line extended farther north than expected, and Horatio Wright’s VI Corps division had not arrived to protect the flank.

Meade ordered an immediate attack

Warren and Griffin preferred to wait, but Meade directed them to advance before all supporting formations were in place.

Nine brigades entered the woods

Griffin and Wadsworth advanced, but the terrain quickly broke their lines into disconnected groups.

The attack began around 1 p.m.

Confederate volleys met Griffin in Saunders Field while Wadsworth’s troops struggled to keep direction and contact south of the clearing.

Wadsworth’s division came apart

One brigade exposed its flank, the Iron Brigade broke after a counterattack, and another brigade became stuck in swampy ground. Within 40 minutes the division no longer operated as a unit.

Bartlett briefly broke the Confederate line

Supported by two guns on Orange Turnpike, Joseph Bartlett’s brigade crossed Saunders Field and captured part of the works but withdrew because adjacent brigades had not broken through.

Fire spread across the battlefield

Dry brush and burning fieldworks ignited the woods. Wounded soldiers unable to move were caught by the flames.

The Saunders Field attack had failed

By about 2:30 p.m., Warren’s unsupported attack was over. Griffin blamed the failure on the absence of support on both flanks.

Hill advanced on Orange Plank Road

Three miles south of Saunders Field, Heth led Hill’s corps east through Parker’s Store toward the Brock Road intersection.

Getty’s infantry blocked the road

Meade’s reinforcement reached the intersection and stopped Heth after some Confederates had approached within roughly 30 yards.

Lee reviewed the separated corps

Hill’s advance placed his corps nearly three miles south and east of Ewell, creating a dangerous gap between the two Confederate columns.

U.S. skirmishers approached Tapp Field

Crawford’s men briefly emerged north of Lee’s headquarters. Lee then sent Wilcox north from Parker’s Store to reestablish contact with Ewell.

Getty built defenses at the crossroads

His division held for roughly two hours while Hancock’s II Corps marched toward the intersection.

Meade repeatedly redirected Hancock

Hancock received several changing orders to countermarch, join Getty, and attack Hill while his divisions were spread along six miles of roads.

II Corps arrived slowly

Hancock and his staff reached Brock Road by about 1 p.m., but only two divisions arrived during the next hour and the others remained farther back.

Meade ordered Getty forward before 4 p.m.

Getty obeyed the direct order rather than wait for Hancock to assemble a larger force.

Getty attacked with about 6,000 men

Three VI Corps brigades left their breastworks and advanced west through dense woods without knowing the exact Confederate position.

A volley struck at about 75 yards

Heth’s line fired repeated volleys into Getty’s troops, who could see only fire and smoke through the vegetation.

Getty’s division held in place

The terrain prevented a rapid bayonet charge. The U.S. line could not advance but remained engaged while Hancock prepared his attack.
