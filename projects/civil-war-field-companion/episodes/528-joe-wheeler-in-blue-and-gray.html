What happened

The episode introduces Wheeler’s three public careers

He served as a Confederate cavalry general, later represented Alabama in Congress, and returned to U.S. Army service during the Spanish-American War.

Wheeler was born in Augusta

Joseph Wheeler was born on September 10, 1836. After his mother died, his father moved the family to Connecticut.

He entered West Point in 1854

Wheeler graduated in 1859, ranking 19th in a class of 22, and entered the 1st U.S. Dragoons.

His first assignments were in the mounted service

Wheeler trained at Carlisle Barracks, transferred to the Mounted Rifles, and served at Fort Craig and other posts in New Mexico Territory.

Wheeler chose Georgia after secession

Despite northern family ties and schooling, he resigned his U.S. Army commission on February 27, 1861, after Georgia left the Union.

He joined the Confederate Army

Wheeler began as an artillery lieutenant at Pensacola and became colonel of the 19th Alabama Infantry in September 1861.

The 19th Alabama fought at Shiloh

Wheeler led repeated attacks on April 6, held ground on April 7, and commanded part of the retreat to Corinth.

Bragg transferred Wheeler to cavalry command

In July 1862, Wheeler received a cavalry brigade. His close-support work earned Bragg’s confidence while his relationship with Nathan Bedford Forrest remained difficult.

Wheeler became the army’s chief of cavalry

He was appointed brigadier general effective October 30, 1862, and became a major general on January 22, 1863, at age 26.

He operated around Stones River

Wheeler delayed the U.S. advance, rode around the Union army with about 2,000 cavalrymen, and later raided toward the Cumberland River.

He covered the retreat from Chattanooga

Wheeler and Patrick Cleburne stopped the U.S. pursuit at Ringgold Gap, where Wheeler was wounded in the foot.

His cavalry served throughout the Atlanta campaign

Wheeler screened Johnston’s army, defeated U.S. cavalry raids, and captured Major General George Stoneman.

The August raid failed to stop Sherman’s supply line

Wheeler captured Dalton but caused limited railroad damage. His absence deprived Hood of cavalry intelligence before the defeat at Jonesboro.

Wheeler opposed Sherman in Georgia and the Carolinas

His cavalry fought during the March to the Sea, won at Aiken, and served under Wade Hampton at Bentonville.

Wheeler was captured after Johnston’s surrender

He tried to join Jefferson Davis but was captured near Conyers, Georgia, on May 9, 1865, and was paroled on June 8.

He entered postwar politics

Wheeler became a planter and lawyer near Courtland, Alabama. He won a congressional seat, lost it after an election contest, returned in a special election, and later won eight more terms.

Wheeler returned to U.S. service in 1898

President William McKinley appointed him major general of volunteers. Wheeler commanded the cavalry division in Cuba during the campaign against Santiago.

He ordered the attack at Las Guasimas

Wheeler attacked before all U.S. troops had landed. The action cost U.S. forces 17 killed and 52 wounded before Spanish troops withdrew.

He served in the Philippines

Wheeler commanded a brigade under Arthur MacArthur and received a regular-army commission as brigadier general.

He retired from the army in 1900

After returning from the Philippines, Wheeler resigned from Congress, commanded the Department of the Lakes, and retired in September.

Wheeler died in 1906

He died in Brooklyn on January 25 at age 69 and was buried in Arlington National Cemetery.
