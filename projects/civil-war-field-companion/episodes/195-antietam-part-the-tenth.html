Episode 195 · September 17, 1862 · The northern Antietam battlefield around the Dunker Church and the southern West Woods, west of the East Woods and astride the Hagerstown Turnpike near Sharpsburg, Maryland

ANTIETAM (Part the Tenth)

At about 10 a.m., George S. Greene’s XII Corps division and supporting artillery stopped Van H. Manning’s attack east of the Dunker Church. Greene then advanced across the Hagerstown Turnpike and established a Federal line in the southern West Woods. He received the 13th New Jersey and Purnell’s Legion but incorrectly believed John Sedgwick’s division still protected his right. Confederate troops from Robert Ransom’s brigade attacked that exposed flank while John R. Cooke’s 27th North Carolina and 3rd Arkansas struck near the church. Greene’s line withdrew east, and Federal canister stopped the pursuit. The episode treats this action as the end of the sustained Federal effort against Lee’s left
Episode scopeCommand decisions and the closing fight around the Dunker ChurchThe first half compares Lee’s and McClellan’s battlefield control and recaps Sedgwick’s defeat. The second half follows Greene’s advance into and withdrawal from the West Woods.

Critical command errorGreene believed Sedgwick protected his rightSedgwick’s division had already been routed. Greene did not learn this until a member of Sumner’s staff told him that the division was gone.
Northern flank attack49th North Carolina from Ransom’s brigadeThe regiment surprised Purnell’s Legion and struck the 13th New Jersey’s flank. The episode attributes the movement to “Matthew W. Ransom”; the brigade commander was Brigadier General Robert Ransom Jr.
Southern attack27th North Carolina and 3rd Arkansas under John R. CookeCooke’s two regiments attacked a Federal two-gun section going into battery near the church as Greene’s infantry line began to give way.
Captured artilleryOne Federal gun left near the churchAlpheus Williams’s official report states that one piece from a temporarily detached section of Knap’s battery had to be abandoned after men and horses were lost.
Pursuit stoppedThree or four Federal batteries fired canisterThe artillery line east of the turnpike stopped Ransom’s and Cooke’s troops and forced them back toward the West Woods.
Chronology differs among accountsThe episode compresses Greene’s final withdrawal into the late-morning closeGreene reported holding the woods near the church for nearly two hours. The Battlefield Board tablet places Tyndale there until noon, while Williams’s report places the forced retirement at about 1:30 p.m.
Northern-field strengthAbout 23,000 Federal and just over 18,000 Confederate troopsThese are the episode’s aggregate figures for the five-hour fight north of the Sunken Road, not the strength in Greene’s final position.
Northern-field casualtiesAbout 6,550 Federal and 7,300 ConfederateThe episode totals nearly 14,000 killed, wounded, or missing in the northern fighting. NPS estimates for the Cornfield and West Woods are approximate and use somewhat different geographic groupings.
Highest division lossSedgwick’s division lost about one man in threeThe episode identifies Sedgwick’s II Corps division as the most heavily damaged Federal division in this portion of the battle.
Highest cited brigade rateWilliam T. Wofford’s brigade lost about 64 percentWofford’s Texans and Georgians had fought earlier around the Cornfield and West Woods.
Wilder DwightMortally wounded; died September 19The episode says Dwight died the next day. Contemporary biographical evidence places his death at midday on September 19, two days after he was wounded.
John Wesley FranksMortally wounded; died September 18The 7th South Carolina soldier was hit by Federal artillery and buried on the field by his brother-in-law John Lewis Wilhite.
Recommended workEzra A. Carman’s Maryland Campaign study, edited by Thomas G. ClemensThe episode recommends volume two of the three-volume edition for its coverage of the Battle of Antietam.

Important leaders killed or wounded

United States

Maj. Gen. Joseph K. F. Mansfield

XII Corps commander. Mortally wounded earlier in the northern battle; his removal left Alpheus Williams in command of XII Corps during Greene’s fight around the church.

United States

Lt. Col. Wilder Dwight

2nd Massachusetts Infantry. Mortally wounded near the Hagerstown Turnpike on September 17; wrote a final postscript to his mother from the field and died September 19, 1862.

United States

Col. William B. Goodrich

3rd Brigade, Greene’s division. Killed earlier in the northern fighting while leading the detached brigade. Greene and Williams both listed his death in their Official Reports.

United States

Lt. Col. Hector Tyndale

1st Brigade, Greene’s division. Severely wounded near the close of the action after commanding Greene’s right brigade through the fight around the church.

Confederacy

Col. Van H. Manning

Walker’s brigade. Severely wounded during the failed advance east of the West Woods and compelled to relinquish command to Colonel Edward D. Hall.

Confederacy

John Wesley Franks

7th South Carolina Infantry, Kershaw’s brigade. Mortally wounded by Federal artillery on September 17 and died September 18, 1862; initially buried in an unmarked grave on the battlefield.
