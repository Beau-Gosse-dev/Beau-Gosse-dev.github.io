Episode 195 · September 17, 1862 · The northern Antietam battlefield around the Dunker Church and the southern West Woods, west of the East Woods and astride the Hagerstown Turnpike near Sharpsburg, Maryland

ANTIETAM (Part the Tenth)

At about 10 a.m., George S. Greene’s XII Corps division and supporting artillery stopped Van H. Manning’s attack east of the Dunker Church. Greene then advanced across the Hagerstown Turnpike and established a Federal line in the southern West Woods. He received the 13th New Jersey and Purnell’s Legion but incorrectly believed John Sedgwick’s division still protected his right. Confederate troops from Robert Ransom’s brigade attacked that exposed flank while John R. Cooke’s 27th North Carolina and 3rd Arkansas struck near the church. Greene’s line withdrew east, and Federal canister stopped the pursuit. The episode treats this action as the end of the sustained Federal effort against Lee’s left

30th Virginia loss160 of 230 men in the episode’s accountThe regiment broke under concentrated infantry and artillery fire. Walker’s official report describes severe losses and says most of the regiment did not return to the battle as an organized unit.
Northern-field strengthAbout 23,000 Federal and just over 18,000 Confederate troopsThese are the episode’s aggregate figures for the five-hour fight north of the Sunken Road, not the strength in Greene’s final position.
Northern-field casualtiesAbout 6,550 Federal and 7,300 ConfederateThe episode totals nearly 14,000 killed, wounded, or missing in the northern fighting. NPS estimates for the Cornfield and West Woods are approximate and use somewhat different geographic groupings.

Important leaders killed or wounded

United States

Maj. Gen. Joseph K. F. Mansfield

XII Corps commander. Mortally wounded earlier in the northern battle; his removal left Alpheus Williams in command of XII Corps during Greene’s fight around the church.

United States

Lt. Col. Wilder Dwight

2nd Massachusetts Infantry. Mortally wounded near the Hagerstown Turnpike on September 17; wrote a final postscript to his mother from the field and died September 19, 1862.

United States

Col. William B. Goodrich

3rd Brigade, Greene’s division. Killed earlier in the northern fighting while leading the detached brigade. Greene and Williams both listed his death in their Official Reports.

United States

Lt. Col. Hector Tyndale

1st Brigade, Greene’s division. Severely wounded near the close of the action after commanding Greene’s right brigade through the fight around the church.

Confederacy

Col. Van H. Manning

Walker’s brigade. Severely wounded during the failed advance east of the West Woods and compelled to relinquish command to Colonel Edward D. Hall.

Confederacy

John Wesley Franks

7th South Carolina Infantry, Kershaw’s brigade. Mortally wounded by Federal artillery on September 17 and died September 18, 1862; initially buried in an unmarked grave on the battlefield.
