Episode 195 · September 17, 1862 · The northern Antietam battlefield around the Dunker Church and the southern West Woods, west of the East Woods and astride the Hagerstown Turnpike near Sharpsburg, Maryland

ANTIETAM (Part the Tenth)

At about 10 a.m., George S. Greene’s XII Corps division and supporting artillery stopped Van H. Manning’s attack east of the Dunker Church. Greene then advanced across the Hagerstown Turnpike and established a Federal line in the southern West Woods. He received the 13th New Jersey and Purnell’s Legion but incorrectly believed John Sedgwick’s division still protected his right. Confederate troops from Robert Ransom’s brigade attacked that exposed flank while John R. Cooke’s 27th North Carolina and 3rd Arkansas struck near the church. Greene’s line withdrew east, and Federal canister stopped the pursuit. The episode treats this action as the end of the sustained Federal effort against Lee’s left

What happened

The episode defines command responsibility

A. Wilson Greene’s framework distinguishes the senior commander’s duty to position and direct forces from subordinates’ duty to adapt during combat.

Lee and McClellan are compared

The hosts contrast Lee’s direct battlefield involvement and reinforcement decisions with McClellan’s distant control from east of Antietam Creek.

Lee moves near the active front

Lee leaves his Sharpsburg headquarters area and operates close enough to the fighting to observe conditions and encounter stragglers.

Lee shifts strength to Jackson

He weakens the quiet Confederate right to send Walker’s division and George T. Anderson’s brigade toward the West Woods.

The last Confederate reserves enter the battle

McLaws moves north toward Jackson while Richard H. Anderson goes to reinforce D. H. Hill at the Sunken Road.

McClellan remains at the Pry House

McClellan and Fitz John Porter watch the distant northern battle from headquarters east of Antietam Creek.

Signals and couriers lag behind events

McClellan depends on communications that cannot keep pace with changes on the northern field.

The piecemeal morning attacks are reviewed

The episode argues that Hooker’s I Corps and Mansfield’s XII Corps should have attacked in concert.

Sumner receives orders at 7:20 a.m.

II Corps begins its delayed march toward the Federal right while Richardson’s division remains temporarily east of the creek.

Sedgwick’s advance is recapped

Sumner takes Sedgwick west without waiting for French and without reliable knowledge of the Confederate position.

The attack omits basic precautions

The episode identifies the crowded brigade lines, lack of reconnaissance, absent skirmish screen, and unsupported flanks.

Confederate reinforcements strike Sedgwick

McLaws, George T. Anderson, part of Walker’s division, and Early attack the exposed Federal formation.

Sedgwick’s losses exceed 2,200

The division loses almost half its strength during about twenty minutes of combat and retreats north.

The narrative returns to the Dunker Church

The episode leaves the Sedgwick recap and focuses on Greene’s division while reserving the Sunken Road for the next installment.

Greene’s two brigades hold east of the church

Tyndale commands the right brigade and Stainrook the left after the 125th Pennsylvania has already withdrawn.

Walker’s second brigade approaches

At about 10 a.m., Van H. Manning turns part of Walker’s division toward Greene’s infantry and artillery supports.

Smoke obscures the church

Greene’s men can barely see the Dunker Church less than 200 yards away as the Confederate line emerges from the West Woods.

Manning’s three regiments charge

The 46th North Carolina, 30th Virginia, and 48th North Carolina cross the turnpike toward Greene.

Greene opens fire at close range

Federal infantry and canister from Tompkins’s battery strike the Confederate line at about 70 yards.

The 30th Virginia is shattered

The episode gives the regiment 160 casualties among 230 men in the assault.

Manning is carried from the field

The brigade commander is severely wounded and his survivors retreat through the West Woods.

Greene counterattacks

Tyndale’s and Stainrook’s men cross the turnpike in pursuit as the 13th New Jersey and Purnell’s Legion come forward.

The Federal line passes the church

Greene pushes about 200 yards into the woods and posts troops along the southern fence line.

Greene asks for reinforcements

He recognizes that the advanced Federal position has penetrated the Confederate line but cannot hold it without support.

Burnside receives his attack order

At 9:10 a.m., McClellan orders IX Corps to attack the Lower Bridge after learning that Franklin’s VI Corps is near.

Greene consolidates in the West Woods

The Federal line occupies ground beyond the church while the 5th and 7th Ohio begin to run out of ammunition.

Fresh regiments fill the Ohio positions

The 13th New Jersey and Purnell’s Legion join the 28th Pennsylvania on the right of Greene’s line.

Carman warns of the exposed right

Greene dismisses the concern because he assumes Sedgwick’s division still occupies the woods beyond his flank.

Greene learns Sedgwick is gone

A member of Sumner’s staff tells him the entire division has been routed from the West Woods.

Confederates strike both flanks

Independent attacks hit opposite ends of Greene’s line before he can adjust to the loss of Sedgwick.

The 49th North Carolina attacks from the north

Ransom’s brigade surprises Purnell’s Legion and catches the 13th New Jersey in flank.

Greene’s line begins to withdraw

The 28th Pennsylvania follows the retreating right-flank regiments, and the movement spreads through the division.

Cooke attacks the Federal guns

The 27th North Carolina and 3rd Arkansas advance on a two-gun section near the church as Greene’s infantry gives way.

One Federal gun is left behind

The episode describes Cooke’s men overrunning one piece while the other gun escapes; Williams reported that one piece had to be abandoned.

Federal canister stops the pursuit

Three or four batteries strike the advancing Confederates and force Ransom’s and Cooke’s men back.

Sustained fighting ends on the northern field

The retreat of Greene and repulse of the pursuit leave only skirmishing and artillery fire around the West Woods.

The northern battle’s scale is counted

The episode gives about 23,000 Federal and more than 18,000 Confederate participants in the five-hour fight north of the Sunken Road.

Losses approach 14,000

The episode reports about 6,550 Federal and 7,300 Confederate casualties in this portion of the battlefield.

Wilder Dwight’s final letter is recalled

Mortally wounded near the Hagerstown Turnpike, the 2nd Massachusetts officer adds a farewell to the letter he had begun that morning.

John Wesley Franks dies of his wound

The 7th South Carolina soldier dies on September 18 after being mortally wounded by Federal artillery.

Carman’s campaign history is recommended

The hosts recommend the three-volume Maryland Campaign study, particularly volume two for Antietam.
