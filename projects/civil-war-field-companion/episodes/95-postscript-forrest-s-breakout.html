POSTSCRIPT: FORREST'S BREAKOUT

Nathan Bedford Forrest rose from a Tennessee and Mississippi businessman and slave trader to lieutenant colonel of Confederate cavalry. At Fort Donelson, his cavalry helped open Forge Road during the Confederate attack on February 15, 1862. The senior commanders then ordered the troops back into their defenses. Before the surrender on February 16, Forrest led roughly 500 mounted men out on the old Charlotte Road, crossed flooded Lick Creek, and reached Nashville on February 18

Chain of command

Fort Donelson · February 15, 1862

Selected field commanders during the breakout battle. Connected branches show command responsibility; this is not a complete roster of officers or units.

United States

  • Overall land command

    Ulysses S. Grant

    Commanded the U.S. force at Fort Donelson and ordered the afternoon counterattacks.

    • First Division · right

      John A. McClernand

      His division bore the main Confederate breakout attack along the roads south of the fort.

    • Second Division · left

      Charles F. Smith

      Led the assault that captured part of the Confederate outer defenses late in the day.

    • Third Division · center

      Lew Wallace

      Reinforced the U.S. right and led the counterattack that recovered lost ground.

Confederacy

  • Fort Donelson garrison

    John B. Floyd

    Held senior command of the garrison during the February 15 breakout battle.

    • Breakout attack · left wing

      Gideon J. Pillow

      Directed the main attack and ordered the troops back inside the defenses.

      • Left-wing infantry

        Bushrod R. Johnson

        Assisted Pillow in directing the infantry attack against McClernand’s division.

      • Cavalry · attack support

        Nathan Bedford Forrest

        Commanded the cavalry supporting Pillow’s attack and later led the mounted escape.

    • Right-wing division

      Simon B. Buckner

      Supported the breakout and returned to confront Smith’s advance against his earthworks.

Command changed overnight

Before dawn on February 16, Floyd relinquished command, Pillow declined it, and Buckner took charge of the garrison; Forrest then obtained permission to lead his cavalry out.
