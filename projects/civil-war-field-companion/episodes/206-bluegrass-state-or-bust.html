BLUEGRASS STATE OR BUST!

Braxton Bragg shifted most of the Confederate Army of Mississippi from Tupelo to Chattanooga by a long, indirect rail-and-water route, ending the immediate threat to that city and helping the Confederacy regain the initiative in the western theater. Edmund Kirby Smith then pressed for an independent advance from East Tennessee into Kentucky rather than a preliminary reduction of Cumberland Gap and a prompt junction with Bragg. Bragg accepted a divided offensive despite warning that Don Carlos Buell could defeat the separated armies. Confederate expectations of a mass uprising in Kentucky rested on exaggerated reports and on a provisional government that never displaced the Union-aligned state government

United States national and western command

Commander in chiefPresident Abraham LincolnMade East Tennessee a longstanding strategic objective and appointed Halleck general in chief
General in chief from July 23, 1862Maj. Gen. Henry W. HalleckLeft western field command for Washington; Grant and Buell then resumed independent army commands
Army of the OhioMaj. Gen. Don Carlos BuellAdvanced toward Chattanooga; Brig. Gen. George W. Morgan commanded the division at Cumberland Gap, while Ormsby Mitchel’s division held parts of northern Alabama
Army of the Tennessee and District of West TennesseeMaj. Gen. Ulysses S. GrantHeld the Corinth–Memphis sector independently of Buell and was the intended target of Price and Van Dorn’s diversion

Confederate national and main western command

Commander in chiefPresident Jefferson DavisReplaced Beauregard with Bragg and attempted to coordinate Bragg, Kirby Smith, and Lee without creating a unified western command
Department No. 2 and Army of MississippiGen. Braxton BraggMoved the main field army to Chattanooga; assigned Sterling Price near Tupelo and Earl Van Dorn to Vicksburg; lent two divisions to Kirby Smith
Confederate force near TupeloMaj. Gen. Sterling PriceWatched the United States forces in northern Mississippi and was expected eventually to cooperate against Grant
District of the MississippiMaj. Gen. Earl Van DornHeld responsibility for defending Vicksburg and was expected to cooperate with Price against Grant

Confederate East Tennessee command

Department and Army of East TennesseeMaj. Gen. Edmund Kirby SmithDefended Knoxville and Chattanooga, received two divisions from Bragg, and chose a separate advance into Kentucky
Division in East TennesseeBrig. Gen. Carter L. StevensonHad withdrawn from Cumberland Gap in June and remained one of Smith’s senior field commanders
Cavalry raiding forceCol. John Hunt MorganRaided Kentucky in July and sent Smith an exaggerated estimate of the recruits an invasion would produce

Kentucky civil authority and rival claimant

Elected governor of KentuckyGov. Beriah MagoffinFavored neutrality and held Southern sympathies but governed with a Unionist legislature that rejected secession
Lawful state legislatureKentucky General AssemblyAligned the state with the United States after Confederate troops occupied Columbus in September 1861
Confederate provisional governorsGeorge W. Johnson; then Richard HawesLed the rival government created at Russellville, recognized by Richmond but unable to displace the government at Frankfort
