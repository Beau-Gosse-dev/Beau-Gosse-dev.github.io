SEVEN DAYS' BATTLES: GLENDALE (Part the First)

This episode covers the deployment and opening operations of June 30 rather than the main infantry fighting at Glendale. By the point where the episode ends, William B. Franklin had blocked Thomas J. “Stonewall” Jackson at White Oak Swamp, Benjamin Huger had been delayed on the Charles City Road, and four United States brigades had left the White Oak sector to reinforce the line near Glendale. The larger Battle of Glendale, completed in episode 165, was indecisive; the United States line of retreat remained open
Episode scopeThe approach to Glendale and the separate action at White Oak SwampThe episode ends before Longstreet’s and A. P. Hill’s main assault west of the Glendale crossroads.
Geographic sequenceSavage Station, White Oak Swamp, Glendale, Malvern Hill, and the James RiverThe episode describes these points from north to south to establish the Army of the Potomac’s withdrawal route.
Savage Station withdrawalThe Federal rear guard resumed its march after dark on June 29McClellan’s order left approximately 2,500 sick and wounded United States soldiers at the Savage Station field hospital.
White Oak crossingThe Army of the Potomac was south of White Oak Swamp by midmorning on June 30The army still had to move its wagon trains and remaining combat formations through Glendale toward the James.
Operational bottleneckThe Glendale crossroads controlled the Willis Church Road toward Malvern HillSupply wagons and troops converged there, and a successful Confederate advance from the west could have cut the withdrawal route.
Federal advance elementsKeyes’s IV Corps and most of Porter’s V Corps had reached Malvern HillGeorge A. McCall’s Pennsylvania Reserves, a V Corps division, remained near Glendale to protect the route.
White Oak defenseFranklin’s VI Corps covered the swamp exitsWilliam F. Smith’s and Henry W. Slocum’s divisions formed the principal force facing Jackson. Israel B. Richardson’s II Corps division was positioned nearby.
Glendale defenseMcCall faced west, with Kearny to the right and Hooker to the leftJohn Sedgwick’s division was posted to the left and rear of McCall. The deployment was not a continuous, centrally directed line.
Federal command arrangementNo battlefield commander controlled all of the divisions around GlendaleMcClellan did not designate a substitute commander. Corps and division commanders coordinated only portions of the defensive line.
McClellan at Haxall’s LandingReached the James River landing at about 10 a.m.He had inspected ground around Glendale earlier that morning, then moved with his staff to the river. The U.S. Army campaign history places him aboard USS Galena at 4 p.m.
Lee’s objectiveConverge on Glendale and cut the Army of the Potomac’s line to the JamesLee directed separated columns toward the Federal rear, flank, center, and withdrawal route. The plan depended on timing and communication between the columns.
Jackson’s assignmentPursue the Federal rear guard through White Oak SwampJackson moved south from Savage Station. Lee’s exact verbal instructions from their morning meeting were not recorded, so the expected extent of Jackson’s action remains a matter of interpretation.
Huger’s assignmentApproach Glendale on the Charles City Road from the northwestLee expected Huger’s movement to help open the coordinated attack, but felled trees blocked the road and delayed the division.
Longstreet and A. P. HillApproach from the west by the Long Bridge RoadTheir combined divisions formed the principal striking force against the Federal line west of the crossroads. Their assault is covered in episode 165.
Magruder and HolmesMagruder served as a reserve; Holmes moved toward Malvern HillMagruder marched by the Darbytown Road in support of Longstreet and Hill. Holmes was to threaten the wagon route and limit reinforcement from the south.
Huger’s road obstructionFelled trees extended for nearly a mile in the episode’s accountWilliam Mahone recommended cutting a new route through the woods. The resulting delay became known as the “Battle of the Axes.”
Jackson reaches White Oak SwampThe head of his column arrived shortly before noonThe bridge was destroyed. United States infantry and artillery occupied higher ground south of the stream.
Confederate bombardmentThirty-one guns opened at about 2 p.m. in the episode’s accountThe artillery fire prompted Franklin to request reinforcements from Sumner, but Jackson did not follow the bombardment with a general infantry crossing.
Munford’s reconnaissanceThe 2nd Virginia Cavalry found an undefended crossing downstreamColonel Thomas T. Munford reported that infantry could use the cow crossing to reach the Federal right flank. Jackson did not act on the report.
Hampton’s crossingA second shallow crossing was found east of the destroyed bridgeWade Hampton’s men built a crude bridge suitable for infantry. No infantry attack followed after Hampton reported that it was ready.
Effect of Jackson’s inactionFederal reinforcements could leave the White Oak sectorThe episode states that four brigades, nearly 10,000 men, ultimately moved from Jackson’s front to the fighting near Glendale. Strength totals are approximate.
Jackson’s performanceThe causes remain disputedThe episode weighs physical exhaustion against unfamiliarity with Lee’s command expectations. Neither explanation can be established conclusively from the surviving evidence.
Important leader losses in this episode’s actionNone identifiedThe episode does not identify a senior commander killed or mortally wounded during the White Oak Swamp action. The main Glendale assault and its casualties are outside this episode’s scope.

What happened

The battlefield geography is established

The episode begins a north-to-south description from Richmond and the Chickahominy toward the James River.

The retreat resumes after Savage Station

The Federal rear guard withdraws after the June 29 fighting, leaving sick and wounded soldiers at the field hospital.

The army clears White Oak Swamp

By midmorning on June 30, the Army of the Potomac has crossed south of the swamp, but its wagon trains still need time to reach the James.

The Federal dispositions are reviewed

Keyes and most of Porter are near Malvern Hill while Franklin covers White Oak Swamp and five divisions remain around Glendale.

McCall occupies the western approach

The Pennsylvania Reserves face west while Kearny, Hooker, and Sedgwick occupy adjoining but imperfectly connected positions.

The absence of central battlefield command is explained

Division commanders operate largely within their own sectors without a commander directing the entire Glendale line.

McClellan leaves for Haxall’s Landing

After examining ground near Glendale, he rides to the James River and reaches the landing at about 10 a.m.

McClellan boards USS Galena later in the day

The episode describes his movement aboard the gunboat after 3 p.m.; the U.S. Army campaign history uses 4 p.m.

No substitute army commander is appointed

McClellan does not give Sumner or another officer authority over the full battlefield deployment.

Lee organizes the converging attack

Confederate columns are directed toward Glendale, White Oak Swamp, and the road system leading to Malvern Hill.

Six Confederate commands move

The episode begins its account of Jackson, Huger, Longstreet, A. P. Hill, Magruder, and Holmes.

Jackson reports his column in motion

At about 3:30 a.m., Jackson tells Magruder that his force has begun crossing the Chickahominy.

Magruder receives a reserve assignment

Lee directs Magruder toward the Darbytown Road in support of Longstreet and A. P. Hill.

Lee meets Jackson at Savage Station

The commanders confer before Jackson continues toward the White Oak Swamp crossing; their precise verbal instructions were not recorded.

Lee’s western columns are identified

Huger, Longstreet, and A. P. Hill approach Glendale while Holmes moves toward the Malvern Hill end of the withdrawal route.

The planned attack sequence is summarized

Huger is expected from the Charles City Road, Jackson from the north, and Longstreet and A. P. Hill from the Long Bridge Road.

Huger encounters felled trees

William Mahone’s leading brigade finds the Charles City Road obstructed for nearly a mile in the episode’s account.

The “Battle of the Axes” delays Huger

Confederates cut a new route through the woods while Federal axmen add obstacles farther ahead.

Jackson reaches the destroyed bridge

The head of his column arrives shortly before noon and faces Franklin’s troops on higher ground south of the stream.

Jackson opens the artillery action

Thirty-one Confederate guns begin firing at about 2 p.m. in the episode’s account.

Franklin requests reinforcements

Expecting an infantry attack to follow the bombardment, Franklin asks Sumner for additional troops.

Munford crosses to reconnoiter

The 2nd Virginia Cavalry crosses the swamp while Jackson and D. H. Hill examine the Federal position.

An undefended cow crossing is reported

Munford finds another route downstream and reports that infantry could use it against the Federal right.

Hampton finds a second crossing

Wade Hampton locates a shallow point closer to the destroyed bridge and reports it to Jackson.

Hampton’s men complete a footbridge

After several hours of work, the brigade has a crossing suitable for infantry but not artillery.

No infantry crossing is ordered

Hampton reports that the bridge is ready and offers to lead his brigade, but Jackson issues no advance order.

Jackson’s inaction is assessed

The episode presents exhaustion and difficulty operating under Lee’s direction as possible contributing factors.

Federal brigades reinforce Glendale

With no infantry attack at White Oak Swamp, four brigades leave Jackson’s front and later fight Longstreet and A. P. Hill.

The episode’s book recommendation is given

The hosts recommend Brian K. Burton’s “Extraordinary Circumstances: The Seven Days Battles.”
