What happened

The episode resumes after Logan’s attack

By noon, the XV Corps attack at Pigeon Hill had ended. Other units continued to press the Confederate line on Big Kennesaw.

McPherson used a heavy skirmish line

Sherman and McPherson wanted the XV, XVI, and XVII Corps troops to keep Confederate units from moving south as reinforcements.

Leggett and Gresham advanced on Big Kennesaw

Skirmishers from Mortimer Leggett’s and Walter Gresham’s XVII Corps divisions moved against Loring’s Confederate corps on the northern slope.

Leggett’s men drove in the Confederate skirmishers

Louisiana skirmishers withdrew to Thomas M. Scott’s main line. Confederate fire then stopped the U.S. advance close to the works.

Leggett’s line remained pinned down

The U.S. soldiers stayed under artillery and small-arms fire for several hours before they withdrew.

Gresham’s skirmishers were also stopped

Men of William W. Quarles’s brigade, including the 1st Alabama skirmishers led by Samuel Knox, stopped the attack.

Gresham blamed the mountain slope

The steep, rough ground broke up the U.S. skirmish line before it could reach the main Confederate position.

Dodge sent three Illinois regiments forward

The 9th and 66th Illinois from Thomas Sweeny’s division and the 64th Illinois from James Veatch’s division advanced against Big Kennesaw.

The XVI Corps advance stopped near the crest

The skirmishers drove Confederate pickets back but could not cross the steep, rocky slope and enter the works.

McPherson reported fewer than 1,000 casualties

Many of the losses occurred among soldiers pinned down for hours at Pigeon Hill and on Kennesaw Mountain.

The episode shifts to George H. Thomas

Thomas commanded more than 50,000 men in the Army of the Cumberland and was responsible for Sherman’s main assault.

Thomas doubted the attack would succeed

Postwar accounts state that Thomas and David Stanley opposed the plan, but Thomas carried out Sherman’s order.

IV Corps officers could not see the works clearly

Joseph Fullerton recorded that dense woods and uneven ground concealed the Confederate position until attackers were very close.

The 125th Ohio joined the forward skirmish line

Harker’s men were awakened at 3 a.m. and moved forward knowing they would lead part of the assault.

Newton received orders for a three-brigade attack

Kimball formed on the left, Wagner in the center, and Harker on the right. Howard ordered Harker and Kimball to use columns.

Harker and Kimball objected to column formations

Columns presented a narrow front and concentrated men under enemy fire. Howard refused to change the order.

A forward brigade already screened Newton

William Grose’s brigade from Stanley’s division held a skirmish line a few hundred yards from the Confederate works.

Seven U.S. batteries opened fire

At 8 a.m., IV Corps artillery began the bombardment that preceded Newton’s infantry attack.

Cleburne’s four brigades held the target line

Hiram Granbury, Daniel Govan, Mark Lowrey, and Lucius Polk held the ridge from right to left, supported by Confederate artillery.
