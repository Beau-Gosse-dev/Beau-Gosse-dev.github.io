Episode 191 · September 17, 1862 · The northern Antietam battlefield near Sharpsburg, Maryland, from the Joseph Poffenberger farm and Miller’s Cornfield south to the Dunker Church plateau, with the East and West Woods on either flank

ANTIETAM (Part the Sixth)

Soon after daybreak, Joseph Hooker opened the Battle of Antietam by sending the United States I Corps south toward the Dunker Church. James B. Ricketts’s division attacked through the Cornfield and East Woods in separate brigade actions. Alexander Lawton’s Confederates, supported by Stephen D. Lee’s artillery and a counterattack by Harry T. Hays’s Louisiana brigade, stopped the advance. By about 7 a.m. the fighting in the eastern Cornfield and East Woods had reached a costly stalemate; Abner Doubleday’s simultaneous attack along the Hagerstown Turnpike continues in the next episode
Episode scopeThe opening action at Antietam, from daybreak to about 7 a.m.The episode follows Hooker’s plan, the opening artillery exchange, and Ricketts’s attack through the eastern Cornfield and East Woods. Doubleday’s attack on the I Corps right is introduced but reserved for the next episode.
Hooker’s objectiveThe Dunker Church plateauHooker planned to attack south along the Hagerstown Turnpike and believed possession of the plateau would begin to roll up the Confederate left.
United States opening forceAbout 8,600 men in I Corps in the episode’s estimateHooker’s three divisions opened the northern attack before Mansfield’s XII Corps could provide effective support.
I Corps deploymentDoubleday right, Ricketts left, Meade supporting the centerTruman Seymour’s brigade of Meade’s Pennsylvania Reserves already held a lodgement in the East Woods after the September 16 skirmish.
Hooker’s expanded responsibilityA semi-independent two-corps wingMcClellan expected Hooker to coordinate I Corps and Mansfield’s XII Corps on the northern battlefield, but Hooker planned his own corps attack before sending Mansfield a general order to support it.
Coordination failureI Corps was engaged before XII Corps received useful directionMansfield had commanded XII Corps for only two days. The episode says his roughly 10,000 troops required an hour or more to assemble and enter the fight.
Confederate opening forceAbout 7,700 men under Jackson in the episode’s estimateThe force included most of Alexander Lawton’s and John R. Jones’s divisions, with John Bell Hood’s two brigades initially in reserve.
Confederate frontAbout three-quarters of a mile facing northLawton’s division covered the ground south of the Cornfield and across the Smoketown Road toward the Mumma farm; Jones’s division occupied the West Woods sector north of the Dunker Church.
Mumma farmhouseBurned by Confederate troops before the main attackThe Confederates destroyed the house so United States sharpshooters could not use it as a strongpoint. Its smoke became a conspicuous landmark over the eastern battlefield.
Dunker Church artilleryFour batteries under Col. Stephen D. LeeLee’s artillery battalion occupied the open plateau east of the church and fired north and northeast into the approaches used by I Corps.
Nicodemus HeightsFourteen guns under Stuart and Pelham in the episode’s accountThe high ground west of the Hagerstown Turnpike allowed Confederate artillery to strike the right flank of the Federal advance. Early’s brigade protected the position.
Opening fireArtillery engaged soon after 5 a.m.A. W. Garber later claimed the opening shot for his battery on Nicodemus Heights. The episode treats that claim cautiously and describes Confederate and Federal batteries opening as visibility improved.
East Woods renewalSeymour’s Pennsylvania Reserves advanced at first lightThree regiments pushed to the southern edge of the woods and fired on James A. Walker’s Confederates along the Mumma farm, renewing the fight that darkness had interrupted.
Ricketts’s lead brigadeAbout 1,100 men under Abram DuryeaDuryea’s New York and Pennsylvania regiments entered the head-high corn at about 6 a.m. without the close support Ricketts intended.
Confederate first lineDouglass’s Georgians south of the CornfieldMarcellus Douglass ordered his men to lie down in the rolling pasture and wait. They rose and fired when Duryea’s troops emerged from the southern edge of the corn.
Converging Confederate fireDouglass in front and Walker from the eastWalker shifted part of Trimble’s brigade across the Smoketown Road to a rocky ledge. The episode says only about 40 of the 12th Georgia’s 100 men remained unhurt after the exchange.
Ricketts’s broken sequenceThree brigades attacked piecemealGeorge Hartsuff’s brigade was delayed after Hartsuff was wounded by a shell fragment, while William Christian abandoned command of the other supporting brigade. Richard Coulter and Peter Lyle brought those formations forward separately.
Duryea’s lossNearly one-third of the brigade in about 30 minutesLow ammunition and mounting pressure forced Duryea back through the Cornfield. The brigade withdrew in order but did not fight again that day.
Hays’s counterattackLouisiana troops struck Coulter’s brigade near the CornfieldThe Louisiana brigade and parts of Douglass’s brigade drove the Federals toward the corn and East Woods before artillery and infantry fire forced the Confederates back.
12th Massachusetts224 killed or wounded of 334 engaged, about 67 percentThe National Park Service identifies this as the highest percentage loss of any United States regiment at Antietam. The episode describes only 32 men initially remaining with the colors when the regiment withdrew.
Louisiana brigade323 casualties among about 550 men, about 59 percentThe episode rounds the brigade’s loss to 61 percent. National Park Service figures list 323 men shot down and every regimental commander hit during about 30 minutes of fighting.
Immediate outcomeA stalemate in the eastern Cornfield and East WoodsBy about 7 a.m. Ricketts’s division and Lawton’s front-line brigades were badly damaged. Stephen D. Lee’s guns had helped disrupt the Federal advance, and neither side had secured a decisive advantage in this sector.
Senior leader casualties in scopeCol. Marcellus Douglass killed; Brig. Gen. George L. Hartsuff woundedDouglass was killed while commanding Lawton’s brigade south of the Cornfield. Hartsuff’s wound delayed his brigade until Col. Richard Coulter assumed command.
Recommended book“The Bloodiest Day: The Battle of Antietam” by Ronald H. BaileyThe volume belongs to the illustrated Time-Life Civil War series and was recommended as an accessible introduction, especially for younger or newer readers.

What happened

The night-before position is recapped

Hooker’s I Corps has crossed the Antietam and Seymour’s brigade holds part of the East Woods after a dusk fight with Hood.

Mansfield crosses in rain and darkness

XII Corps moves across the creek after nightfall and bivouacs roughly a mile behind and left of Hooker.

Hood goes into reserve

Jackson permits Hood’s exhausted brigades to pull back and cook, warning that they must return at the double if called.

A Wisconsin soldier waits for dawn

William F. Goodhue’s recollection describes wet muskets, fog, abandoned breakfasts, and the expectation of immediate battle.

Blackford describes civilians under the guns

William W. Blackford recalls helping women and children cross a plowed field while the opposing batteries temporarily hold their fire.

Hooker chooses the Dunker Church plateau

From the picket line shortly after 5 a.m., Hooker identifies the raised ground and Confederate guns near the church as his objective.

The I Corps plan is laid out

Doubleday will advance along the Hagerstown Turnpike, Ricketts through the Cornfield and East Woods, and Meade in support.

I Corps attacks without coordinated support

Hooker’s roughly 8,600 men open the battle before a working plan has been arranged with Mansfield’s XII Corps.

Hooker acts as a corps commander

The episode argues that Hooker organizes his own divisions first and only then sends Mansfield a general instruction to support him.

Jackson’s strength and line are defined

About 7,700 Confederates occupy the northern battlefield under Jackson, including Lawton’s and Jones’s divisions and Hood’s reserve.

The Mumma farmhouse burns

Confederates torch the building to deny it to Federal sharpshooters, creating a column of smoke over the eastern battlefield.

Stephen D. Lee masses guns by the church

Four Confederate batteries on the plateau east of the Dunker Church command the approaches from the north and east.

Nicodemus Heights threatens Hooker’s flank

Stuart and Pelham place 14 guns on the commanding ridge, supported by Early’s infantry brigade.

The opening cannonade begins

Garber later claims the first shot; soon after 5 a.m. artillery on both sides exchanges fire through fog and overcast light.

Seymour renews the East Woods fight

Pennsylvania Reserves advance to the southern edge of the woods and engage Walker’s brigade on the Mumma farm.

Ricketts and Doubleday form for attack

Duryea leads Ricketts’s left-wing advance while Gibbon’s brigade spearheads Doubleday’s movement on the right.

Duryea enters the Cornfield

After Federal guns fire precautionary canister, about 1,100 men move into the head-high corn at roughly 6 a.m.

Douglass’s Georgians rise and fire

The concealed Confederate line delivers a close volley as Duryea’s brigade emerges from the southern edge of the corn.

Walker adds converging fire

Trimble’s brigade shifts from the Mumma farm to a rocky ledge and strikes Duryea from the east while Seymour’s remnants fire into Walker’s flank.

Ricketts’s reinforcements break down

Hartsuff is wounded, Coulter takes over his brigade, and Christian abandons the other supporting brigade.

A fourteen-year-old rallies the colors

Private John Carroll Delaney helps recover the fallen flags of the 107th Pennsylvania during Duryea’s withdrawal.

Hays’s Louisianans counterattack

The Louisiana brigade charges across the pasture and collides with Coulter’s Federal brigade near the Cornfield and East Woods.

The 12th Massachusetts is devastated

The episode counts 224 casualties and a 67 percent loss rate after the regiment’s fight against Hays and Douglass.

Thompson pushes a battery into the corn

Federal artillery occupies a small rise inside the Cornfield and contributes to the converging fire that stops the Confederate counterattack.

Douglass is killed

The Confederate colonel falls while every regimental commander in Hays’s brigade is also down.

Lyle’s brigade finally enters

At about 7 a.m., Peter Lyle leads Christian’s former brigade through the East Woods and relieves Coulter’s shattered formations.

The loss rates are compared

The episode contrasts the 12th Massachusetts at 67 percent with the Louisiana brigade at roughly 61 percent.

The eastern attack ends in stalemate

Ricketts’s separate brigade attacks fail to break Lawton’s position, and Confederate artillery has prevented Federal guns from dominating the field.

The book recommendation closes the episode

The hosts recommend Ronald H. Bailey’s illustrated Time-Life volume “The Bloodiest Day: The Battle of Antietam.”
