Episode 191 · September 17, 1862 · The northern Antietam battlefield near Sharpsburg, Maryland, from the Joseph Poffenberger farm and Miller’s Cornfield south to the Dunker Church plateau, with the East and West Woods on either flank

ANTIETAM (Part the Sixth)

Soon after daybreak, Joseph Hooker opened the Battle of Antietam by sending the United States I Corps south toward the Dunker Church. James B. Ricketts’s division attacked through the Cornfield and East Woods in separate brigade actions. Alexander Lawton’s Confederates, supported by Stephen D. Lee’s artillery and a counterattack by Harry T. Hays’s Louisiana brigade, stopped the advance. By about 7 a.m. the fighting in the eastern Cornfield and East Woods had reached a costly stalemate; Abner Doubleday’s simultaneous attack along the Hagerstown Turnpike continues in the next episode

Figures reported in this guide
ScopeReported figure
United States opening forceAbout 8,600 men in I Corps in the episode’s estimateHooker’s three divisions opened the northern attack before Mansfield’s XII Corps could provide effective support.
Confederate opening forceAbout 7,700 men under Jackson in the episode’s estimateThe force included most of Alexander Lawton’s and John R. Jones’s divisions, with John Bell Hood’s two brigades initially in reserve.
Ricketts’s lead brigadeAbout 1,100 men under Abram DuryeaDuryea’s New York and Pennsylvania regiments entered the head-high corn at about 6 a.m. without the close support Ricketts intended.
12th Massachusetts224 killed or wounded of 334 engaged, about 67 percentThe National Park Service identifies this as the highest percentage loss of any United States regiment at Antietam. The episode describes only 32 men initially remaining with the colors when the regiment withdrew.
Louisiana brigade323 casualties among about 550 men, about 59 percentThe episode rounds the brigade’s loss to 61 percent. National Park Service figures list 323 men shot down and every regimental commander hit during about 30 minutes of fighting.

Important leaders killed or wounded

Confederacy

Col. Marcellus Douglass

Commander of Lawton’s brigade, Ewell’s division. Killed while directing his Georgians in the fighting south of the Cornfield; the National Park Service account says rifle fire wounded him before an artillery shell killed him.

United States

Brig. Gen. George L. Hartsuff

Commander of 3rd Brigade, Ricketts’s division, I Corps. Wounded by a shell fragment before his brigade entered the main fight; Col. Richard Coulter assumed command and led it forward.
