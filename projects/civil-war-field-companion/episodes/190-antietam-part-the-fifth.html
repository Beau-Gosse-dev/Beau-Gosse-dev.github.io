Episode 190 · September 16–17, 1862 · The northern Antietam battlefield between the Upper Bridge and Pry Ford, the Miller farm, Smoketown Road, and the East Woods north of Sharpsburg, Maryland

ANTIETAM (Part the Fifth)

Joseph Hooker’s I Corps crossed Antietam Creek on September 16 and advanced against Robert E. Lee’s left. George G. Meade’s Pennsylvania Reserves fought John B. Hood’s Confederates around the East Woods until darkness. The United States troops retained an advanced foothold, Joseph K. F. Mansfield’s XII Corps crossed near midnight to support them, and both sides prepared to renew the battle at dawn

ScopeReported figure
Confederate skirmish screenAbout 100 men of the 4th Texas occupied a fence-line positionHood sent skirmishers ahead of the main division. The episode places this detachment beyond the East Woods with a clear field of fire toward the approaching Federals.
Bucktail losses in the episode29 killed and 65 wounded during the evening advanceThe episode attributes these figures to the movement across the open ground. Regimental and army returns often combine casualties from September 16 and 17, so totals vary by accounting method.
Important Confederate lossCol. Phillip F. Liddell, 11th Mississippi, mortally woundedThe transcript renders his name as “Philip Little.” Historical records identify Col. Phillip Frank Liddell, wounded on September 16 and dead at Shepherdstown on September 25.

Confederacy

Army of Northern VirginiaGen. Robert E. LeeReacted to Stuart’s warning by directing that Hood’s division move north to protect the army’s left

Law’s Brigade, Hood’s DivisionCol. Evander M. LawHeld the division right toward the East Woods; the 11th Mississippi lost Col. Phillip F. Liddell in the evening action

Important leaders killed or wounded

United States

Col. Hugh W. McNeil

Commander, 13th Pennsylvania Reserves (First Pennsylvania Rifles or Bucktails). Shot in the chest and killed while calling the regiment forward near the East Woods on September 16; the Bucktails carried the contested fence line after he fell

Confederacy

Col. Phillip F. Liddell

Commander, 11th Mississippi Infantry, Law’s Brigade, Hood’s Division. Mortally wounded while defending the Confederate line south of the East Woods on September 16; died at Shepherdstown on September 25, 1862
