Episode 190 · September 16–17, 1862 · The northern Antietam battlefield between the Upper Bridge and Pry Ford, the Miller farm, Smoketown Road, and the East Woods north of Sharpsburg, Maryland

ANTIETAM (Part the Fifth)

Joseph Hooker’s I Corps crossed Antietam Creek on September 16 and advanced against Robert E. Lee’s left. George G. Meade’s Pennsylvania Reserves fought John B. Hood’s Confederates around the East Woods until darkness. The United States troops retained an advanced foothold, Joseph K. F. Mansfield’s XII Corps crossed near midnight to support them, and both sides prepared to renew the battle at dawn
Episode scopeThe opening infantry fight at Antietam on Tuesday evening, September 16The episode backtracks from the planned dawn attack to examine Hooker’s crossing, the East Woods skirmish, and the wet night before the main battle.
Hooker’s ordersCross the Antietam, incline left, and feel for Lee’s left flankHooker received the order between 1 and 2 p.m. His official report says he was to attack the Confederate left; the episode emphasizes that McClellan supplied few additional details.
I Corps strengthAbout 12,000–13,000 in Hooker’s official reportHooker told McClellan that his corps had already lost nearly 1,000 men at South Mountain and asked for prompt reinforcement.
Crossing beginsHooker’s three divisions started across at about 4 p.m.The episode’s time is consistent with the Army staff-ride chronology, which places the crossing at approximately 3:30–4 p.m.
Upper BridgeGeorge G. Meade’s and James B. Ricketts’s divisions crossed hereMeade’s Pennsylvania Reserves led the corps and then turned across country toward the Confederate left.
Pry FordAbner Doubleday’s division crossed just downstreamThe ford had been located and improved during the approach to Sharpsburg. It is distinct from the Pry House, McClellan’s battlefield headquarters farther south.
Hooker’s approachGain the divide, then incline south toward the Confederate flankMeade advanced first. Hooker’s official report describes the initial direction as nearly perpendicular to Antietam Creek before the corps turned left.
Northern battlefield triangleHagerstown Pike, Smoketown Road, and Dunker ChurchThe roads met near the church. The Miller cornfield occupied much of the ground between them, with the West Woods on the pike side and the East Woods along the Federal approach.
Miller’s cornfieldAbout 30 acres in the episode’s descriptionThe field extended east from the Hagerstown Pike toward the East Woods and became the principal landmark of the next morning’s fighting.
Confederate warningJ. E. B. Stuart’s mounted sentries detected Hooker’s movementThe episode traces the report from vedettes near the Upper Bridge to Stuart at Dunker Church, then to Lee, Longstreet, and Jackson in Sharpsburg.
Lee’s responseJohn B. Hood’s division moved north to protect the army’s leftLee directed Longstreet to send Hood. Once in the northern sector, Hood operated in Stonewall Jackson’s area of responsibility.
Hood’s command arrangementA Longstreet division temporarily taking orders from JacksonHood’s two brigades were commanded by Col. William T. Wofford and Col. Evander M. Law. The episode uses the transfer to illustrate Lee’s flexible battlefield command structure.
Hood’s initial lineFrom Hagerstown Pike along the south edge of the cornfield into the East WoodsThe exhausted division had been resting near Dunker Church before the long roll called it into line.
Condition of Hood’s menFatigued, hungry, and living largely on corn and green applesThey had marched hard through the Maryland Campaign and had not received a proper meal. The episode connects the poor diet with widespread illness in the ranks.
Long-range Federal artilleryHeavy guns fired from the high ground east of Antietam CreekThe episode identifies 20-pounder Parrott rifles in the Army of the Potomac’s artillery reserve near the Pry House. Their range allowed them to reach Hood’s movement north of Dunker Church.
Confederate skirmish screenAbout 100 men of the 4th Texas occupied a fence-line positionHood sent skirmishers ahead of the main division. The episode places this detachment beyond the East Woods with a clear field of fire toward the approaching Federals.
Federal advance guardThe 13th Pennsylvania Reserves, the BucktailsHooker personally selected Col. Hugh W. McNeil’s regiment as skirmishers. Four companies led on both sides of Smoketown Road while six followed in support.
Bucktail equipmentSharps breech-loading riflesThe rifles could be loaded while the men lay prone, letting the regiment keep firing from shallow furrows as it crossed exposed ground.
ContactThe 4th Texas opened fire at about 6 p.m.After roughly fifteen minutes of exchanging fire, McNeil ordered the Bucktails forward across the plowed field toward the fence.
McNeil’s deathKilled while leading the final rushThe episode says McNeil was shot in the chest as he called the Bucktails forward. His men carried the fence and pursued the Confederate skirmishers into the East Woods.
Bucktail losses in the episode29 killed and 65 wounded during the evening advanceThe episode attributes these figures to the movement across the open ground. Regimental and army returns often combine casualties from September 16 and 17, so totals vary by accounting method.
Confederate counter5th Texas and 18th Georgia checked the Bucktails in the woodsCapt. Ike Turner led the 5th Texas forward as the 4th Texas fell back; the 18th Georgia advanced on its left.
Seymour reinforcesThe rest of First Brigade secured a Federal footholdTruman Seymour sent the 1st, 2nd, 5th, and 6th Pennsylvania Reserves forward after the Bucktails disappeared into the trees.
Meade deploys the divisionMagilton’s and Anderson’s brigades moved up in supportMeade’s remaining brigades formed and advanced while Capt. Dunbar R. Ransom’s Battery C, 5th U.S. Artillery, enfiladed Confederate infantry and artillery near the cornfield.
End of the evening fightDarkness stopped the organized musketry and cannon fireThe United States held part of the East Woods. Hood withdrew toward the West Woods, while the opposing pickets remained close enough to exchange fire through the damp night.
XII Corps reinforcementMansfield crossed shortly before midnightThe corps moved into a supporting bivouac north and rear of Hooker. The episode places the end of its confused, rain-soaked night march at about 2 a.m.
Casualty reporting limitNo complete separate total survives for the September 16 clashThe episode explains that many Confederate reports merged losses from the evening and the next day. The guide therefore does not present a combined battlefield total for this preliminary action.
Contested interpretationDid Hooker’s movement sacrifice surprise or draw troops north?Longstreet and Stephen W. Sears argued that the crossing revealed the Federal approach. The hosts contend that it fit McClellan’s plan to pressure both flanks before striking Lee’s center. That defense is the episode’s interpretation, not a settled fact.
Important Confederate lossCol. Phillip F. Liddell, 11th Mississippi, mortally woundedThe transcript renders his name as “Philip Little.” Historical records identify Col. Phillip Frank Liddell, wounded on September 16 and dead at Shepherdstown on September 25.
Recommended field guide“Antietam Expedition Guide” by TravelBrains and the History ChannelThe hosts recommend the self-guided audio tour and accompanying booklet for a battlefield visit.
Transcript normalizationsLiddell, Magilton, Pry Ford, Hagerstown Pike, Smoketown Road, Dunker Church, Sharps rifle, Parrott rifle, and PoffenbergerThe automated transcript produces phonetic or variant forms including “Philip Little,” “Magelton,” “Sharp’s,” and “Parrot.” This guide uses standard historical spellings.

What happened

The episode returns to Tuesday evening

Hooker’s I Corps has crossed Antietam Creek and is moving toward the northern end of Lee’s line.

McClellan’s order is summarized

Hooker is told to cross, incline left, move south toward Sharpsburg, and feel for the Confederate flank.

I Corps starts across

The episode places the beginning of the three-division crossing at about 4 p.m.

The crossings divide by route

Meade and Ricketts use the Upper Bridge while Doubleday crosses downstream at Pry Ford.

Hooker seeks clarification

He rides to McClellan’s headquarters, receives no more precise direction, and is promised command of any reinforcements sent to him.

Meade leads the probe

The Pennsylvania Reserves advance toward the East Woods at the head of I Corps.

The road triangle is drawn

Hagerstown Pike and Smoketown Road meet near Dunker Church, framing the northern battlefield.

The cornfield fills the center

The episode locates David R. Miller’s field between the roads and the two woodlots.

Stuart’s sentries spot Hooker

Mounted vedettes near the Upper Bridge report the Federal movement through Stuart to Lee.

Lee sends Hood north

Hood’s division is shifted from Longstreet’s command area to meet the threat on Jackson’s front.

Hood’s two brigades form

Wofford’s and Law’s brigades move beyond Dunker Church into line along the cornfield and East Woods.

Exhaustion and hunger are described

Hood’s men have marched hard since Hagerstown and subsisted largely on corn and green apples.

Heavy Federal guns open

Long-range artillery from the high ground near the Pry House shells Hood’s movement.

The 4th Texas takes the skirmish line

About 100 Texans move to a fence-line position ahead of Hood’s main force.

Hooker selects the Bucktails

The 13th Pennsylvania Reserves advances toward the East Woods as Meade’s skirmish screen.

Col. Hugh W. McNeil is introduced

The episode explains his experience, the regiment’s bucktail badge, and its reputation for marksmanship.

Breech-loading rifles matter

The Bucktails carry Sharps rifles, which soon let them reload while prone in the furrows.

The skirmish formation advances

Four Bucktail companies lead on both sides of Smoketown Road while six companies follow in reserve.

The first infantry fire begins

At about 6 p.m., the waiting 4th Texas skirmishers fire and the Bucktails answer.

McNeil orders an advance

The Pennsylvanians move across the exposed plowed field after roughly fifteen minutes of skirmishing.

The Bucktails go to ground

Intense fire stops a conventional advance about 75 yards from the fence, so the men fire prone and move in short rushes.

McNeil leads the charge and falls

He calls the Bucktails forward, is shot in the chest, and dies as the regiment surges toward the fence.

The Bucktails take the fence

The regiment drives back the Confederate skirmishers and continues into the East Woods.

The Confederate counter begins

Ike Turner’s 5th Texas and the 18th Georgia help stop and push back the Bucktails in the fading light.

Seymour sends in First Brigade

The 1st, 2nd, 5th, and 6th Pennsylvania Reserves advance to support the Bucktails.

The Federal foothold holds

Seymour’s brigade becomes closely engaged and retains an advanced position in the East Woods.

Ransom’s battery enfilades the Confederates

Battery C, 5th U.S. Artillery, fires from the woods’ edge into Confederate infantry and guns near the cornfield, forcing the artillery to shift.

Darkness ends the organized fight

Musketry and cannon fire subside after the United States advance has secured ground west of the creek.

Hooker asks for support

Concerned about his isolated corps, Hooker tells McClellan that prompt reinforcements are essential.

Mansfield is ordered across

McClellan sends XII Corps to support Hooker on the west side of Antietam Creek.

XII Corps struggles through the rain

New regiments stumble through the dark and finally halt in bivouac at about 2 a.m.

Hooker promises a dawn attack

He reports that the assault will resume when there is enough light to distinguish friend from foe.

The casualty record remains incomplete

The episode explains why many Confederate reports combine losses from Tuesday evening and Wednesday.

Longstreet judges the preliminary fight

He later argues that the Federal gain was limited and that Hooker’s advance disclosed McClellan’s purpose.

Sears criticizes the loss of surprise

The episode presents Stephen W. Sears’s argument that Lee used the warning to strengthen his left.

The hosts challenge that interpretation

They argue that Hooker’s movement drew Confederate strength north as McClellan’s broader plan intended.

The episode explains its backtrack

The hosts decided that the September 16 action deserved fuller treatment before the main battle began.

McNeil and Liddell define the stakes

The deaths of the Bucktail colonel and the 11th Mississippi’s mortally wounded commander show that the preliminary fight was not minor to its participants.

The field-guide recommendation closes the episode

The hosts recommend the Antietam Expedition Guide for its audio tour and battlefield booklet.
