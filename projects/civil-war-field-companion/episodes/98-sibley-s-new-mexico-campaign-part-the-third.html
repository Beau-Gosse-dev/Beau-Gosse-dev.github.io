SIBLEY'S NEW MEXICO CAMPAIGN (Part the Third)

Henry Sibley bypassed Fort Craig, occupied Albuquerque and Santa Fe, and sent troops toward Fort Union. The 1st Colorado Volunteers reached Fort Union before the Confederates. On March 26, John Chivington’s 418-man advance guard defeated Charles Pyron’s smaller force at Apache Canyon, captured about 70 Texans, and withdrew to await the main battle

What happened

Fort Craig remained in U.S. hands

The Confederate victory at Valverde did not capture the fort or its supplies. Canby still had a strong force behind the fortifications.

Sibley decided to continue north

With only about five days of provisions and no way to assault or besiege Fort Craig, the Confederates chose to move toward Albuquerque and Santa Fe.

Canby stayed at Fort Craig

Canby kept a strong force astride the Confederate line of communications and protected the fort’s supplies instead of immediately pursuing Sibley.

Canby ordered the northern depots emptied

Couriers told officers at Albuquerque and Santa Fe to move supplies to Fort Union and burn what they could not carry.

Pyron reached Albuquerque

Charles Pyron took 200 men ahead of the main column. He entered Albuquerque on March 2, one day after U.S. troops removed or burned most federal stores.

U.S. troops evacuated Santa Fe

The quartermaster loaded 120 wagons and moved the supplies, garrison, and territorial government toward Fort Union on March 4. Stores left behind were destroyed.

Confederates entered Santa Fe

The advance entered on March 10. Pyron arrived three days later and raised the Confederate flag over the Palace of the Governors.

Scurry moved toward the Santa Fe Trail

The 4th Texas and part of the 7th Texas bypassed Santa Fe and moved northeast toward the road to Fort Union. Sibley and Green remained at Albuquerque.

Fort Union held the main federal supply depot

The post stood where the Mountain and Cimarron branches of the Santa Fe Trail joined. Supplies reached it over a route of more than 700 miles from Missouri and Kansas.

Gabriel Paul prepared Fort Union

Colonel Gabriel R. Paul assumed command of troops and depots outside Canby’s immediate control and received orders to hold Fort Union at any cost.

Reinforcements started from California and Colorado

James H. Carleton organized a California column at Fort Yuma. The 1st Colorado Volunteers received orders to march south from Denver.

The 1st Colorado began its march

John P. Slough’s regiment left Camp Weld on February 22 and covered more than 300 miles to Fort Union in 13 days.

The Coloradans made a forced march

After learning that Albuquerque and Santa Fe had fallen, the regiment covered 92 miles in its final 36 hours and reached Fort Union on March 10.

Slough took command from Paul

Slough’s volunteer commission predated Paul’s colonelcy, making Slough the senior officer despite Paul’s greater regular-army experience.

Slough advanced from Fort Union

Against Paul’s interpretation of Canby’s orders, Slough left a small garrison and marched about 1,350 Colorado, regular, and New Mexico troops west on March 22.

Pyron moved east with about 400 men

Unaware that the Colorado regiment had reached Fort Union, Pyron expected to meet only part of the post’s old garrison.

Chivington entered Apache Canyon

On March 26, Chivington’s 418-man advance guard captured a Confederate scouting party and then encountered Pyron’s guns and mounted troops.

U.S. troops flanked the first Confederate position

Chivington sent infantry and dismounted cavalry onto the wooded slopes. Their fire forced Pyron to withdraw about a mile and a half.

Pyron formed a second defensive line

The Confederates destroyed a log bridge, placed guns on the road, and put skirmishers on the slopes. Chivington again sent troops around both flanks.

Colorado cavalry broke the second position

A mounted charge down the road reached the Confederate line. Pyron withdrew toward Johnson’s Ranch, and Chivington ended the pursuit near dark.

Apache Canyon ended in a U.S. victory

The U.S. force captured about 70 Confederates. Both sides arranged a truce to recover casualties, and Chivington withdrew to Pigeon’s Ranch.

Scurry reinforced Pyron

William R. Scurry marched through the night with the 4th Texas and part of the 7th Texas, reached Johnson’s Ranch early on March 27, and took command of about 1,000 men.

Both main forces prepared to fight

Scurry left his wagon train at Johnson’s Ranch and advanced on March 28. Slough had joined Chivington at Kozlowski’s Ranch and planned to meet him in Glorieta Pass.
