Episode 482 · May 5–6, 1864 · Brock Road–Orange Plank Road intersection, Tapp Field, and the Wilderness of Spotsylvania, Virginia

THE WILDERNESS (Part the Fourth)

A.P. Hill’s corps stopped Hancock’s attacks late on May 5 but did not reorganize or build strong works overnight. Hancock attacked at 5 a.m. on May 6 and drove Hill’s divisions west to Tapp Field. Longstreet’s arriving corps counterattacked and pushed the U.S. troops partway back. Burnside did not reach Hill’s flank in time. By 10 a.m., Confederate engineers had found the unfinished railroad that provided a route to Hancock’s exposed left

What happened

Getty and Hancock attacked near Brock Road

Getty’s VI Corps division opened the fighting at the intersection while Hancock brought II Corps divisions into position behind it.

Mott’s division collapsed

Two Confederate volleys stopped Gershom Mott’s under-strength division. Expiring enlistments and heavy casualties contributed to the breakdown.

Birney’s brigades entered separately

David Birney’s division supported Getty one brigade at a time and suffered heavy losses, including brigade commander Alexander Hays.

Heth faced a much larger force

Heth had about 6,500 men in line. Hancock already had roughly 17,000 engaged and two additional divisions approaching.

Lee recalled Wilcox

At 5 p.m., Lee ordered Cadmus Wilcox’s division back from its connection with Ewell to reinforce Heth along Orange Plank Road.

Wilcox counterattacked brigade by brigade

His arriving formations moved toward the heaviest firing without clear visibility or coordination in the woods.

McGowan’s brigade charged

Five South Carolina regiments took heavy U.S. fire, suffered fire from Heth’s troops behind them, briefly captured a gun, and withdrew.

The counterattacks slowed Hancock

Although they failed tactically, the repeated Confederate attacks made U.S. commanders focus on holding Brock and Orange Plank roads.

Grant misidentified Wilcox’s movement

Signalmen saw a Confederate column moving south. Grant believed it came from Ewell and ordered pressure against Ewell’s supposedly weakened line.

Wadsworth moved against Hill’s left

Grant sent James Wadsworth’s division through a mile of thickets and swampy ground toward the Confederate flank.

Wadsworth reached the sector at 7 p.m.

His leading troops threatened Hill’s left, but a charge by a 150-man Alabama battalion guarding prisoners caused them to withdraw.

Hancock stopped attacking for the night

II Corps began building fieldworks along Brock Road. Hill’s exhausted troops rested instead of fully reorganizing and entrenching.

Lee expected Longstreet by midnight

Lee planned a major counterattack after Longstreet’s fresh corps arrived, while Grant and Meade prepared to attack Hill at dawn.

Grant and Meade planned the May 6 attack

Hancock, Getty, and Wadsworth would attack west on Orange Plank Road while Burnside moved into the gap against Hill’s flank and rear.

Burnside’s independent status complicated control

Because IX Corps reported directly to Grant rather than Meade, Meade doubted it could reach its position on time. Grant delayed the start only to 5 a.m.

Ewell attacked at 4:45 a.m.

The Confederate attack made little progress but disrupted the planned U.S. advance on the Orange Turnpike.

The U.S. Turnpike attack did not develop

Sedgwick made a limited two-brigade attack, and Warren refused to leave his works until the whole VI Corps advanced.

Hancock attacked at 5 a.m.

Five divisions advanced west against Heth and Wilcox, whose tired and disorganized brigades could not hold.

Six of eight Confederate brigades withdrew

Hill’s line collapsed within minutes, sending soldiers back through Tapp Field and past Lee’s headquarters.

Sixteen guns defended Tapp Field

Confederate artillery fired canister after the retreating infantry cleared its front, but the guns lacked infantry support.

Burnside was not ready to attack

Hancock learned that IX Corps had not reached Hill’s flank and concluded that timely support could have destroyed Hill’s position.

IX Corps halted after a forced march

Burnside’s men had marched 40 miles in 36 hours with about four hours of rest and stopped near Germanna Ford while the battle continued.

Longstreet’s corps arrived

After marching for 35 of the previous 40 hours, Longstreet’s troops came east on Orange Plank Road and entered the fight by brigade.

The Texas Brigade went forward at Tapp Field

Lee tried to accompany John Gregg’s brigade. The soldiers stopped him and about 800 Texans advanced without him; fewer than 250 remained standing by day’s end.

Longstreet stopped Hancock’s advance

Fresh Confederate divisions attacked in separate waves and pushed the disordered U.S. troops about halfway back to their morning position.

Hancock requested reinforcements

At 7:10 a.m., he reported heavy pressure, refilled cartridge boxes, and rebuilt his line.

False reports worried Hancock’s left

Reports of Confederates near Todd’s Tavern caused Hancock to hold back much of two divisions even though Pickett was near Richmond and Anderson was still approaching.

Burnside stalled near the Tuning farm

Two IX Corps divisions stopped before Confederate-held high ground while commanders considered how to continue toward Parker’s Store.

Lee gave Anderson’s division to Longstreet

With the immediate crisis contained, Lee reinforced Longstreet and searched for a way to regain the initiative.

Martin Smith found the unfinished railroad

The Confederate chief engineer reported an unguarded route through the woods toward Hancock’s left.

Sorrel received the flank-attack assignment

Longstreet ordered his chief of staff to collect brigades south of the road and prepare them to strike together.
