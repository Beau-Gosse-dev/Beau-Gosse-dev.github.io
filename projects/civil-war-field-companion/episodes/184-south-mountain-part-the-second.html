SOUTH MOUNTAIN (Part the Second)

Morning United States success at Fox’s Gap. By shortly after noon on September 14, Jacob D. Cox’s Kanawha Division had broken Samuel Garland’s brigade and occupied the pass. Cox then halted to consolidate and await reinforcements. The episode ends during that lull, before the later fighting at Fox’s, Turner’s, Frosttown, and Crampton’s gaps decided the Battle of South Mountain
Episode scopeMcClellan’s September 13 plan, Lee’s emergency response, and the morning fight at Fox’s Gap on September 14The episode ends early in the afternoon as both armies reinforce the mountain. The rest of the battle is reserved for the next episode.
Battle dateSunday, September 14, 1862South Mountain was the first major battle of the Maryland Campaign and was fought three days before Antietam.
Operational crisisThe Army of Northern Virginia remained divided while the Army of the Potomac advanced west from FrederickLee’s detachments around Harpers Ferry had not completed their assignment. McClellan’s possession of Special Orders No. 191 revealed the separation and created an opportunity to defeat the Confederate columns before they reunited.
South Mountain barrierA north–south ridge crossed by a limited number of usable gapsThe National Road crossed at Turner’s Gap. The Old Sharpsburg Road crossed at Fox’s Gap less than a mile to the south, and the Burkittsville Road crossed at Crampton’s Gap about six miles farther south.
McClellan’s main movementBurnside’s Right Wing toward Turner’s and Fox’s gaps, supported by Sumner’s Center WingJesse Reno’s IX Corps led the advance, with Joseph Hooker’s I Corps following. McClellan accompanied this part of the army.
McClellan’s decisive assignmentWilliam B. Franklin’s VI Corps through Crampton’s GapFranklin was to enter Pleasant Valley, threaten the rear of Lafayette McLaws’s force at Maryland Heights, help relieve Harpers Ferry, and either turn north or block a Confederate withdrawal as circumstances required.
Federal estimate errorMcClellan incorrectly estimated roughly 60,000 Confederates near BoonsboroHe expected Longstreet to be near Boonsboro, although Longstreet had gone to Hagerstown and D. H. Hill’s division formed the rear guard.
Stuart’s initial assessmentCrampton’s Gap appeared to be the greater dangerJ. E. B. Stuart believed McClellan’s main purpose was to relieve Harpers Ferry. He told D. H. Hill that one brigade should be enough at Turner’s Gap and then rode south.
Warning from the campfiresAlfred Colquitt recognized a major Federal concentrationOn the night of September 13, Colquitt saw thousands of Federal campfires around Middletown and asked Hill for help at Turner’s Gap.
Lee changes courseLongstreet was ordered back from Hagerstown and Hill was ordered to direct the defenseLee had not intended to make a stand on South Mountain. The rapid Federal advance forced him to hold the gaps long enough to protect the separated portions of his army.
Threat to McLawsA Federal breakthrough at Crampton’s Gap could cut him offLafayette McLaws commanded Confederate troops on Maryland Heights as part of the Harpers Ferry operation. Lee repeatedly warned him to watch his rear.
Hill’s rear guardFive veteran brigades, about 7,000–8,000 men in the episodeHill’s division had been posted near Boonsboro under Special Orders No. 191. Much of it was not yet at the gaps when he reached Turner’s Gap around 5:30 a.m.
Initial defenseTwo brigades and two batteries for a front nearly five miles wideHill initially had the brigades of Alfred Colquitt and Samuel Garland and the batteries of John Lane and Jefferson W. Bondurant while also accounting for Orr’s, Frosttown, Turner’s, and Fox’s gaps.
Fox’s Gap outpostAbout 250 cavalrymen and two guns before Garland arrivedThomas L. Rosser’s 5th Virginia Cavalry detachment and two guns commanded by John Pelham held the gap. The Daniel Wise farm stood at the summit amid wooded, broken terrain.
Garland’s forceAbout 1,100 fit men in five North Carolina regimentsThe brigade was stretched across difficult ground to cover multiple approaches. Hill ordered Garland to hold Fox’s Gap while Colquitt defended closer to Turner’s Gap.
Morning attack beginsAbout 9:00 a.m. at Fox’s GapThe 23rd Ohio of Eliakim P. Scammon’s brigade, Cox’s Kanawha Division, encountered the 5th North Carolina on Garland’s right.
Rutherford B. Hayes woundedShot through the left arm above the elbowThe lieutenant colonel of the 23rd Ohio was hit while ordering a charge. Lt. Benjamin Jackson helped move him to cover. Hayes retained a stiff arm but avoided amputation and became the nineteenth United States president in 1877.
Samuel Garland killedMortally wounded by a bullet through the chestGarland was struck while directing his brigade near the Wise farm after Col. Thomas Ruffin of the 13th North Carolina was wounded in the hip. Col. Duncan K. McRae assumed brigade command.
Confederate line breaksAbout 11:30 a.m.Federal troops pressed Garland’s depleted regiments from the front and around their flanks. The brigade withdrew to avoid encirclement.
Fox’s Gap occupiedShortly after noonCox’s division held the summit after roughly three hours of fighting. The action wrecked Garland’s brigade but also exhausted the Federal attackers.
Cox pausesConsolidate the captured ground and wait for reinforcementsConfederate prisoners reported that more of Hill’s division and Longstreet’s command were near. Cox did not immediately turn north against Turner’s Gap.
Confederate reinforcementsRodes and Ripley reached the mountain during the lullHill sent Robert E. Rodes north of the National Road and Roswell S. Ripley toward Fox’s Gap after deciding that the Federal threat required more of his division.
Episode-end situationThe United States held Fox’s Gap, but the battle remained unresolvedFederal and Confederate columns were still arriving. Fighting later resumed at Fox’s Gap and expanded around Frosttown, Turner’s, and Crampton’s gaps.
Recommended book“Lee’s Maverick General: Daniel Harvey Hill” by Hal BridgesThe episode recommends the biography for D. H. Hill’s career and perspective.

What happened

The battle date and purpose are introduced

The hosts identify September 14, 1862, as the date of South Mountain and place the battle on the road to Antietam.

The Lost Order changes the campaign

Special Orders No. 191 reaches McClellan on September 13 and reveals that Lee’s army is divided.

Both commanders face a deadline

McClellan must cross South Mountain before Lee can reunite his separated columns.

South Mountain becomes the physical barrier

The north–south ridge west of Frederick channels army movement through a limited number of gaps.

Turner’s, Fox’s, and Crampton’s gaps are located

The episode connects each pass to its road and explains that Crampton’s Gap opens into Pleasant Valley behind Harpers Ferry.

McClellan forms his plan

The Army of the Potomac commander uses the information in the Lost Order to plan attacks for September 14.

Burnside’s Right Wing takes the northern route

Reno’s IX Corps leads toward Turner’s Gap with Hooker’s I Corps following and Sumner’s Center Wing in support.

Franklin receives the key southern assignment

VI Corps is to move through Burkittsville, force Crampton’s Gap, and enter Pleasant Valley.

McClellan states his objective

His instruction to Franklin describes a plan to split the enemy and defeat the separated parts.

Lee grows concerned at Hagerstown

Reports from the mountain show that Federal cavalry and infantry are closing faster than expected.

Stuart is driven back to the ridge

Federal cavalry and the front of IX Corps push Confederate horsemen through Middletown toward South Mountain.

The Harpers Ferry delay becomes clear

Stuart learns that the Confederate army remains divided and reports the threat to Lee and Hill.

Only Colquitt is sent to Turner’s Gap

Stuart’s belief that Crampton’s Gap is the primary Federal objective leaves one brigade initially guarding the National Road.

Campfires reveal the scale of the threat

Colquitt looks east from the mountain, sees the Federal concentration around Middletown, and asks Hill for reinforcements.

Garland and two batteries move up

Hill sends Garland’s North Carolinians and the batteries of Lane and Bondurant toward the threatened gaps.

Lee recognizes the danger

The Federal advance is faster than Lee expected while his forces remain separated around Hagerstown, Boonsboro, and Harpers Ferry.

Longstreet is ordered back

Lee directs Longstreet to march from Hagerstown toward Boonsboro at first light so his troops can reinforce the gaps.

Hill must personally defend Turner’s Gap

Lee orders his rear-guard commander to oversee the mountain defense that had not been part of the original campaign plan.

Hill rides from Boonsboro before sunrise

The episode follows Hill to the summit as he assumes responsibility for protecting the divided army.

A nearly five-mile front comes into view

Hill must account for Orr’s, Frosttown, Turner’s, and Fox’s gaps with few troops immediately available.

Garland is sent to Fox’s Gap

Hill directs the 31-year-old brigadier to hold the southern pass and its road approaches.

Rosser and Pelham hold the outpost

Garland finds a detachment of the 5th Virginia Cavalry and two guns already at Fox’s Gap.

Gunfire opens around 9 a.m.

Combat begins on the right of Garland’s extended defensive line near the summit.

The 23rd Ohio meets the 5th North Carolina

Hayes’s regiment leads Scammon’s brigade and Cox’s Kanawha Division into the fight at Fox’s Gap.

Hayes is wounded during a charge

A musket ball strikes his left arm above the elbow; he remains exposed until Benjamin Jackson moves him behind a log.

Garland directs the line beside Ruffin

As the 13th North Carolina exchanges fire with the 30th Ohio, Ruffin is seriously wounded in the hip.

Samuel Garland is mortally wounded

A bullet passes through the brigade commander’s chest. He is carried from the field and dies soon afterward.

Garland’s brigade gives way

Federal pressure against the front and flanks breaks the Confederate line around 11:30 a.m.

Cox occupies Fox’s Gap

Shortly after noon, the Kanawha Division controls the pass after about three hours of fighting.

The Federal advance pauses

Exhaustion and reports of nearby Confederate reinforcements lead Cox to consolidate rather than immediately turn north.

Rodes and Ripley reach the mountain

Hill deploys new Confederate brigades north of the National Road and toward Fox’s Gap as the noon lull continues.

Hill explains the purpose of delay

He intends to hold long enough for Longstreet to arrive and for Jackson to finish the Harpers Ferry operation.

The episode recommends Hal Bridges’s biography

The hosts recommend “Lee’s Maverick General: Daniel Harvey Hill.”
