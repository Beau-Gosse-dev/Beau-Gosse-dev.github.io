Episode 511 · May 13–16, 1864 · Resaca and Lay’s Ferry, Georgia

THE ATLANTA CAMPAIGN (Part the Tenth)

The battle was tactically indecisive, but the U.S. crossing at Lay’s Ferry threatened Johnston’s railroad and forced the Confederates to withdraw south of the Oostanaula River

What happened

Sherman concentrated at Snake Creek Gap

By May 12, most U.S. forces had joined McPherson while Johnston moved south from Dalton toward Resaca.

U.S. troops met the Confederate army at Resaca

Advanced Confederate units delayed the U.S. deployment on May 13 while Johnston formed west of town.

Johnston covered the river crossings

Polk held the left near the Oostanaula, Hardee held the center, and Hood held the right toward the Conasauga River.

The position could be turned downstream

The Oostanaula protected Sherman’s right and offered a route to cross below Resaca and threaten the Western and Atlantic Railroad.

Sherman deployed three armies

McPherson formed on the U.S. right, Thomas in the center, Schofield to the left, and IV Corps on the far left.

Fighting began early on May 14

Sherman ordered Thomas Sweeney’s division downstream to find a crossing while the main line pressed the Confederate position.

U.S. attacks failed on the Confederate right-center

Assaults by XXIII and XIV Corps caused heavy U.S. losses without taking important ground.

Hood counterattacked the U.S. left

The Confederate attack initially advanced against an exposed flank before XX Corps reinforcements stopped it.

U.S. artillery gained a position above the bridges

XV Corps troops took a hill from which artillery could fire on the Resaca crossings.

Sweeney found a crossing at Lay’s Ferry

He concealed pontoon boats near the mouth of Snake Creek while most of the division demonstrated opposite the ferry.

U.S. troops crossed the Oostanaula

Soldiers of the 65th and 85th Ohio crossed in boats at 5 p.m. and drove away a small Confederate cavalry guard.

A false report ended the first crossing

Sweeney withdrew after hearing that a large Confederate force was crossing above him. The report proved incorrect.

Sherman prepared two attacks for May 15

Sweeney was ordered to cross again, while Hooker’s XX Corps moved into the U.S. left-center.

Hooker’s attack failed

Hood’s troops stopped the three U.S. divisions. Federals briefly overran a small fort held by Max Van Den Corput’s Georgia battery.

U.S. troops recovered four guns after dark

The cannon remained between the lines until soldiers removed part of the fort’s parapet and rolled them back.

Sweeney secured a second crossing

U.S. soldiers crossed in a flatboat before the planned operation. Walker’s Confederate division could not drive them back.

One Confederate attack went forward after cancellation

The order canceling Hood’s counterattack did not reach Alexander P. Stewart’s division in time. U.S. troops repulsed it with heavy losses.

Johnston ordered the evacuation of Resaca

The U.S. bridgehead at Lay’s Ferry threatened the railroad south of town. Hood withdrew first, followed by Hardee and Polk.

The Confederates completed the withdrawal

At 3:30 a.m. on May 16, the rear guard burned the railroad bridge and removed its pontoon bridge. U.S. troops captured the road bridge intact.

The campaign’s operating pattern was established

Johnston repeatedly chose prepared defensive positions, while Sherman generally used flank movements instead of large frontal attacks.
