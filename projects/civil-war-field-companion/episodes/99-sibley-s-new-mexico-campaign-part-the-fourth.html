SIBLEY'S NEW MEXICO CAMPAIGN (Part the Fourth)

Confederate troops drove John Slough’s force from the field at Glorieta Pass, but John Chivington’s detachment destroyed their wagon train and ammunition at Johnson’s Ranch. The loss forced Henry Sibley to abandon Santa Fe and Albuquerque and retreat to Texas. The last Confederate force left New Mexico in July 1862

What happened

The main forces assembled

William Scurry had about 1,000 Confederates at Johnson’s Ranch. John Slough commanded about 1,300 U.S. troops east of Glorieta Pass.

Slough divided his force

On March 28, Slough took about 850 men west on the Santa Fe Trail while Chivington led roughly 490 men south of the pass with New Mexico guides.

Scurry advanced without his wagon train

Scurry left about 80 wagons under a small guard at Johnson’s Ranch and moved east into Glorieta Pass.

The main battle began

The forces met about half a mile west of Pigeon’s Ranch at about 11 a.m. Slough placed artillery across the trail and sent skirmishers onto both slopes.

The Confederates turned Slough’s first line

Scurry stopped a U.S. flanking move at an irrigation ditch while Pyron advanced around the opposite flank. Slough withdrew toward Pigeon’s Ranch.

U.S. artillery disabled Confederate guns

Fire from Artillery Hill knocked out two Confederate pieces and sharpshooters reduced the crews of the remaining gun.

Scurry ordered a general attack

The Confederate attacks against the U.S. center and Artillery Hill failed, and Major John Shropshire was killed.

The Confederate left forced another withdrawal

Texans on the northern slopes turned the U.S. line. Henry Raguet was mortally wounded, and Slough formed a third position east of Pigeon’s Ranch.

Slough left the battlefield

After about six hours of fighting, the exhausted U.S. force withdrew to Kozlowski’s Ranch. The Confederates held the field.

Chivington destroyed the Confederate supplies

Guided by Manuel Chaves, Chivington crossed 16 miles of mountain terrain, descended on Johnson’s Ranch, and burned about 80 wagons with food, ammunition, forage, tents, and medical stores.

Both armies reported heavy losses

The episode gives U.S. losses as 47 killed and 78 wounded and Confederate losses as 42 killed and about 60 wounded.

The supply loss decided the campaign

The Confederates returned to Santa Fe with only about 20 days of food and 35 to 40 cartridges per man.

Canby advanced from Fort Craig

Canby moved north with 1,200 men on April 1 and coordinated with the Fort Union force to retake Santa Fe and pressure Albuquerque.

U.S. troops reoccupied Santa Fe

The Coloradans entered Santa Fe without opposition on April 11. The Confederates evacuated Albuquerque the next day.

Canby fought Green at Peralta

On April 15, U.S. troops pushed the 5th Texas into Peralta. A dust storm covered the Confederate withdrawal across the Rio Grande.

The Confederates bypassed Fort Craig

Sibley took a 100-mile detour west of the fort. The waterless eight-day march broke down unit organization and cost more men and equipment.

The California Column approached

James Carleton’s 2,350-man force advanced east from Fort Yuma. The last Confederate rearguard left New Mexico on July 8.

The survivors returned to Texas

Men reached San Antonio through the summer. The episode states that about 1,500 of the roughly 3,500 Texans who went west did not return.
