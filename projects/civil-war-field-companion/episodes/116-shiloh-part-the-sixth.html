Episode 116 · April 6, 1862, about 7 a.m.–noon · Savannah, Crump’s Landing, Pittsburg Landing, Shiloh Church, the Hamburg–Purdy Road, and Jones Field, Tennessee

SHILOH (Part the Sixth)

Sherman abandoned the line near Shiloh Church at about 10 a.m. and joined McClernand along the Hamburg–Purdy Road. Grant reached Pittsburg Landing at about 9 a.m., organized ammunition and straggler control, ordered Lew Wallace and William Nelson toward the battlefield, and met Sherman around 10 a.m. A renewed Confederate attack broke Marsh’s and Raith’s brigades, then forced Sherman, McClernand, and Veatch to retreat toward Jones Field. The attack lost momentum while intermingled Confederate units reorganized and soldiers searched the captured camps

What happened

Hildebrand’s brigade came apart

The 53rd, 57th, and 77th Ohio withdrew in succession as Confederate pressure spread across Sherman’s left.

Sherman abandoned Shiloh Church

He ordered Buckland and McDowell to fall back about 600 yards to the Hamburg–Purdy Road; Raith’s brigade withdrew with them.

McDowell became separated

During the retreat, McDowell’s brigade moved off course. Buckland remained Sherman’s only organized brigade, reinforced by rallied men from Hildebrand.

The second United States line formed

Sherman joined McClernand’s three brigades along the Hamburg–Purdy Road. Wallace’s division stood 200–300 yards beyond McClernand’s left.

Grant re-entered the battle narrative

He reached the Sherman–McClernand sector after arriving from Savannah and inspecting conditions around Pittsburg Landing.

Grant heard the battle from Savannah

Between 7 and 7:30 a.m., artillery interrupted breakfast at the Cherry Mansion. Grant immediately ordered preparations to leave.

The Tigress departed

Grant’s headquarters steamer traveled south against the Tennessee River’s current toward Pittsburg Landing.

Nelson was ordered toward the battlefield

Before leaving Savannah, Grant directed William “Bull” Nelson’s division down the east bank to a point opposite Pittsburg Landing.

Grant stopped at Crump’s Landing

He told Lew Wallace to hold the 3rd Division ready and continued upriver without disembarking.

Grant reached Pittsburg Landing

At about 9 a.m., he found at least 3,000 wounded men, teamsters, and stragglers gathered around the bluff.

Grant organized the landing

He used the 15th and 16th Iowa to intercept stragglers and organized an ammunition train for the firing line.

Wallace briefed Grant

W. H. L. Wallace reported the situation. Grant then ordered Lew Wallace to march and urged Nelson forward across the river.

Grant met Sherman and McClernand

At about 10 a.m., he inspected their line at the Corinth and Hamburg–Purdy crossroads before continuing along the army’s front.

The Confederate attack resumed

At about 11 a.m., brigades from three corps advanced without an overall tactical commander against Sherman and McClernand.

Marsh’s brigade was attacked first

Wood’s and Shaver’s brigades placed eleven regiments or battalions against Marsh’s four Illinois regiments near Review Field.

Barlston identified the approaching force

Major Frederick Barlston rode forward, was wounded, and returned warning that the troops moving through the low ground were Confederates.

Marsh’s regiments lost leaders rapidly

The 48th and 11th Illinois lost commanders and other officers during a few minutes of close-range fire, and the brigade broke.

Raith’s brigade broke at the same time

Poor positioning, heavy casualties, and the mortal wounding of Julius Raith contributed to the collapse.

Veatch’s brigade covered the retreat

Four regiments from Hurlbut’s division briefly held after Sherman and McClernand withdrew, but unsupported flanks forced Veatch back.

The Hamburg–Purdy Road line was gone

By about 11:30 a.m., the 1st and 5th Divisions were retreating north and beginning to rally around Jones Field.

The Confederate advance paused

Casualties, intermingled formations, leader losses, and searching the captured camps stopped the pursuit on the western battlefield.
