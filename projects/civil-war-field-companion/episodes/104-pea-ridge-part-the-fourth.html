PEA RIDGE (Part the Fourth)

United States victory. Van Dorn captured Elkhorn Tavern late on March 7 but could not break Curtis’s army. What happened

The Leetown fight had removed half of Van Dorn’s army

McCulloch’s division was defeated and fragmented west of Elkhorn Tavern. Van Dorn and Price continued fighting for the Telegraph Road with about 5,000 men.

Three roads shaped the Elkhorn Tavern action

The Telegraph Road ran north and south. Huntsville Road entered near the tavern from the east, while Ford Road joined from the west about 500 yards south.

Van Dorn and Price attacked through Cross Timber Hollow

Their force moved south on the Telegraph Road and encountered Carr’s division in rugged ground immediately north of Elkhorn Tavern.

Van Dorn learned that McCulloch was heavily engaged

At about 2 p.m., an officer reported fighting on Ford Road. Van Dorn could no longer assume that McCulloch would soon reinforce the attack at the tavern.

Sterling Price was wounded

A bullet struck Price in the right arm and side. His aides bandaged the wound with handkerchiefs, and he remained on the field.

Curtis committed most of the army northward

With no Confederates appearing at Little Sugar Creek, Curtis accepted that the main threat was in his rear and ordered additional forces toward Elkhorn Tavern.

The final Confederate assault began at about 4:30 p.m.

Van Dorn directed the right and Price the left. Their troops attacked Carr’s thin line in and above Cross Timber Hollow before United States reinforcements could fully arrive.

Carr withdrew south of Elkhorn Tavern

Confederates emerged from the hollow and threatened to surround the defenders. Carr moved to a fence line south of the tavern.

The Confederates captured the tavern but stopped

Broken formations, difficult ground, hunger, and fatigue ended the advance. Soldiers searched the tavern and outbuildings for food with only about 30 minutes of daylight remaining.

Curtis counterattacked at dusk

Curtis reached the field and directed an attack that recovered some ground but did not retake Elkhorn Tavern. The United States line remained intact.

The first day ended without a decision

The Confederates held Elkhorn Tavern, but Curtis had prevented his army from being destroyed. The fighting at Leetown had already eliminated much of Van Dorn’s striking force.

Curtis reversed the Army of the Southwest

During March 7, the army fought two separated actions, protected its wagon train, moved back about two and a half miles, and turned almost 200 degrees to face northeast.

Curtis formed all four divisions for March 8

During the night, the divisions assembled south of the Confederate position and centered their line on the Telegraph Road.

The Confederate ammunition remained on the detour

Van Dorn’s supply and ordnance train had not reached the battlefield. Artillery batteries and infantry cartridge boxes had little ammunition available for another full day.

The numerical advantage reversed

Van Dorn and Price had outnumbered Carr about two to one for much of March 7. On March 8, all four United States divisions outnumbered the remaining Confederate force about two to one.

Curtis launched the March 8 attack

About 10,000 United States troops advanced from a line nearly one mile long. Van Dorn learned that his ammunition wagons were still five or six hours away.

Van Dorn ordered a retreat

With artillery ammunition almost gone and no means to stop Curtis’s advance, Van Dorn told Price that the army had to leave the field.

The Confederate army withdrew in three groups

One group went north on the Telegraph Road, another withdrew with Martin Green’s wagon train by the Bentonville Detour, and Van Dorn and Price led the main body east on Huntsville Road.

Van Dorn arrested James Rains

Rains said that Van Dorn, rather than the soldiers, had been defeated. Van Dorn heard the remark and placed him under arrest.

The main Confederate column lacked food and shelter

Many men had gone three days without rations. The troops separated from the wagon train had few tents, blankets, or overcoats and had to cross the Boston Mountains in winter weather.

Van Dorn left the army during the retreat

Van Dorn departed with a small party and reached Van Buren two days later. Wounded Sterling Price remained responsible for moving the main column to safety.

The retreat continued for more than a week

Scattered Confederate groups crossed the Boston Mountains into the Arkansas River valley. Curtis remained near Pea Ridge for ten days before returning north to Keetsville, Missouri.

The episode gives the battle’s casualty totals

The Army of the Southwest reported 1,384 casualties, more than half in Carr’s division. Confederate losses were not officially reported and are estimated in the episode at about 2,000.

The Army of the West was ordered east

On March 25, Albert Sidney Johnston ordered Van Dorn to Corinth. The army crossed the Mississippi too late to participate at Shiloh.

Prairie Grove ended the next large Confederate attempt

Thomas Hindman built another army in northwest Arkansas later in 1862, but it was defeated at Prairie Grove in December. No organized Confederate force threatened Missouri again for two years.

Curtis was nominated for promotion

Curtis was nominated for major general on March 27. His army later moved through eastern Arkansas and reached Helena on the Mississippi River on July 12.

Pea Ridge secured the region

The victory maintained United States control of Missouri and northern Arkansas. Van Dorn’s army never again fought as a major force west of the Mississippi.
