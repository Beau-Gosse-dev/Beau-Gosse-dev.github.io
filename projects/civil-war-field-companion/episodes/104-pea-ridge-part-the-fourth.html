PEA RIDGE (Part the Fourth)

United States victory. Van Dorn captured Elkhorn Tavern late on March 7 but could not break Curtis’s army. On March 8, Curtis attacked with all four divisions while the Confederate ammunition train remained hours away. Van Dorn retreated, and Confederate forces did not regain control of northern Arkansas or Missouri
United States strength on March 8About 10,000 men in a line nearly one mile long
United States casualties1,384; more than half in Carr’s division
Estimated Confederate casualtiesAbout 2,000 in the battle, plus additional losses during the marches
Confederate supply trainFive or six hours from the battlefield when the March 8 attack began
Van Buren from Pea RidgeAbout 90 miles directly, but farther by the available retreat routes
Territory securedNorthern Arkansas and Missouri remained under United States control

Important leaders killed or wounded

Confederacy

Benjamin McCulloch

Division commander at Leetown. Killed by United States skirmishers on March 7. His death contributed to the collapse of half of Van Dorn’s army.

Confederacy

James McIntosh

Cavalry commander and McCulloch’s successor. Killed at Leetown minutes after McCulloch.

Confederacy

Sterling Price

Missouri State Guard commander. Wounded in the right arm and side near Elkhorn Tavern on March 7; remained in command.
