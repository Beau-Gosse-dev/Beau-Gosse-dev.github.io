What happened

Johnston shifted troops toward Dalton

Reports about U.S. movements led Johnston to reinforce the northern end of his line and move cavalry away from Resaca.

Canty remained at Resaca

Johnston repeatedly changed Canty’s orders but ultimately kept his infantry near Resaca while Polk’s reinforcements moved toward north Georgia.

Johnston misread McPherson’s objective

Confederate reports tracked McPherson south through LaFayette, but Johnston continued to expect an advance toward Rome.

U.S. troops attacked Rocky Face Ridge

On May 8, Oliver O. Howard’s IV Corps climbed the northern end of the ridge and met the main Confederate line.

Hooker attacked Dug Gap

Four U.S. regiments reached the pass before a Confederate counterattack drove them back.

The demonstrations fixed Johnston in place

The attacks cost about 400 U.S. casualties and gained no important ground, but they kept Confederate attention on Dalton.

McPherson occupied Snake Creek Gap

The 9th Illinois Mounted Infantry and 39th Iowa reached the unguarded pass late on May 8 without Confederate detection.

The railroad was exposed

From the gap, McPherson could move east toward Resaca and cut Johnston’s railroad supply and retreat route to Atlanta.

Confederate cavalry was sent to the gap

J. Warren Grigsby’s brigade was delayed by a guide and reached the southern entrance around dawn on May 9.

The Confederates discovered the U.S. position

Grigsby’s cavalry drove back a small mounted patrol but was stopped by U.S. infantry inside the gap.

McPherson advanced toward Resaca

Grenville Dodge’s XVI Corps led, followed by John A. Logan’s XV Corps. Bad roads and Confederate cavalry slowed the march.

The column reached the Resaca approaches

Dodge arrived at a crossroads about two miles west of town during the afternoon and detached a division to cover the northern roads.

McPherson halted before the earthworks

He did not know their strength and feared Confederate troops could arrive from Dalton against his left flank.

The U.S. column withdrew to the gap

McPherson ordered Dodge back and entrenched at the entrance instead of cutting the railroad.

A cavalry patrol reached the railroad

The patrol cut telegraph wires and burned locomotive fuel but could not disable the line.

Control of the gap still forced a Confederate response

Although the railroad remained open, Johnston could no longer safely hold Dalton while a large U.S. force occupied his rear.

Sherman shifted south

Beginning May 10, most of Thomas’s and Schofield’s troops marched west of Rocky Face Ridge to join McPherson.

Most U.S. forces reached Snake Creek Gap

By the night of May 12, only IV Corps and some cavalry remained in front of Dalton.

Polk’s reinforcements reached Resaca

The leading units from Alabama arrived on May 11. Polk became the senior subordinate in the enlarged Army of Tennessee.

Johnston finally ordered a movement to Resaca

A cavalry reconnaissance found only one U.S. corps at Dalton. Johnston then directed Hardee and Hood to move south.
