Episode 186 · September 14–15, 1862 · Crampton’s Gap and Pleasant Valley near Burkittsville, Maryland; South Mountain through Boonsboro and Keedysville to the Antietam Creek line outside Sharpsburg; and Harpers Ferry

SOUTH MOUNTAIN (Part the Fourth) / ANTIETAM (Part the First)

Decisive United States tactical victory at Crampton’s Gap and a United States victory at South Mountain, but without the immediate operational exploitation McClellan intended. Confederate resistance delayed VI Corps, Harpers Ferry surrendered on September 15, and Robert E. Lee halted his withdrawal to begin assembling the Army of Northern Virginia behind Antietam Creek near Sharpsburg

Forces and losses

Figures reported in this guide
ScopeReported figure
VI Corps strengthNearly 13,000 men in Franklin’s two divisionsThe National Park Service rounds the corps to about 12,000. Henry W. Slocum’s division made the principal assault; William F. Smith’s division supported the operation.
Confederate reinforcementHowell Cobb’s brigade, about 1,300 menLafayette McLaws sent Cobb from the Harpers Ferry operation with an order to hold the gap. The 10th Georgia from Semmes’s brigade was also directed toward the threatened sector.
United States losses at Crampton’s Gap533 killed, wounded, or missingThe transcript gives 113 killed, 418 wounded, and 2 missing. Franklin’s official report and the Army of the Potomac casualty table give 115 killed, 416 wounded, and 2 missing; the total is the same.
Confederate losses at Crampton’s GapRoughly 900 to 1,000, most of them captured or missingThe episode says losses neared 1,000 and more than 600 were prisoners. Returns are incomplete and later totals vary, but all show a severe defeat for the small defending force.

People and command

United States

Army of the PotomacMaj. Gen. George B. McClellanDirected Franklin to force Crampton’s Gap and relieve Harpers Ferry; organized the September 15 pursuit through the abandoned northern gaps
VI CorpsMaj. Gen. William B. FranklinDivisions of Henry W. Slocum and William F. Smith, plus supporting troops from IV Corps; moved slowly on both September 14 and 15 despite urgent operational objectives
1st Division, VI CorpsMaj. Gen. Henry W. SlocumBrigades of Joseph J. Bartlett, John Newton, and Alfred T. A. Torbert; formed the assault column that broke the Mountain Church Road line and carried the gap
2nd Brigade, Slocum’s DivisionCol. Joseph J. Bartlett5th Maine, 16th and 27th New York, and 96th Pennsylvania; led the advance and joined the united charge about 5:30 p.m.
2nd Division, VI CorpsMaj. Gen. William F. SmithSupported Slocum’s attack and entered Pleasant Valley after the pass was taken; the episode centers on Slocum’s division
Harpers Ferry garrisonCol. Dixon S. MilesAbout 14,000 soldiers at Harpers Ferry and Martinsburg before the siege; agreed to surrender on September 15 and was mortally wounded after white flags appeared

Confederacy

Army of Northern VirginiaGen. Robert E. LeeOrdered withdrawal from South Mountain, halted near Keedysville to cover McLaws, and formed a new line near Sharpsburg after learning Harpers Ferry would fall
McLaws’s Division and Pleasant Valley defenseMaj. Gen. Lafayette McLawsOversaw the Maryland Heights operation; sent Cobb toward Crampton’s Gap and formed Kershaw, Barksdale, Wilcox, and remnants across Pleasant Valley after the breakthrough
Southern South Mountain sectorBrig. Gen. Paul J. SemmesDivided his available regiments between Brownsville Pass and Crampton’s Gap, expecting the main Federal effort at Brownsville
Cavalry at Crampton’s GapCol. Thomas T. MunfordSenior officer at the gap; combined his horsemen with Parham’s infantry behind the Mountain Church Road stone wall
Mahone’s BrigadeCol. William A. ParhamCommanded the 6th, 12th, 16th, and 41st Virginia while William Mahone recovered from a wound; the 41st was detached nearby
Cobb’s BrigadeBrig. Gen. Howell CobbGeorgia and North Carolina regiments sent from the Harpers Ferry operation; reached the eastern slope while the original defense was breaking
Harpers Ferry operationMaj. Gen. Thomas J. “Stonewall” JacksonCoordinated McLaws, John G. Walker, and A. P. Hill around the garrison; confirmed its surrender and directed most of his force toward Sharpsburg
South Mountain withdrawal and first Sharpsburg lineMaj. Gen. James Longstreet and Maj. Gen. D. H. HillReported Turner’s Gap untenable, withdrew through Boonsboro, and formed on the high ground west of Antietam Creek

Important leaders killed or wounded

United States

Col. Roderick N. Matheson

Commander, 32nd New York Infantry, Newton’s Brigade. Mortally wounded while leading his regiment at Crampton’s Gap on September 14; died of the wound afterward

Confederacy

Lt. Col. Jefferson M. Lamar

Commander, Cobb’s Legion Infantry. Mortally wounded and captured during the Confederate collapse at Crampton’s Gap on September 14; died in Burkittsville on September 15

United States

Col. Dixon S. Miles

Commander, Harpers Ferry garrison. Mortally wounded by a Confederate shell shortly after white flags appeared on September 15; Brig. Gen. Julius White completed the surrender arrangements
