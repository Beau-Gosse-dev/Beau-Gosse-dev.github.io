Episode 107 · March 6–8, 1862 · Gosport Navy Yard, the Elizabeth River, Newport News Point, and Hampton Roads, Virginia

CLASH OF THE IRONCLADS (Part the Third)

CSS Virginia surprised the United States blockading squadron on March 8. It rammed and sank USS Cumberland, forced USS Congress to surrender and then burned it, and fired at the grounded USS Minnesota before withdrawing because of darkness and the falling tide. More than 250 United States sailors were killed. Virginia lost two men killed, and its commander Franklin Buchanan was wounded. USS Monitor arrived that night

What happened

The episode covers the first day at Hampton Roads

The March 8 fighting pitted CSS Virginia against the wooden ships of the United States blockading squadron. The ironclad duel happened the next day.

Virginia was still incomplete

Franklin Buchanan reached Gosport in late February. Workers were still finishing the ship, its powder supply was short, and the crew was incomplete.

Army volunteers filled the crew

Lieutenant John Taylor Wood obtained permission to recruit nearby Confederate soldiers. He selected 80 artillerymen and former sailors from 200 volunteers.

Deep draft restricted Buchanan’s options

Virginia drew nearly 22 feet of water. Shallows and narrow channels limited where it could maneuver in the Elizabeth River and Hampton Roads.

The first night attack was canceled

Buchanan planned to leave on March 6 and surprise the ships at Newport News, but weather, unfinished work, and the pilots’ refusal to navigate at night delayed the sortie.

The casemate received a coating of tallow

Catesby ap Roger Jones asked for time to coat the armor with animal fat in the hope that projectiles would glance away more easily.

The last powder arrived on March 7

Virginia received enough gunpowder the day before the attack.

Mallory proposed attacks beyond Hampton Roads

Stephen Mallory suggested that Virginia attack New York after breaking the blockade. The ship’s draft and poor seakeeping made such plans impractical.

Virginia left Gosport at 11 a.m.

Workers jumped to the dock as the ship moved away. Gun-port shields and internal compartments were unfinished, and the guns and engines had not been tested under operating conditions.

The approach took about ninety minutes

The navy yard was about ten miles from Hampton Roads. Virginia made five or six knots and needed tugs to help keep its bow pointed downriver.

Buchanan selected Cumberland as the first target

He believed Cumberland’s rifled stern gun posed the greatest danger and told the engineer that he intended to ram the ship.

The crews sighted each other near Sewell’s Point

Around 12:30 p.m., Virginia entered Hampton Roads and its crew saw Cumberland and Congress off Newport News.

More than sixty United States vessels were present

The roadstead held warships, transports, supply ships, tugs, tenders, and dispatch vessels. All had wooden hulls.

The senior squadron commander was absent

Louis Goldsborough was at Hatteras Inlet. Captain John Marston of USS Roanoke was the senior naval officer in Hampton Roads, but Roanoke’s propulsion machinery was under repair.

The main ships were divided

Minnesota, Roanoke, and St. Lawrence lay near Fort Monroe. Congress and Cumberland were stationed several miles west off Newport News Point.

Congress and Cumberland were vulnerable

Congress was a 50-gun sailing frigate with about 400 men. Cumberland was a 24-gun sailing sloop under executive officer George U. Morris.

The army warning did not reach the squadron

Signalers at Newport News reported smoke on the Elizabeth River, but the army command did not promptly share the warning with naval officers.

Zouave fired the opening shots

The armed tug investigated the smoke and fired six rounds from its 30-pounder Parrott rifle at about 1:20 p.m.

Buchanan ordered the crew to battle stations

He told the crew the sortie was an attack and directed the ship to eliminate Cumberland and Congress before the ships near Fort Monroe could intervene.

Virginia opened fire around 2 p.m.

The bow Brooke rifle fired at Cumberland from less than a mile. Congress answered as the range closed, but its solid shot glanced from the casemate.

Virginia’s first broadsides hit Congress

Grape and shell killed or wounded gun crews, disabled guns, and started fires. Congress cut its anchor cables and was towed into shallow water.

Virginia rammed Cumberland

At about 3 p.m., the iron ram struck Cumberland amidships and opened a large hole below the waterline. The ram broke off as Virginia backed away.

Cumberland sank while firing

The crew continued firing as the ship flooded. Two Virginia crewmen were killed at a bow gun port, and many wounded sailors aboard Cumberland drowned below deck.

Virginia required about thirty minutes to turn

After sinking Cumberland, the slow ironclad made a wide turn. Confederate gunboats from the James River joined the action.

Minnesota and St. Lawrence grounded

The large United States steam frigates attempted to move west and ran into shallow water. St. Lawrence was freed; Minnesota remained aground.

Virginia returned to Congress

At about 4 p.m., the ironclad raked the grounded frigate from roughly 150 yards. Lieutenant Joseph B. Smith was killed.

Congress surrendered

Commander William B. Smith raised white flags after about thirty minutes of renewed fire. Confederate boats and tugs moved in to accept the surrender and remove prisoners.

Shore fire interrupted the surrender

United States artillery and musket fire struck the Confederate boats. Buchanan ordered heated shot fired into Congress.

Buchanan was wounded

A rifle bullet struck his thigh while he was exposed on the upper deck. Catesby ap Roger Jones took command of Virginia.

Jones fired at Minnesota from long range

Virginia could not approach the grounded frigate because of shallow water. Darkness and the ebbing tide made continued action unsafe.

Virginia withdrew at about 6:30 p.m.

Jones took the ship toward Sewell’s Point and anchored under Confederate shore batteries around 8 p.m.

The attack destroyed two warships

Cumberland had sunk and Congress burned until its magazine exploded after midnight. The episode states that more than 250 United States sailors were killed.

Monitor arrived before Virginia returned

The new United States ironclad reached the roadstead, reported to Roanoke, and moved to protect Minnesota before the March 9 battle.
