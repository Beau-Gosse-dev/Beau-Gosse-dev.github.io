Episode 136 · May 25–31, 1862 (episode coverage ends late on May 31) · The Chickahominy River approaches east of Richmond, including Fair Oaks Station, Seven Pines, the Williamsburg Road, Nine Mile Road, Charles City Road, and White Oak Swamp, Virginia

PENINSULA CAMPAIGN: Fair Oaks (Part the First)

Joseph E. Johnston tried to attack the two United States corps south of the flooded Chickahominy before the three corps north of the river could reinforce them. Confused orders and James Longstreet’s movement onto the Williamsburg Road delayed the attack for about five hours and blocked Benjamin Huger. D. H. Hill nevertheless broke Silas Casey’s forward line, while Philip Kearny reinforced the defense and Micah Jenkins split part of the United States line. The episode stops as Johnston leads W. H. C. Whiting toward Fair Oaks; the first day and the battle remain unresolved at that point

The episode begins the battle narrative

People and command

United States

Commander, Army of the PotomacGeorge B. McClellanDirected five corps on both sides of the Chickahominy; the episode does not place him at the forward Seven Pines fighting
Commander, IV Corps; south of the ChickahominyErasmus D. KeyesSilas Casey held the forward line and redoubt; Darius N. Couch formed behind Casey near Seven Pines and Fair Oaks
Commander, III Corps; south of the ChickahominySamuel P. HeintzelmanPhilip Kearny reinforced Keyes with two brigades; Joseph Hooker’s division was also in III Corps
Commander, II Corps; north of the ChickahominyEdwin V. SumnerJohn Sedgwick and Israel B. Richardson; their later crossing falls outside this episode’s stopping point
Commanders, V and VI Corps; north of the ChickahominyFitz John Porter and William B. FranklinHeld the northern wing with Sumner while the opening attack struck south of the river

Confederacy

Commander, Confederate field army defending RichmondJoseph E. JohnstonCreated a left wing under Gustavus W. Smith and a right wing under James Longstreet, then personally advanced with Whiting late in the episode
Left wingGustavus W. SmithW. H. C. Whiting was designated to support Longstreet; John B. Magruder and A. P. Hill remained in reserve along the upper Chickahominy
Right wing and tactical commander of the main attackJames LongstreetHis own division, D. H. Hill’s division, and Benjamin Huger’s division were assigned to the three-road operation
Division on Williamsburg RoadD. H. HillRobert E. Rodes, Samuel Garland Jr., George B. Anderson, and Gabriel J. Rains; Hill’s was the only division to begin on its assigned road
Division on Charles City RoadBenjamin HugerHis route was blocked by Longstreet’s column, and his unclear command relationship with Longstreet compounded the delay
Brigade in Longstreet’s divisionRichard H. AndersonMicah Jenkins led two regiments in the late-afternoon thrust that separated Couch from the rest of the United States line

    Silas Casey

    Commanded the exposed IV Corps division whose picket line, redoubt, and camps were overrun by Hill’s attack

  • DN
    United States

    Darius N. Couch

    Commanded the second IV Corps division and reinforced Casey before Jenkins’s advance separated part of his force

  • United States

    Philip Kearny

    Led two III Corps brigades into a counterattack south of Williamsburg Road after Casey’s line gave way

  • Confederacy

    Joseph E. Johnston

    Planned the attack, failed to issue complete written orders, and moved with Whiting toward Fair Oaks late in the episode

  • Confederacy

    James Longstreet

    Held tactical command of the main attack; his movement onto Williamsburg Road disrupted Johnston’s intended three-road advance

  • DH
